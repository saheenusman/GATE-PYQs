# 2010 CS Q40: Which of four languages of the form 0^i 1^j are context free

## Question

Consider the languages $L_1 = \lbrace 0^i 1^j \mid i \ne j \rbrace$, $L_2 = \lbrace 0^i 1^j \mid i = j \rbrace$, $L_3 = \lbrace 0^i 1^j \mid i = 2j + 1 \rbrace$, $L_4 = \lbrace 0^i 1^j \mid i \ne 2j \rbrace$. Which one of the following statements is true?

- (A) Only $L_2$ is context free
- (B) Only $L_2$ and $L_3$ are context free
- (C) Only $L_1$ and $L_2$ are context free
- (D) All are context free

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Each language compares the count of 0s with a fixed multiple of the count of 1s, which a PDA can do with its stack. So all four are context free.

- $L_2$: $S \to 0S1 \mid \varepsilon$.
- $L_3$: $S \to 00S1 \mid 0$ (two 0s per 1, plus one extra 0).
- $L_1$ ($i > j$ or $i < j$): the union of $S_1 \to 0S_11 \mid A$, $A \to 0A \mid 0$ (more 0s) and $S_2 \to 0S_21 \mid B$, $B \to 1B \mid 1$ (more 1s).
- $L_4$ ($i > 2j$ or $i < 2j$): the union of $T_1 \to 00T_11 \mid A$ (more than two 0s per 1) and $T_2 \to 00T_21 \mid C$, $C \to D \mid 0D$, $D \to 1D \mid 1$ (fewer than two 0s per 1).

Context-free languages are closed under union, so $L_1$ and $L_4$ are context free. All four are: (D).

- (A), (B), (C) each deny context-freeness to $L_1$ or $L_4$, but "$\ne$" is just "$<$ or $>$", a union of two CFLs. (CFLs are not closed under complement, but we never needed one.)

**Key idea:** "$\ne$" is a union of "$<$" and "$>$"; each is context free because a stack can count.

</details>
