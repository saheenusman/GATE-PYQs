# 2013 CS Q32: Complement of L1 and L2

## Question

Consider the following languages.

$L_1 = \lbrace 0^p 1^q 0^r \mid p, q, r \ge 0 \rbrace$

$L_2 = \lbrace 0^p 1^q 0^r \mid p, q, r \ge 0,\ p \ne r \rbrace$

Which one of the following statements is **FALSE**?

- (A) $L_2$ is context-free.
- (B) $L_1 \cap L_2$ is context-free.
- (C) Complement of $L_2$ is recursive.
- (D) Complement of $L_1$ is context-free but not regular.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

$L_1$ is the regular language $0^*1^*0^*$.

- (A) **True.** A PDA pushes the leading 0s, skips the 1s, and pops against the trailing 0s, accepting when the counts differ.
- (B) **True.** $L_2 \subseteq L_1$, so $L_1 \cap L_2 = L_2$, which is context-free.
- (C) **True.** Context-free languages are decidable, and decidable (recursive) languages are closed under complement.
- (D) **False.** $L_1$ is regular, and regular languages are closed under complement, so $\overline{L_1}$ is regular. The claim "not regular" is wrong.

**Key idea:** the complement of a regular language is regular; CFLs are not closed under complement, but they are always recursive.

</details>
