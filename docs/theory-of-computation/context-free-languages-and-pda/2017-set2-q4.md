# 2017 CS2 Q4: Closure properties of context-free languages

## Question

Let $L_1$, $L_2$ be any two context-free languages and $R$ be any regular language. Then which of the following is/are CORRECT?

- I. $L_1 \cup L_2$ is context-free.
- II. $\overline{L_1}$ is context-free.
- III. $L_1 - R$ is context-free.
- IV. $L_1 \cap L_2$ is context-free.

Options:

- (A) I, II and IV only
- (B) I and III only
- (C) II and IV only
- (D) I only

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**I. True.** CFLs are closed under union: add $S \to S_1 \mid S_2$.

**II. False.** CFLs are not closed under complement. Example: $L = \lbrace a^ib^jc^k \mid i\ne j \text{ or } j \ne k\rbrace$ is context-free (a union of two CFLs). If $\overline{L}$ were context-free, then $\overline{L}\cap a^*b^*c^*$ would be too (closed under intersection with a regular set), but that set is $\lbrace a^nb^nc^n\rbrace$, which is not context-free.

**III. True.** $L_1 - R = L_1 \cap \overline{R}$. The complement of a regular language is regular, and the intersection of a CFL with a regular language is a CFL (run the PDA and the DFA together).

**IV. False.** CFLs are not closed under intersection: $\lbrace a^nb^nc^m\rbrace \cap \lbrace a^mb^nc^n\rbrace = \lbrace a^nb^nc^n\rbrace$.

So I and III only: **(B)**.

**Why the others fail.** (A), (C) include II or IV. (D) leaves out III, which is true.

**Status note:** closure properties are shown by construction and by the counterexamples above (no program applies); the answer matches the official key.

**Key idea:** CFLs are closed under union, concatenation, star and intersection with a regular language, but not under complement or intersection.

</details>
