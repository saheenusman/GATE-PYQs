# 2021 CS2 Q51: Which languages built from w, x and their reversals are context-free

## Question

For a string $w$, we define $w^R$ to be the reverse of $w$. For example, if $w=01101$ then $w^R=10110$.

Which of the following languages is/are context-free?

- (A) $\lbrace wxw^Rx^R \mid w,x\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^*\rbrace$
- (B) $\lbrace ww^Rxx^R \mid w,x\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^*\rbrace$
- (C) $\lbrace wxw^R \mid w,x\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^*\rbrace$
- (D) $\lbrace wxx^Rw^R \mid w,x\in\lbrace0,1\rbrace^*\rbrace$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B, C and D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Context-free: (B), (C), (D).**

- **(B)** $ww^R\,xx^R$ is two even-length palindromes side by side: $S\to PP$, $P\to0P0\mid1P1\mid\varepsilon$.
- **(C)** $wxw^R$ has one nested pair around a free middle: $S\to0S0\mid1S1\mid X$, $X\to0X\mid1X\mid\varepsilon$.
- **(D)** $w\,(xx^R)\,w^R$ is just the set of even-length palindromes: $S\to0S0\mid1S1\mid\varepsilon$.

I generated the strings of each grammar up to length 12 and compared them with a brute-force membership test on the definition. The sets are identical for (B), (C) and (D).

**Not context-free: (A)** $wxw^Rx^R$. The reversals are **interleaved**. After reading $w$ and then $x$, a PDA would have to match $w^R$ against $w$, but $x$ is on top of $w$ on the stack. Then it would also have to match $x^R$ against $x$, which is buried under $w$. The dependencies cross, unlike the nested ones in (B), (C), (D).

**What I could not verify:** I do not have a short formal proof that (A) is not context-free. The easy route fails: intersecting with $0^*1^*0^*1^*$ does not isolate $\lbrace0^a1^b0^a1^b\rbrace$, because other strings of that shape also belong to (A), for example $010001=0\cdot10\cdot0\cdot01$. So the exclusion of (A) rests on the official key and the crossing-dependency argument above. Hence the status is `unverified`.

**Key idea:** nested dependencies ($ww^R$ style) are context-free; crossing ones ($wxw^Rx^R$) are not.

</details>
