# 2008 CS Q50: True statements about grammars

## Question

Which of the following statements are true?

- I. Every left-recursive grammar can be converted to a right-recursive grammar and vice-versa
- II. All $\varepsilon$-productions can be removed from any context-free grammar by suitable transformations
- III. The language generated by a context-free grammar all of whose productions are of the form $X \to w$ or $X \to wY$ (where $w$ is a string of terminals and $Y$ is a non-terminal) is always regular
- IV. The derivation trees of strings generated by a context-free grammar in Chomsky Normal Form are always binary trees

Options:

- (A) I, II, III and IV
- (B) II, III and IV only
- (C) I, III and IV only
- (D) I, II and IV only

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

- **I. True.** Left recursion can be removed and an equivalent right-recursive grammar obtained (and the reverse), without changing the language.
- **II. True** (the standard theorem). All $\varepsilon$-productions can be eliminated. The one exception is a language that contains $\varepsilon$ itself, where a single $S \to \varepsilon$ must stay.
- **III. True.** Productions $X \to w$ and $X \to wY$ form a right-linear grammar, which always generates a regular language.
- **IV. True.** In Chomsky Normal Form every production is $A \to BC$ or $A \to a$, so every node has at most two children: a binary tree.

All four hold: (A).

- (B) wrongly drops I, (C) wrongly drops II, and (D) wrongly drops III.

**Caveat on the wording.** Statement II is exact only up to the empty string, as noted above. The key accepts it as true.

**Key idea:** CNF gives binary derivation trees, right-linear grammars give regular languages, and left and right recursion are interchangeable.

</details>
