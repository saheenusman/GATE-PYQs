# 2024 CS1 Q59: Length of a derivation in Chomsky Normal Form

## Question

Let $G = (V, \Sigma, S, P)$ be a context-free grammar in Chomsky Normal Form with $\Sigma = \lbrace a, b, c \rbrace$ and $V$ containing 10 variable symbols including the start symbol $S$. The string $w = a^{30} b^{30} c^{30}$ is derivable from $S$. The number of steps (application of rules) in the derivation $S \to^* w$ is ________

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**179**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

In CNF every rule is $A \to BC$ or $A \to a$. Let $|w| = n = 90$.

- Each rule $A \to BC$ increases the number of symbols in the sentential form by **1**. Going from 1 symbol ($S$) to 90 needs $n - 1 = 89$ such steps.
- Each of the 90 final symbols must be turned into a terminal by a rule $A \to a$: **90** steps.

Total steps $= (n-1) + n = 2n - 1 = 2 \cdot 90 - 1 = 179$.

The count of 10 variables is irrelevant, and so is the particular grammar.

**Key idea:** a CNF derivation of a string of length $n$ always takes exactly $2n - 1$ steps.

</details>
