# 2017 CS1 Q37: Intersection of two context-free grammars with c-runs

## Question

Consider the context-free grammars over the alphabet $\lbrace a, b, c\rbrace$ given below. $S$ and $T$ are non-terminals.

$$G_1:\ S \to aSb \mid T,\quad T \to cT \mid \epsilon$$
$$G_2:\ S \to bSa \mid T,\quad T \to cT \mid \epsilon$$

The language $L(G_1) \cap L(G_2)$ is

- (A) Finite.
- (B) Not finite but regular.
- (C) Context-Free but not regular.
- (D) Recursive but not context-free.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**The two languages.** $T$ generates $c^{k}$ for $k \ge 0$.

- $L(G_1) = \lbrace a^{n} c^{k} b^{n} \mid n,k \ge 0\rbrace$
- $L(G_2) = \lbrace b^{n} c^{k} a^{n} \mid n,k \ge 0\rbrace$

**Intersection.** A string with $n \ge 1$ in $L(G_1)$ starts with $a$. A string with $n \ge 1$ in $L(G_2)$ starts with $b$. They cannot be equal, so the only common strings have $n = 0$ in both, i.e. $c^{k}$. Every $c^k$ is in both languages. So

$$L(G_1)\cap L(G_2) = \lbrace c^{k} \mid k \ge 0\rbrace = c^{*}$$

This is infinite and regular: **(B)**.

**Why the others fail.** (A) $c^*$ has infinitely many strings. (C), (D) $c^*$ is regular, hence also context-free.

**Code check:** generating both languages up to length 9 and intersecting them gave exactly the strings $\epsilon, c, cc, \ldots, c^9$.

**Key idea:** the intersection of two context-free languages can be simpler than either; here the $a$/$b$ parts clash, leaving only the $c$'s.

</details>
