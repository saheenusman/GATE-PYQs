# 2019 CS2 Q25: A valid pumping length for a union of two unary languages

## Question

For $\Sigma = \lbrace a, b \rbrace$, let us consider the regular language $L = \lbrace x \mid x = a^{2+3k}\ \text{or}\ x = b^{10+12k},\ k \ge 0 \rbrace$. Which one of the following can be a pumping length (the constant guaranteed by the pumping lemma) for $L$?

- (A) 3
- (B) 5
- (C) 9
- (D) 24

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

A number $p$ is a pumping length if every $w \in L$ with $\lvert w \rvert \ge p$ can be written $w = xyz$ with $\lvert xy \rvert \le p$, $\lvert y \rvert \ge 1$ and $xy^iz \in L$ for all $i \ge 0$. Any $p$ above a valid one is also valid.

**The $b$-strings set the minimum.** In $b^{10+12k}$ the length must stay $\equiv 10 \pmod{12}$ after pumping. So $\lvert y\rvert$ must be a multiple of 12, and $\lvert y\rvert \le \lvert xy\rvert \le p$ forces $p \ge 12$.

**The $a$-strings are easy.** Take $y = aaa$ (period 3). It works for any $p \ge 3$.

So every $p \ge 12$ works. Among the options only **24** qualifies.

**Why 3, 5, 9 fail:** take $w = b^{10}$ (length 10, so $\lvert w\rvert \ge p$). Then $\lvert y \rvert \le 9$ and pumping changes the length by 1 to 9, which leaves the form $10 + 12k$.

**Code check:** testing the pumping lemma on all strings of $L$ up to length 100 shows 3, 5 and 9 fail, and 12 and 24 pass. The smallest valid $p$ is 12.

**Key idea:** the period 12 of the $b$-part forces any pumping length to be at least 12.

</details>
