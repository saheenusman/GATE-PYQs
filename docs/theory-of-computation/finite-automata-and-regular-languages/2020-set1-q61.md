# 2020 CS Q61: Minimum DFA states for number of a's divisible by 2 but not 3

## Question

Consider the following language.

$L = \lbrace x \in \lbrace a, b \rbrace^* \mid \text{number of } a\text{'s in } x \text{ is divisible by 2 but not divisible by 3} \rbrace$

The minimum number of states in a DFA that accepts $L$ is ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**6**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Rewrite the condition.** Let $c$ be the number of a's. "Divisible by 2 but not by 3" depends only on $c \bmod 6$:

| $c \bmod 6$ | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Divisible by 2? | yes | no | yes | no | yes | no |
| Divisible by 3? | yes | no | no | yes | no | no |
| In $L$? | no (3 divides it) | no | **yes** | no | **yes** | no |

So $L$ is the set of strings with $c \bmod 6 \in \lbrace 2, 4 \rbrace$.

**A 6-state DFA works.** Let state $i$ mean "$c \bmod 6 = i$". An `a` moves $i \to (i+1) \bmod 6$, a `b` loops, and states 2 and 4 accept.

**No fewer states are possible.** Two states $i \ne j$ are distinguished by a suffix $a^k$ iff exactly one of $i+k$ and $j+k$ is in $\lbrace 2,4 \rbrace \pmod 6$. The accepting set $\lbrace 2,4 \rbrace$ does not map onto itself under any shift except 0, so every pair of different states is distinguishable. By the Myhill-Nerode theorem all 6 states are needed.

I confirmed this with the table-filling algorithm in code: all 15 pairs of the 6 states are distinguishable, so the DFA cannot be reduced.

**Key idea:** counting modulo $m$ needs $m$ states, and the "divisible by 2 but not 3" condition needs the full modulus $\operatorname{lcm}(2,3) = 6$.

</details>
