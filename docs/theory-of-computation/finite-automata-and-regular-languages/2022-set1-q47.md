# 2022 CS Q47: Regularity of two string languages

## Question

Consider the following languages:

$L_1 = \lbrace a^n w a^n \mid w \in \lbrace a, b \rbrace^* \rbrace$

$L_2 = \lbrace w x w^R \mid w, x \in \lbrace a, b \rbrace^*,\ |w|, |x| > 0 \rbrace$

Note that $w^R$ is the reversal of the string $w$. Which of the following is/are TRUE?

- (A) $L_1$ and $L_2$ are regular.
- (B) $L_1$ and $L_2$ are context-free.
- (C) $L_1$ is regular and $L_2$ is context-free.
- (D) $L_1$ and $L_2$ are context-free but not regular.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A, B and C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**$L_1$ is regular.** Taking $n = 0$ gives every string $w \in \lbrace a, b \rbrace^*$, so $L_1 = \lbrace a, b \rbrace^*$. (Even if $n \ge 1$ were required, $a\,(a^{n-1} w a^{n-1})\,a$ with $a^{n-1} w a^{n-1}$ ranging over all strings gives "starts and ends with $a$, length at least 2", which is also regular.)

**$L_2$ is regular.** A string $w x w^R$ with $|w|, |x| > 0$ ends with the same letter it starts with (the first letter of $w$ is the last letter of $w^R$), and has length at least 3. Conversely, any string of length at least 3 that starts and ends with the same letter $c$ is $c\,x\,c$, taking $w = c$. So

$$L_2 = a\,(a+b)^+\,a \;+\; b\,(a+b)^+\,b$$

which is regular.

**Verdict.** Both are regular, hence also context-free. (A), (B) and (C) are true. (D) is false: it claims they are not regular.

**Key idea:** a "reversal" condition that only matters at the ends (first letter equals last letter) does not need a stack.

</details>
