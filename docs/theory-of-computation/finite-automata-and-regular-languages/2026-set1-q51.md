# 2026 CS1 Q51: Closure facts when L1 intersect L2 and L2 are regular

## Question

Let $L_1$ and $L_2$ be two languages over a finite alphabet such that $L_1 \cap L_2$ and $L_2$ are regular languages. Which of the following statements is/are **always** true?

- (A) $L_1$ is regular
- (B) $L_1 \cup L_2$ is regular
- (C) $L_2$ is context-free
- (D) $L_1$ is context-free

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**What we know.** Only two facts: $L_2$ is regular, and $L_1 \cap L_2$ is regular. Nothing is said about the part of $L_1$ outside $L_2$.

**Counterexample for A, B and D.** Take $L_2 = \emptyset$ (regular). Then $L_1 \cap L_2 = \emptyset$ is regular for **any** $L_1$. So $L_1$ can be any language at all, for example a non-regular, non-context-free language such as $\lbrace a^n b^n c^n \mid n \ge 0 \rbrace$.

| Option | Always true? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| (A) $L_1$ regular | no | $L_1$ can be anything when $L_2 = \emptyset$ |
| (B) $L_1 \cup L_2$ regular | no | with $L_2 = \emptyset$ this is $L_1$ |
| (C) $L_2$ context-free | **yes** | regular languages are a subset of context-free languages |
| (D) $L_1$ context-free | no | same counterexample ($a^n b^n c^n$ is not context-free) |

**Key idea:** to test "always true", look for an extreme case ($L_2 = \emptyset$ here) that leaves one language completely free. Only facts about $L_2$ itself are guaranteed.

</details>
