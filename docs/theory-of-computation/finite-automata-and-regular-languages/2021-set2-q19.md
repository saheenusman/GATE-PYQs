# 2021 CS2 Q19: Which language is accepted by a minimal DFA with k states

## Question

Let $L\subseteq\lbrace0,1\rbrace^*$ be an arbitrary regular language accepted by a minimal DFA with $k$ states. Which one of the following languages must necessarily be accepted by a minimal DFA with $k$ states?

- (A) $L-\lbrace01\rbrace$
- (B) $L\cup\lbrace01\rbrace$
- (C) $\lbrace0,1\rbrace^*-L$
- (D) $L\cdot L$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

- (C) **Complement.** Take the minimal complete DFA for $L$ and swap accepting and non-accepting states. It still has $k$ states, and no two states became equivalent, because two states are distinguishable for $L$ exactly when they are for $\overline L$. So the minimal DFA for $\overline L$ has exactly $k$ states. **Correct.**
- (A) Removing 01: for $L=\Sigma^*$ ($k=1$), $L-\lbrace01\rbrace$ needs 4 states. Not $k$.
- (B) Adding 01: for $L=\emptyset$ ($k=1$), $L\cup\lbrace01\rbrace=\lbrace01\rbrace$ needs 4 states. Not $k$.
- (D) $L\cdot L$: for $L=\lbrace0\rbrace$ ($k=3$), $L\cdot L=\lbrace00\rbrace$ needs 4 states. Not $k$.

I counted the minimal DFA sizes of these example languages with a Myhill-Nerode check over all strings up to length 5: $\Sigma^*-\lbrace01\rbrace$ gives 4, $\lbrace01\rbrace$ gives 4, $\lbrace00\rbrace$ gives 4, while $\Sigma^*$, $\emptyset$ and $\lbrace0\rbrace$ give 1, 1 and 3.

**Key idea:** complementing a complete minimal DFA keeps the same states, so the state count does not change.

</details>
