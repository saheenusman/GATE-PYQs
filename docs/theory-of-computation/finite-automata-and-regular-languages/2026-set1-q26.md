# 2026 CS1 Q26: Possible minimal DFA sizes from a 6-state NFA

## Question

Let $M$ be a nondeterministic finite automaton (NFA) with 6 states over a finite alphabet. Which of the following options **CANNOT** be the number of states in the minimal deterministic finite automaton (DFA) equivalent to $M$?

- (A) 32
- (B) 65
- (C) 1
- (D) 128

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B and D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Refresher (subset construction).** Converting an NFA with $n$ states gives a DFA whose states are *subsets* of the NFA's states. So the DFA has at most $2^n$ states, and the minimal DFA has no more than that.

For $n = 6$: at most $2^6 = 64$ states.

| Option | Possible? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 32 | yes | $\le 64$, and it is reached (below) |
| 65 | **no** | more than 64 |
| 1 | yes | e.g. the language $\Sigma^*$ (one accepting state looping on everything) |
| 128 | **no** | more than 64 |

**32 really happens.** Take "the 5th symbol from the end is 1". An NFA with 6 states guesses the position: state 0 loops on 0,1; on a 1 it moves to state 1; states 1 to 4 each move forward on any symbol; state 5 is accepting. Its minimal DFA must remember the last 5 symbols, so it has $2^5 = 32$ states.

**Verified.** I built that NFA and ran subset construction plus minimisation in Python: 32 states.

**Key idea:** an $n$-state NFA gives a minimal DFA with at most $2^n$ states, and the bound can be nearly tight. The exact size is not always $2^n$ (here 32, not 64).

</details>
