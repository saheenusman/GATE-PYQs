# 2024 CS1 Q23: Always-true statements about regular and non-regular languages

## Question

Let $L_1$, $L_2$ be two regular languages and $L_3$ a language which is not regular. Which of the following statements is/are always TRUE?

- (A) $L_1 = L_2$ if and only if $L_1 \cap \overline{L_2} = \phi$
- (B) $L_1 \cup L_3$ is not regular
- (C) $\overline{L_3}$ is not regular
- (D) $\overline{L_1} \cup \overline{L_2}$ is regular

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C and D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Refresher.** Regular languages are closed under union, intersection and complement. If a language is not regular, its complement is not regular either (otherwise the complement of the complement would be regular).

- **(A) False.** $L_1 \cap \overline{L_2} = \phi$ only says $L_1 \subseteq L_2$. It does not give equality. Take $L_1 = \lbrace a \rbrace$ and $L_2 = \lbrace a, b \rbrace$: the intersection is empty but $L_1 \ne L_2$.
- **(B) False.** Take $L_1 = \Sigma^*$ and any non-regular $L_3$. Then $L_1 \cup L_3 = \Sigma^*$, which is regular.
- **(C) True.** If $\overline{L_3}$ were regular, then $L_3 = \overline{\overline{L_3}}$ would be regular. Contradiction.
- **(D) True.** $\overline{L_1}$ and $\overline{L_2}$ are regular, and a union of regular languages is regular.

**Key idea:** regular languages are closed under complement, so a language and its complement are regular or non-regular together.

</details>
