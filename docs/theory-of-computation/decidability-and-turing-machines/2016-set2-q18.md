# 2016 CS2 Q18: Closure of language classes under union, intersection and complement

## Question

Consider the following types of languages: $L_1$: Regular, $L_2$: Context-free, $L_3$: Recursive, $L_4$: Recursively enumerable. Which of the following is/are TRUE?

- I. $\overline{L_3} \cup L_4$ is recursively enumerable
- II. $\overline{L_2} \cup L_3$ is recursive
- III. $L_1^* \cap L_2$ is context-free
- IV. $L_1 \cup \overline{L_2}$ is context-free

- (A) I only
- (B) I and III only
- (C) I and IV only
- (D) I, II and III only

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

(The bars are complements; they are easy to lose when copying the question.)

- **I true.** Recursive languages are closed under complement, so $\overline{L_3}$ is recursive, hence r.e. The union of two r.e. languages is r.e.
- **II true.** A CFL is recursive, and recursive languages are closed under complement, so $\overline{L_2}$ is recursive. The union of two recursive languages is recursive.
- **III true.** $L_1^*$ is regular, and the intersection of a regular language with a CFL is a CFL.
- **IV false.** CFLs are **not** closed under complement, so $\overline{L_2}$ need not be context-free, and neither is its union with a regular language in general.

I, II and III only: **(D)**.

**Key idea:** CFLs are not closed under complement, so IV fails; the other three follow from closure properties.

</details>
