# 2008 CS Q10: Decidable problems about regular and context-free languages

## Question

Which of the following are decidable?

- I. Whether the intersection of two regular languages is infinite
- II. Whether a given context-free language is regular
- III. Whether two push-down automata accept the same language
- IV. Whether a given grammar is context-free

Options:

- (A) I and II
- (B) I and IV
- (C) II and III
- (D) II and IV

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

- **I. Decidable.** Regular languages are closed under intersection, so build a DFA for the intersection. A DFA accepts an infinite language iff a cycle is reachable from the start state and can reach an accepting state. That is a graph check.
- **II. Undecidable.** Deciding whether a given CFL is regular is undecidable.
- **III. Undecidable.** Equivalence of two PDAs (two CFLs) is undecidable. (Even "is $L = \Sigma^*$" is undecidable for CFLs.)
- **IV. Decidable.** Whether a grammar is context-free is a syntactic check: every production must have a single non-terminal on the left-hand side.

So I and IV are decidable: (B).

- (A), (C) and (D) each include II or III.

**Key idea:** emptiness, finiteness and membership are decidable for CFLs, but equivalence and regularity of a CFL are not.

</details>
