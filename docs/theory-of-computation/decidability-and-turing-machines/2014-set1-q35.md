# 2014 CS1 Q35: Language and its complement: which case is impossible

## Question

Let $L$ be a language and $\bar{L}$ be its complement. Which one of the following is **NOT** a viable possibility?

- (A) Neither $L$ nor $\bar{L}$ is recursively enumerable (r.e.).
- (B) One of $L$ and $\bar{L}$ is r.e. but not recursive; the other is not r.e.
- (C) Both $L$ and $\bar{L}$ are r.e. but not recursive.
- (D) Both $L$ and $\bar{L}$ are recursive.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

If both $L$ and $\bar{L}$ are r.e., run the two recognisers in parallel. Exactly one halts and accepts, which decides $L$. So $L$ is recursive. Hence **both r.e. but not recursive is impossible**: (C).

The others can occur:

- (A) there are languages such that neither they nor their complements are r.e.
- (B) $L_u$ (the universal language) is r.e. but not recursive, and its complement is not r.e.
- (D) every regular or context-free language is recursive, and so is its complement.

**Key idea:** $L$ and $\bar{L}$ both r.e. implies $L$ is recursive.

</details>
