# 2014 CS1 Q40: Hash table with chaining: max, min and average chain length

## Question

Consider a hash table with 9 slots. The hash function is $h(k) = k \bmod 9$. The collisions are resolved by chaining. The following 9 keys are inserted in the order: 5, 28, 19, 15, 20, 33, 12, 17, 10. The maximum, minimum, and average chain lengths in the hash table, respectively, are

- (A) 3, 0, and 1
- (B) 3, 3, and 3
- (C) 4, 0, and 1
- (D) 3, 0, and 2

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Hash each key:

| Key | 5 | 28 | 19 | 15 | 20 | 33 | 12 | 17 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $k \bmod 9$ | 5 | 1 | 1 | 6 | 2 | 6 | 3 | 8 | 1 |

Chains:

| Slot | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Length | 0 | 3 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 0 | 1 |

Slot 1 holds 28, 19, 10. Maximum $=3$, minimum $=0$ (slots 0, 4, 7), average $= 9/9 = 1$.

**Code check:** a short script gives lengths `[0, 3, 1, 1, 0, 1, 2, 0, 1]`.

- (B) no slot has all the keys; empty slots exist.
- (C) maximum is 3, not 4.
- (D) the average is keys $\div$ slots $= 1$, not 2.

**Key idea:** average chain length is the load factor $n/m$.

</details>
