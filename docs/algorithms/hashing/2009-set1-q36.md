# 2009 CS Q36: Resulting table after inserting keys with linear probing

## Question

The keys $12, 18, 13, 2, 3, 23, 5$ and $15$ are inserted into an initially empty hash table of length $10$ using open addressing with hash function $h(k) = k \bmod 10$ and linear probing. What is the resultant hash table?

![Four hash tables labelled A to D, each with slots 0 to 9. Table A holds 2 at slot 2, 23 at slot 3, 15 at slot 5 and 18 at slot 8. Table B holds 12 at slot 2, 13 at slot 3, 5 at slot 5 and 18 at slot 8. Table C holds 12 at slot 2, 13 at slot 3, 2 at slot 4, 3 at slot 5, 23 at slot 6, 5 at slot 7, 18 at slot 8 and 15 at slot 9. Table D holds the lists 12 and 2 at slot 2, 13 and 3 and 23 at slot 3, 5 and 15 at slot 5, and 18 at slot 8.](../../assets/2009/set1-q36.png)

The options, written as slot: contents:

- (A) 2: 2; 3: 23; 5: 15; 8: 18
- (B) 2: 12; 3: 13; 5: 5; 8: 18
- (C) 2: 12; 3: 13; 4: 2; 5: 3; 6: 23; 7: 5; 8: 18; 9: 15
- (D) 2: 12, 2; 3: 13, 3, 23; 5: 5, 15; 8: 18

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Insert in the given order. If the home slot $k \bmod 10$ is taken, try the next slot (wrapping around).

| Key | Home slot | Slots tried | Placed in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| 18 | 8 | 8 | 8 |
| 13 | 3 | 3 | 3 |
| 2 | 2 | 2 (full), 3 (full), 4 | 4 |
| 3 | 3 | 3 (full), 4 (full), 5 | 5 |
| 23 | 3 | 3, 4, 5 (full), 6 | 6 |
| 5 | 5 | 5 (full), 6 (full), 7 | 7 |
| 15 | 5 | 5, 6, 7, 8 (full), 9 | 9 |

Final table: slots 2 to 9 hold $12, 13, 2, 3, 23, 5, 18, 15$. This is (C).

- (A) and (B) store only four keys, so they have lost four of the eight keys.
- (D) shows several keys in one slot, which is **chaining**, not open addressing.

**Code check:** a short linear-probing simulation gave slots $\lbrace 2{:}12, 3{:}13, 4{:}2, 5{:}3, 6{:}23, 7{:}5, 8{:}18, 9{:}15 \rbrace$.

**Key idea:** with linear probing a key goes to the first free slot at or after its home slot, so collisions pile up into clusters.

</details>
