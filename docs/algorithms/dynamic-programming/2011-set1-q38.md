# 2011 CS Q38: Minimum scalar multiplications for a chain of four matrices

## Question

Four matrices $M_1$, $M_2$, $M_3$ and $M_4$ of dimensions $p \times q$, $q \times r$, $r \times s$ and $s \times t$ respectively can be multiplied in several ways with different number of total scalar multiplications. For example when multiplied as $((M_1 \times M_2) \times (M_3 \times M_4))$, the total number of scalar multiplications is $pqr + rst + prt$. When multiplied as $(((M_1 \times M_2) \times M_3) \times M_4)$, the total number of scalar multiplications is $pqr + prs + pst$.

If $p = 10$, $q = 100$, $r = 20$, $s = 5$ and $t = 80$, then the minimum number of scalar multiplications needed is

- (A) 248000
- (B) 44000
- (C) 19000
- (D) 25000

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

The dimension list is $d = (10, 100, 20, 5, 80)$. Let $m[i,j]$ be the least cost to multiply $M_i \dots M_j$; a product $M_i \dots M_k \times M_{k+1} \dots M_j$ costs $d_{i-1} d_k d_j$ for the last multiplication.

| Chain | Best split and cost |
|---|---|
| $M_1 M_2$ | $10 \cdot 100 \cdot 20 = 20000$ |
| $M_2 M_3$ | $100 \cdot 20 \cdot 5 = 10000$ |
| $M_3 M_4$ | $20 \cdot 5 \cdot 80 = 8000$ |
| $M_1 M_2 M_3$ | $M_1 (M_2 M_3)$: $10000 + 10 \cdot 100 \cdot 5 = 15000$ (the other split gives $21000$) |
| $M_2 M_3 M_4$ | $(M_2 M_3) M_4$: $10000 + 100 \cdot 5 \cdot 80 = 50000$ |
| $M_1 \dots M_4$ | splits: $50000 + 80000 = 130000$; $20000 + 8000 + 16000 = 44000$; $15000 + 4000 = \mathbf{19000}$ |

The minimum is $19000$, from $((M_1 (M_2 M_3)) M_4)$: (C).

- (A) $248000$ is the cost of $(M_1 (M_2 (M_3 M_4)))$.
- (B) $44000$ is the cost of the example $((M_1 M_2)(M_3 M_4))$.
- (D) $25000$ is the cost of the example $(((M_1 M_2) M_3) M_4)$.

**Code check:** the matrix-chain DP gave $19000$; the two example parenthesizations gave $44000$ and $25000$.

**Key idea:** matrix-chain order: try every last split and reuse optimal costs of shorter chains.

</details>
