# 2010 CS Q34: Best weighted subsequence in terms of the best for the tail

## Question

The weight of a sequence $a_0, a_1, \dots, a_{n-1}$ of real numbers is defined as $a_0 + a_1/2 + \dots + a_{n-1}/2^{n-1}$. A subsequence of a sequence is obtained by deleting some elements from the sequence, keeping the order of the remaining elements the same. Let $X$ denote the maximum possible weight of a subsequence of $a_0, a_1, \dots, a_{n-1}$. Then $X$ is equal to

- (A) $\max(Y,\ a_0 + Y)$
- (B) $\max(Y,\ a_0 + Y/2)$
- (C) $\max(Y,\ a_0 + 2Y)$
- (D) $a_0 + Y/2$

*Printed as is: the PDF never defines $Y$. Other published copies of this paper add "and $Y$ the maximum possible weight of a subsequence of $a_1, a_2, \dots, a_{n-1}$". I use that reading, since the options only make sense with it. This definition is not in the supplied PDF.*

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Status: disputed.** The official key in the supplied PDF is (D). My own analysis gives (B). Both follow.

**What I get.** An optimal subsequence of $a_0, \dots, a_{n-1}$ either leaves out $a_0$ or starts with it.

- **Without $a_0$:** it is a subsequence of $a_1, \dots, a_{n-1}$. Weight is measured from the subsequence's own first element, so its best weight is exactly $Y$.
- **With $a_0$:** $a_0$ has weight $a_0$. Every later chosen element moves one place further from the front, so its weight is halved. The best total is $a_0 + Y/2$.

Hence $X = \max(Y,\ a_0 + Y/2)$, which is (B).

- (A) and (C) scale the tail by $1$ or $2$ instead of $\tfrac12$. Example $a = (10, 4)$: $X = 10 + 2 = 12$, but (A) gives $14$ and (C) gives $18$.
- (D) always includes $a_0$. Example $a = (-10, 4)$: dropping $a_0$ gives $X = 4$ (and $Y = 4$), but (D) gives $-10 + 2 = -8$. (D) is only right when $a_0 \ge Y/2$.

**What the key says.** (D), "$a_0 + Y/2$". I could not find a reading of the question where $a_0$ must be kept; $a_0$ can be negative ("real numbers"). The public solution sets I looked at also give (B). I record the official key (D) and flag the conflict.

**Code check:** for $400$ random sequences (length $1$ to $8$, entries $-9$ to $9$), the true maximum over all subsequences matched option (B) every time. Options (A), (C), (D) failed on $158$, $244$ and $235$ of them.

**Key idea:** split on whether $a_0$ is used; including it halves the weight of everything after it, hence $Y/2$, and skipping it leaves $Y$.
