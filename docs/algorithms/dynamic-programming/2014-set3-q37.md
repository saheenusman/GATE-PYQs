# 2014 CS3 Q37: Shortcuts on a number line: T(9) = 1 + min(T(y), T(z))

## Question

Suppose you want to move from 0 to 100 on the number line. In each step, you either move right by a unit distance or you take a *shortcut*. A shortcut is simply a pre-specified pair of integers $i, j$ with $i < j$. Given a shortcut $(i, j)$, if you are at position $i$ on the number line, you may directly move to $j$. Suppose $T(k)$ denotes the smallest number of steps needed to move from $k$ to 100. Suppose further that there is at most 1 shortcut involving any number, and in particular from 9 there is a shortcut to 15. Let $y$ and $z$ be such that $T(9) = 1 + \min(T(y), T(z))$. Then the value of the product $yz$ is ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**150**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

From position 9 there are exactly two possible first moves:

- the unit step to $10$,
- the shortcut from $9$ to $15$.

After one step you are at 10 or at 15, and the remaining cost is $T(10)$ or $T(15)$. So

$$T(9) = 1 + \min\big(T(10),\ T(15)\big),$$

which gives $\lbrace y, z \rbrace = \lbrace 10, 15 \rbrace$ and $yz = 10 \times 15 = 150$.

The condition "at most one shortcut involving any number" ensures that 9 has no other shortcut, so these are the only two choices.

**Key idea:** a shortest-path recurrence takes the minimum over the next positions reachable in one step.

</details>
