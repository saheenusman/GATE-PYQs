# 2021 CS1 Q50: Rod cutting with seven pieces of prices

## Question

Define $R_n$ to be the maximum amount earned by cutting a rod of length $n$ meters into one or more pieces of integer length and selling them. For $i>0$, let `p[i]` denote the selling price of a rod whose length is $i$ meters. Consider the array of prices:

`p[1] = 1, p[2] = 5, p[3] = 8, p[4] = 9, p[5] = 10, p[6] = 17, p[7] = 18`

Which of the following statements is/are correct about $R_7$?

- (A) $R_7=18$
- (B) $R_7=19$
- (C) $R_7$ is achieved by three different solutions.
- (D) $R_7$ cannot be achieved by a solution consisting of three pieces.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A and C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

List the ways to cut 7 into integer pieces (order ignored) with their revenue. The best ones:

| Cut | Revenue |
|---|---|
| 7 | 18 |
| 6 + 1 | 17 + 1 = 18 |
| 3 + 2 + 2 | 8 + 5 + 5 = 18 |
| 4 + 3 | 9 + 8 = 17 |
| 3 + 3 + 1 | 8 + 8 + 1 = 17 |
| 5 + 2 | 10 + 5 = 15 |

So $R_7=18$, and it is achieved by exactly three solutions: $\lbrace7\rbrace$, $\lbrace6,1\rbrace$ and $\lbrace3,2,2\rbrace$.

- (A) **True.**
- (B) False. 19 is not reachable.
- (C) **True.** Three different (unordered) solutions.
- (D) False. $3+2+2$ has three pieces and reaches 18.

A script enumerated all 15 partitions of 7: the maximum is 18, reached by (7), (6,1) and (3,2,2).

Counting 6+1 and 1+6 as different would give more than three solutions, so "different" means different multisets of pieces.

**Key idea:** try all partitions (or the recurrence $R_n=\max_i(p_i+R_{n-i})$) and count the ones that reach the maximum.

</details>
