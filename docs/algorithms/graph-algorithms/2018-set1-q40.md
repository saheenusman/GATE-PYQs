# 2018 CS Q40: Cross edges in DFS and BFS trees of an undirected graph

## Question

Let $G$ be a simple undirected graph. Let $T_D$ be a depth first search tree of $G$. Let $T_B$ be a breadth first search tree of $G$. Consider the following statements.

- (I) No edge of $G$ is a cross edge with respect to $T_D$. (A cross edge in $G$ is between two nodes neither of which is an ancestor of the other in $T_D$.)
- (II) For every edge $(u,v)$ of $G$, if $u$ is at depth $i$ and $v$ is at depth $j$ in $T_B$, then $|i-j| = 1$.

Which of the statements above must necessarily be true?

- (A) I only
- (B) II only
- (C) Both I and II
- (D) Neither I nor II

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**(I) true.** In a DFS of an *undirected* graph, every non-tree edge joins a vertex to one of its ancestors or descendants (a back edge). If $u$ and $v$ were unrelated, DFS would have explored the edge $(u,v)$ from whichever was visited first and made it a tree edge. So there are no cross edges.

**(II) false.** BFS levels of adjacent vertices differ by **at most 1**, but they can be equal. In a triangle $a,b,c$ with BFS from $a$, vertices $b$ and $c$ are both at depth 1 and are joined by an edge, so $|i-j| = 0$.

Only I is necessarily true: **A**.

**Code check:** on 3000 random connected graphs, DFS trees had 0 cross edges; BFS trees had some edge with $|i-j|\ne1$ in 2174 of them.

**Key idea:** undirected DFS gives only tree and back edges; undirected BFS allows edges within a level or between neighbouring levels.

</details>
