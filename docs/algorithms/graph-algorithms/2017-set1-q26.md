# 2017 CS1 Q26: Uniqueness of MST and shortest paths when edge weights are distinct

## Question

Let $G = (V, E)$ be *any* connected undirected edge-weighted graph. The weights of the edges in $E$ are positive and distinct. Consider the following statements:

- (I) Minimum Spanning Tree of $G$ is always unique.
- (II) Shortest path between any two vertices of $G$ is always unique.

Which of the above statements is/are necessarily true?

- (A) (I) only
- (B) (II) only
- (C) both (I) and (II)
- (D) neither (I) nor (II)

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**(I) is true.** Suppose two different MSTs $T_1, T_2$ exist. Take the lightest edge $e$ that is in exactly one of them, say $e \in T_1 \setminus T_2$ (weights are distinct, so "lightest" is well defined). Adding $e$ to $T_2$ creates a cycle, and that cycle has an edge $f \notin T_1$ with $w(f) > w(e)$. Swapping gives a spanning tree lighter than $T_2$, contradicting that $T_2$ is minimum. So the MST is unique.

**(II) is false.** Distinct edge weights do not make *path sums* distinct. Take the vertices $0,1,2,3$ with edges $0\text{-}1$ (weight 1), $1\text{-}3$ (4), $0\text{-}2$ (2), $2\text{-}3$ (3). All four weights are distinct, but $0\to1\to3$ and $0\to2\to3$ both have length $5$. There are two shortest paths from $0$ to $3$.

So only (I): **(A)**.

**Code check:** for 379 random connected graphs with 3-6 vertices and distinct weights, a brute-force enumeration of every spanning tree found a unique minimum in all 379. The graph above has two shortest $0\to3$ paths of length 5 with distinct edge weights.

**Key idea:** distinct edge weights give a unique MST, but not unique shortest paths, because different paths can have equal sums.

</details>
