# 2016 CS1 Q39: Maximum possible MST weight of K4 with edge weights 1 to 6

## Question

Let $G$ be a complete undirected graph on 4 vertices, having 6 edges with weights being 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6. The maximum possible weight that a minimum weight spanning tree of $G$ can have is ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**7**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

A spanning tree of 4 vertices has 3 edges. We want the weight assignment that makes the MST as heavy as possible.

**Kruskal's order.**

- Edges $1$ and $2$ are always taken: two different edges of a simple graph cannot form a cycle.
- The next candidate is edge $3$. It is rejected only if it closes a cycle with edges $1$ and $2$, i.e. if $1, 2, 3$ form a **triangle**.

**Make edge 3 get rejected.** Put weights $1, 2, 3$ on a triangle. The other three edges join the 4th vertex to the triangle, with weights $4, 5, 6$. Then:

- Kruskal takes $1$ and $2$, rejects $3$ (cycle), and takes $4$ (it reaches the fourth vertex).
- Total: $1 + 2 + 4 = 7$.

**Why not more.** Only the triangle $\lbrace 1, 2, 3 \rbrace$ can reject edge $3$, and then edge $4$ joins the new vertex, so the third MST edge is at most $4$. If edge $3$ is accepted, the total is $1 + 2 + 3 = 6$.

**Code check:** computing the MST weight for all $720$ ways of assigning the weights gave a maximum of $7$.

**Key idea:** make edges 1, 2, 3 a triangle so Kruskal skips 3 and picks 4, giving $1 + 2 + 4$.

</details>
