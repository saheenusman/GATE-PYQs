# 2014 CS3 Q13: Maximum recursion depth of DFS on a grid-like graph

## Question

Suppose depth first search is executed on the graph below starting at some unknown vertex. Assume that a recursive call to visit a vertex is made only after first checking that the vertex has not been visited earlier. Then the maximum possible recursion depth (including the initial call) is ______.

![Undirected graph with 21 vertices. A 3 by 3 grid on the left and a 3 by 3 grid on the right. The bottom-right vertex of the left grid is joined to a middle vertex, which is joined to the bottom-left vertex of the right grid. The middle vertex also has one pendant vertex above it. The middle-right vertex of the right grid has one pendant vertex to its right.](../../assets/2014/set3-q13.png)

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**19**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

At any moment, the vertices on the recursion stack form a **simple path** (a vertex is entered only if it is unvisited). So the recursion depth is at most the number of vertices on the longest simple path. DFS can reach that depth by trying neighbours in the right order, so the maximum depth equals that number.

The graph has $9 + 9 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 21$ vertices: two $3\times3$ grids, the bottom-row vertex $b$ between them, a pendant above $b$, and a pendant beside the right grid.

A path with **19** vertices:

1. Start at the left grid's top-left corner. Go along the top row, down one, back along the middle row, down one, and along the bottom row. This visits **all 9** vertices of the left grid and ends at its bottom-right corner.
2. Step to $b$ (1 vertex).
3. Enter the right grid at its bottom-left corner and visit **all 9** vertices: along the bottom row, up the right column, back along the top row, then down the left column and into the centre.

$$9 + 1 + 9 = 19.$$

The pendant vertices cannot be added. A path through $b$ already uses both of $b$'s grid neighbours, so using the pendant above it would end the path at $b$, and then one of the two grids could not be traversed.

**Code check:** I entered the 21 vertices and 28 edges as read from the figure and found the longest simple path by exhaustive search from every start vertex: **19 vertices**, which is the key.

**Key idea:** the recursion depth of DFS is the length of a simple path, so the maximum is the longest simple path.

</details>
