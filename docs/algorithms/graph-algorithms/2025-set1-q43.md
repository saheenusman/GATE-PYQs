# 2025 CS1 Q43: Height of a BFS tree when the diameter is 30

## Question

Let $G(V,E)$ be an undirected and unweighted graph with 100 vertices. Let $d(u,v)$ denote the number of edges in a shortest path between vertices $u$ and $v$ in $V$. Let the maximum value of $d(u,v)$, $u,v \in V$ such that $u \ne v$, be 30. Let $T$ be any breadth-first-search tree of $G$. Which ONE of the given options is CORRECT for every such graph $G$?

- (A) The height of $T$ is exactly 15.
- (B) The height of $T$ is exactly 30.
- (C) The height of $T$ is at least 15.
- (D) The height of $T$ is at least 30.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

The graph's **diameter** is 30. A BFS tree rooted at $r$ has height equal to the eccentricity of $r$: the distance to the farthest vertex.

**Lower bound.** Let $u,v$ be two vertices with $d(u,v) = 30$. For any root $r$, the triangle inequality gives

$$30 = d(u,v) \le d(r,u) + d(r,v) \le 2\cdot \text{ecc}(r),$$

so the height $\text{ecc}(r) \ge 15$. So the height is **at least 15** for every root, hence for every BFS tree.

**Why it can be larger (so A is false) and smaller than 30 (so B, D are false).** Take a path of 31 vertices (diameter 30) and attach the other 69 vertices as leaves to the middle vertex. The diameter stays 30.

| BFS root | Height of the BFS tree |
|---|---|
| middle vertex | 15 |
| an end of the path | 30 |

Computing this example confirmed both heights.

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| A: exactly 15 | Fails: rooted at an end the height is 30 |
| B: exactly 30 | Fails: rooted at the middle the height is 15 |
| **C: at least 15** | True for every graph and root |
| D: at least 30 | Fails: the middle root gives 15 |

**Key idea:** for any root, $\text{ecc}(r) \ge \lceil \text{diameter}/2 \rceil$, and $\text{ecc}(r) \le \text{diameter}$.

</details>
