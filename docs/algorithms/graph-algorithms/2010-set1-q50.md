# 2010 CS Q50: Minimum spanning tree in which vertex 0 is a leaf

## Question

**Common Data Questions 50 and 51.**

Consider a complete undirected graph with vertex set $\lbrace 0, 1, 2, 3, 4 \rbrace$. Entry $W_{ij}$ in the matrix $W$ below is the weight of the edge $\lbrace i, j \rbrace$.

$$W = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 & 8 & 1 & 4 \\ 1 & 0 & 12 & 4 & 9 \\ 8 & 12 & 0 & 7 & 3 \\ 1 & 4 & 7 & 0 & 2 \\ 4 & 9 & 3 & 2 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$$

What is the minimum possible weight of a spanning tree $T$ in this graph such that vertex $0$ is a leaf node in the tree $T$?

- (A) $7$
- (B) $8$
- (C) $9$
- (D) $10$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

If $0$ is a leaf, removing it leaves a spanning tree on $\lbrace 1, 2, 3, 4 \rbrace$, and $0$ hangs off it by one edge. These two choices are independent, so minimise each.

- **Cheapest edge at $0$:** $W_{01} = W_{03} = 1$, cost $1$.
- **MST on $\lbrace 1, 2, 3, 4 \rbrace$** (Kruskal): edges $\lbrace 3,4 \rbrace$ ($2$), $\lbrace 2,4 \rbrace$ ($3$), $\lbrace 1,3 \rbrace$ ($4$). Total $9$.

Minimum weight: $9 + 1 = 10$: (D).

- (A) $7$ is the unconstrained MST ($0$-$1$, $0$-$3$, $3$-$4$, $2$-$4$ with weights $1+1+2+3$), but there $0$ has degree $2$, so it is not a leaf.
- (C) $9$ is the MST of the other four vertices alone; it forgets to attach $0$.

**Code check:** over all $4$-edge subsets of the $10$ edges, the cheapest tree with $\deg(0) = 1$ weighs $10$.

**Key idea:** a leaf can be peeled off, so the problem splits into "cheapest edge at the leaf" plus "MST of the rest".

</details>
