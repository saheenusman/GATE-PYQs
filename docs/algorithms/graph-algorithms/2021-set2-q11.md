# 2021 CS2 Q11: Minimum weight edges and uniqueness of the MST

## Question

Let $G$ be a connected undirected weighted graph. Consider the following two statements.

$S_1$: There exists a minimum weight edge in $G$ which is present in every minimum spanning tree of $G$.

$S_2$: If every edge in $G$ has distinct weight, then $G$ has a unique minimum spanning tree.

Which one of the following options is correct?

- (A) Both $S_1$ and $S_2$ are true.
- (B) $S_1$ is true and $S_2$ is false.
- (C) $S_1$ is false and $S_2$ is true.
- (D) Both $S_1$ and $S_2$ are false.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**$S_1$ is false.** Take a triangle with all three edges of weight 1. Every edge has the minimum weight, and the minimum spanning trees are the 3 pairs of edges. No single edge lies in all three. (If you break ties with distinct weights, the lightest edge is always in the MST, but $S_1$ does not assume distinct weights.)

**$S_2$ is true.** With distinct weights, at each step of Kruskal's algorithm the cheapest edge crossing a cut is unique, so the MST is unique.

So (C). I checked both by brute force: the equal-weight triangle has 3 MSTs and no edge common to all, and 300 random graphs with distinct weights each had exactly one MST.

**Key idea:** distinct edge weights give a unique MST, but ties can make even the lightest edge optional.

</details>
