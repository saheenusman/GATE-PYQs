# 2022 CS Q49: Properties of minimum spanning trees with distinct weights

## Question

Consider a simple undirected weighted graph $G$, all of whose edge weights are distinct. Which of the following statements about the minimum spanning trees of $G$ is/are TRUE?

- (A) The edge with the second smallest weight is always part of any minimum spanning tree of $G$.
- (B) One or both of the edges with the third smallest and the fourth smallest weights are part of any minimum spanning tree of $G$.
- (C) Suppose $S \subseteq V$ be such that $S \ne \emptyset$ and $S \ne V$. Consider the edge with the minimum weight such that one of its vertices is in $S$ and the other in $V \setminus S$. Such an edge will always be part of any minimum spanning tree of $G$.
- (D) $G$ can have multiple minimum spanning trees.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A, B and C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Think of Kruskal's algorithm.** With distinct weights, the MST is unique and Kruskal adds edges in sorted order unless they close a cycle.

- **(A) True.** The two lightest edges cannot form a cycle, since a cycle in a simple graph needs at least 3 edges. Both are taken.
- **(B) True.** The third edge is skipped only if it closes a cycle with edges 1 and 2, which means all three form a triangle. Then edge 4 cannot close a cycle with just edges 1 and 2 (it is not the missing triangle edge, since that is edge 3 and the graph is simple). So at least one of edges 3 and 4 is taken.
- **(C) True.** This is the cut property: the lightest edge across any cut belongs to every MST when weights are distinct.
- **(D) False.** Distinct weights make the MST unique.

**Key idea:** the cut property and Kruskal's order explain which edges are forced; distinct weights give a unique MST.

</details>
