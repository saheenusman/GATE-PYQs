# 2023 CS Q29: f(n) = n versus g(n) = n²

## Question

Let $f$ and $g$ be functions of natural numbers given by $f(n) = n$ and $g(n) = n^2$.

Which of the following statements is/are TRUE?

- (A) $f \in O(g)$
- (B) $f \in \Omega(g)$
- (C) $f \in o(g)$
- (D) $f \in \Theta(g)$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A and C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Compare the ratio** $f(n)/g(n) = 1/n \to 0$ as $n \to \infty$.

- $f \in O(g)$: ratio is bounded. **True.**
- $f \in o(g)$: ratio tends to 0. **True.**
- $f \in \Omega(g)$: would need $n \ge c\,n^2$ for large $n$. **False.**
- $f \in \Theta(g)$: needs both $O$ and $\Omega$. **False.**

**Key idea:** ratio tends to 0 means little-o (and so big-O). Ratio tends to a positive constant means $\Theta$.

</details>
