# 2026 CS1 Q17: Two coupled recurrences

## Question

Consider the following recurrence relations. For all $n > 1$:

$$T_1(n) = 4\,T_1\!\left(\tfrac{n}{2}\right) + T_2(n)$$

$$T_2(n) = 5\,T_2\!\left(\tfrac{n}{4}\right) + \Theta(\log_2 n)$$

Assume $T_1(n) = T_2(n) = 1$ for all $n \le 1$. Which one of the following is correct?

- (A) $T_1(n) = \Theta(n^2)$
- (B) $T_1(n) = \Theta(n^2 \log_2 n)$
- (C) $T_1(n) = \Theta(n^{\log_4 5})$
- (D) $T_1(n) = \Theta(n^{\log_4 5} \log_2 n)$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Refresher (Master theorem).** For $T(n) = aT(n/b) + f(n)$, compare $f(n)$ with $n^{\log_b a}$:

| Case | Condition | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $f$ polynomially smaller | $\Theta(n^{\log_b a})$ |
| 2 | $f = \Theta(n^{\log_b a})$ | $\Theta(n^{\log_b a} \log n)$ |
| 3 | $f$ polynomially larger | $\Theta(f)$ |

**Step 1: solve $T_2$ first.** Here $a=5$, $b=4$.

$\log_4 5 = \frac{\log_2 5}{2} \approx 1.161$.

$f(n) = \Theta(\log n)$ is polynomially smaller than $n^{1.161}$, so case 1 gives

$$T_2(n) = \Theta(n^{\log_4 5}).$$

**Step 2: plug into $T_1$.** Here $a=4$, $b=2$, so $n^{\log_2 4} = n^2$.

The extra term is $T_2(n) = \Theta(n^{1.161})$. That is polynomially smaller than $n^2$, so case 1 again:

$$T_1(n) = \Theta(n^2).$$

**Numeric check.** Computing the recurrences exactly for $n = 4^k$ (up to $n \approx 1.6 \times 10^7$):

| $n$ | $T_1/n^2$ | $T_2/n^{\log_4 5}$ |
|---|---|---|
| $4^6$ | 3.916 | 1.625 |
| $4^{10}$ | 3.919 | 1.625 |
| $4^{12}$ | 3.919 | 1.625 |

Both ratios settle to constants, which matches $\Theta$.

**Why the others fail:**

- **(B)** needs the extra term to be $\Theta(n^2)$ (case 2). It is much smaller.
- **(C)** is the growth of $T_2$. $T_1$ keeps the $4T_1(n/2)$ part, which is bigger.
- **(D)** mixes the exponent of $T_2$ with a $\log$ factor. Nothing in the recurrence produces that.

**Key idea:** when a recurrence has a smaller recurrence inside its $f(n)$, solve the inner one first, then compare it with $n^{\log_b a}$.

</details>
