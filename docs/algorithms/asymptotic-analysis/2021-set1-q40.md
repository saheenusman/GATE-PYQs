# 2021 CS1 Q40: Recurrence T(n) = T(n/2) + T(2n/5) + 7n

## Question

Consider the following recurrence relation.

$$T(n)=\begin{cases}T(n/2)+T(2n/5)+7n & \text{if } n>0\\ 1 & \text{if } n=0\end{cases}$$

Which one of the following options is correct?

- (A) $T(n)=\Theta(n^{5/2})$
- (B) $T(n)=\Theta(n\log n)$
- (C) $T(n)=\Theta(n)$
- (D) $T(n)=\Theta((\log n)^{5/2})$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Recursion tree.** The sizes of the two children add up to $\dfrac n2+\dfrac{2n}5=\dfrac{9n}{10}<n$. So each level costs at most $0.9$ times the level above it, and the level costs form a decreasing geometric series:

$$T(n)\le 7n\,(1+0.9+0.9^2+\cdots)=70n$$

Also $T(n)\ge 7n$ from the root. So $T(n)=\Theta(n)$.

(A) and (B) over-estimate (they would need the child sizes to sum to $n$ or more). (D) under-estimates, since the root alone costs $7n$.

Computed numerically with a memoised function (integer division): $T(n)/n$ is 42.9, 55.2, 61.9, 65.6 for $n=10^3,10^5,10^7,10^9$. It rises slowly towards the limit 70 and stays bounded.

**Key idea:** if the subproblem sizes sum to a fraction $<1$ of $n$, the root dominates and $T(n)=\Theta(\text{non-recursive cost})$.

</details>
