# 2014 CS2 Q38: Worst-case comparisons for optimal merging of five sorted lists

## Question

Suppose P, Q, R, S, T are sorted sequences having lengths 20, 24, 30, 35, 50 respectively. They are to be merged into a single sequence by merging together two sequences at a time. The number of comparisons that will be needed in the worst case by the optimal algorithm for doing this is ____.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**358**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Merging sorted lists of sizes $m$ and $n$ takes $m + n - 1$ comparisons in the worst case. The optimal merge order is greedy (as in Huffman coding): always merge the two smallest lists.

| Step | Merge | Result | Comparisons |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 20 + 24 | 44 | 43 |
| 2 | 30 + 35 | 65 | 64 |
| 3 | 44 + 50 | 94 | 93 |
| 4 | 65 + 94 | 159 | 158 |

$$43 + 64 + 93 + 158 = 358$$

**Code check:** a priority-queue implementation gives the same four merges and the total 358.

**Key idea:** optimal merge pattern: merge the two shortest lists first.

</details>
