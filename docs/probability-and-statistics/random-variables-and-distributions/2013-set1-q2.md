# 2013 CS Q2: Poisson cars per minute

## Question

Suppose $p$ is the number of cars per minute passing through a certain road junction between 5 PM and 6 PM, and $p$ has a Poisson distribution with mean 3.
What is the probability of observing fewer than 3 cars during any given minute in this interval?

- (A) $8/(2e^3)$
- (B) $9/(2e^3)$
- (C) $17/(2e^3)$
- (D) $26/(2e^3)$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

For a Poisson variable with mean $\lambda = 3$: $P(X=k) = e^{-3}\,3^k/k!$.

$$P(X<3) = e^{-3}\left(1 + 3 + \tfrac{9}{2}\right) = e^{-3}\cdot\tfrac{17}{2} = \frac{17}{2e^3}$$

| $k$ | $P(X=k)\cdot e^{3}$ |
|---|---|
| 0 | 1 |
| 1 | 3 |
| 2 | 9/2 |
| sum | 17/2 |

**Why the others fail:**

- (A) $8/(2e^3) = 4e^{-3}$ is $P(X\le 1)$; it stops one term early.
- (B) $9/(2e^3)$ is only the $k=2$ term, $P(X=2)$.
- (D) $26/(2e^3) = 13e^{-3}$ is $P(X\le 3)$; it wrongly includes $k=3$.

**Code check:** the numeric value is $0.42319$, equal to $17/(2e^3)$.

**Key idea:** "fewer than 3" means $k=0,1,2$; sum the Poisson pmf over those values.

</details>
