# 2017 CS1 Q19: Median of max(X, 0) for a zero-mean Gaussian X

## Question

Let $X$ be a Gaussian random variable with mean $0$ and variance $\sigma^2$. Let $Y = \max(X, 0)$ where $\max(a,b)$ is the maximum of $a$ and $b$. The median of $Y$ is ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**0**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Distribution of $Y$.** Since $X$ is symmetric about $0$, $P(X \le 0) = \tfrac12$. So

$$P(Y = 0) = P(X \le 0) = \tfrac12$$

and the other half of the probability sits on $Y>0$.

**Median.** A median $m$ satisfies $P(Y \le m) \ge \tfrac12$ and $P(Y \ge m) \ge \tfrac12$.

- $m = 0$: $P(Y \le 0) = \tfrac12$ and $P(Y \ge 0) = 1$. Both hold.
- $m < 0$: $P(Y \le m) = 0$. Fails.
- $m > 0$: $P(Y \ge m) = P(X \ge m) < \tfrac12$. Fails.

So the median is **0**, for every $\sigma$.

**Code check:** a simulation of 200,001 draws with $\sigma=3$ gave $49.9\%$ exact zeros and a sample median of $0.0085$, which is $0$ up to sampling noise (the zero mass is exactly $\tfrac12$).

**Key idea:** clipping at $0$ puts probability $\tfrac12$ on the point $0$, so the median lands exactly on that point mass.

</details>
