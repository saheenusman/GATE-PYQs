# 2008 CS Q29: Standard deviation of a normal variable from a probability equality

## Question

Let $X$ be a random variable following normal distribution with mean $+1$ and variance $4$. Let $Y$ be another normal variable with mean $-1$ and variance unknown. If $P(X \le -1) = P(Y \ge 2)$, the standard deviation of $Y$ is

- (A) 3
- (B) 2
- (C) $\sqrt{2}$
- (D) 1

<summary>Solution</summary>

**Official key:** the key table in the supplied PDF has no entry for Q29, so I cannot check against it. The answer below is my own derivation.

Standardise both sides.

$X \sim N(1, 4)$ has standard deviation $2$:

$$P(X \le -1) = P\!\left(Z \le \frac{-1 - 1}{2}\right) = P(Z \le -1).$$

$Y \sim N(-1, \sigma^2)$:

$$P(Y \ge 2) = P\!\left(Z \ge \frac{2 - (-1)}{\sigma}\right) = P\!\left(Z \ge \frac{3}{\sigma}\right) = P\!\left(Z \le -\frac{3}{\sigma}\right)$$

(by the symmetry of the normal curve).

Equal probabilities require $-1 = -3/\sigma$, so $\sigma = 3$: (A).

- (B) 2 and (D) 1 would give $P(Y \ge 2)$ of $0.067$ and $0.0013$, which are smaller than $0.1587$; (C) $\sqrt{2}$ gives $0.017$.

**Code check:** $P(X \le -1) = 0.15866$. $P(Y \ge 2)$ is $0.15866$ for $\sigma = 3$, $0.06681$ for $\sigma = 2$, $0.01695$ for $\sigma = \sqrt{2}$ and $0.00135$ for $\sigma = 1$.

**Key idea:** convert both sides to the standard normal; equal tail probabilities mean equal $z$-scores (up to sign).
