# 2024 CS1 Q14: Order of 1,2 and 3,4 in a random permutation

## Question

Consider a permutation sampled uniformly at random from the set of all permutations of $\lbrace 1, 2, 3, \cdots, n \rbrace$ for some $n \ge 4$. Let $X$ be the event that 1 occurs before 2 in the permutation, and $Y$ the event that 3 occurs before 4. Which one of the following statements is TRUE?

- (A) The events $X$ and $Y$ are mutually exclusive
- (B) The events $X$ and $Y$ are independent
- (C) Either event $X$ or $Y$ must occur
- (D) Event $X$ is more likely than event $Y$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**$P(X)$.** By symmetry, among the relative orders of 1 and 2, "1 before 2" and "2 before 1" are equally likely: $P(X) = \tfrac{1}{2}$. Likewise $P(Y) = \tfrac{1}{2}$.

**$P(X \cap Y)$.** Look only at the relative order of the four elements $1,2,3,4$; all $4!$ orders are equally likely. The order of $\lbrace 1, 2 \rbrace$ and the order of $\lbrace 3, 4 \rbrace$ are independent (each of the 4 combinations of "pair $\lbrace 1,2 \rbrace$ order" and "pair $\lbrace 3,4 \rbrace$ order" covers exactly 6 of the 24 orders). So

$$P(X \cap Y) = \tfrac{1}{4} = P(X)\,P(Y).$$

So $X$ and $Y$ are **independent**. (Checked by brute force for $n = 4, 5, 6$.)

**Why the others fail:**

- **(A)** $P(X \cap Y) = \tfrac14 \ne 0$.
- **(C)** both can fail together, e.g. 2 before 1 and 4 before 3.
- **(D)** both have probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$.

**Key idea:** the relative order of one pair of elements does not affect the relative order of a disjoint pair.

</details>
