# 2011 CS Q34: Two cards drawn with consecutive numbers in decreasing order

## Question

A deck of 5 cards (each carrying a distinct number from 1 to 5) is shuffled thoroughly. Two cards are then removed one at a time from the deck. What is the probability that the two cards are selected with the number on the first card being one higher than the number on the second card?

- (A) 1/5
- (B) 4/25
- (C) 1/4
- (D) 2/5

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

The two cards form an ordered pair of distinct numbers: $5 \times 4 = 20$ equally likely outcomes.

Favourable pairs (first, second) with first $=$ second $+ 1$: $(2,1), (3,2), (4,3), (5,4)$, that is $4$.

$$P = \frac{4}{20} = \frac15$$

So (A).

- (B) $\frac{4}{25}$ would be the answer if the first card were put back ($5 \times 5$ outcomes). The cards are removed, so there are $20$ outcomes.
- (C) $\frac14$ and (D) $\frac25$ do not match $4$ favourable pairs out of $20$.

**Code check:** enumerating the $20$ ordered pairs gave $4$ favourable, $P = 1/5$.

**Key idea:** count ordered draws without replacement (5 x 4) and the favourable pairs.

</details>
