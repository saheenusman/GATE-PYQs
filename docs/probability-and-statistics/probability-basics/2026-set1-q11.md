# 2026 CS1 Q11: Urn that adds a ball of the drawn colour

## Question

An urn contains one red ball and one blue ball. At each step, a ball is picked uniformly at random from the urn, and this ball together with another ball of the same color is put back in the urn. The probability that there are equal number of red and blue balls after two steps is

- (A) $1/4$
- (B) $1/3$
- (C) $1/2$
- (D) $2/3$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

After two steps there are 4 balls. Equal numbers means **2 red and 2 blue**, so the two draws must be of **different colours**.

**Case 1: red first, then blue.**

- Step 1: $P(R) = \frac{1}{2}$. Urn becomes 2R, 1B.
- Step 2: $P(B) = \frac{1}{3}$. Urn becomes 2R, 2B. 
- Probability: $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{6}$

**Case 2: blue first, then red.** Symmetric: $\frac{1}{2} \times \frac{1}{3} = \frac{1}{6}$.

Total $= \frac{1}{6} + \frac{1}{6} = \frac{1}{3}$.

**Why the others fail:** $\frac{1}{4}$ and $\frac{1}{2}$ come from treating the second draw as if the urn were unchanged (1 red of 2). The urn has 3 balls at step 2, so the second draw is out of 3.

**Key idea:** the draw changes the urn (Pólya urn), so use the updated counts at step 2.

</details>
