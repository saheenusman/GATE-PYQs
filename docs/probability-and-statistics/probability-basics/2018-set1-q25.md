# 2018 CS Q25: Probability that a win happens on the third trial

## Question

Two people, P and Q, decide to independently roll two identical dice, each with 6 faces, numbered 1 to 6. The person with the lower number wins. In case of a tie, they roll the dice repeatedly until there is no tie. Define a trial as a throw of the dice by P and Q. Assume that all 6 numbers on each dice are equi-probable and that all trials are independent. The probability (rounded to 3 decimal places) that one of them wins on the third trial is _____.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**0.021 to 0.024**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**A trial ties with probability** $\dfrac{6}{36} = \dfrac{1}{6}$, so it is decisive with probability $\dfrac{5}{6}$.

**"Wins on the third trial"** means trials 1 and 2 tie, and trial 3 does not:

$$\left(\frac16\right)^2\cdot\frac56 = \frac{5}{216} \approx 0.0231$$

So the answer is **0.023**, inside the key's range 0.021 to 0.024.

**Key idea:** independent trials, so multiply: tie, tie, then a decisive trial. Which player wins does not matter.

</details>
