# 2021 CS1 Q64: Bayes on a noisy channel for signals H and L

## Question

A sender (S) transmits a signal, which can be one of the two kinds: $H$ and $L$ with probabilities 0.1 and 0.9 respectively, to a receiver (R).

In the graph below, the weight of edge $(u,v)$ is the probability of receiving $v$ when $u$ is transmitted, where $u,v\in\lbrace H,L\rbrace$. For example, the probability that the received signal is $L$ given the transmitted signal was $H$, is 0.7.

![A bipartite graph. Sender nodes H (top) and L (bottom) on the left, receiver nodes H and L on the right. Edge H to H has weight 0.3. Edge L to L has weight 0.2. The diagonal edge from H (sender) to L (receiver) has weight 0.7. The diagonal edge from L (sender) to H (receiver) has weight 0.8.](../../assets/2021/set1-q64.png)

If the received signal is $H$, the probability that the transmitted signal was $H$ (rounded to 2 decimal places) is ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**0.04**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

From the graph: $P(R{=}H\mid S{=}H)=0.3$ and $P(R{=}H\mid S{=}L)=0.8$. The priors are $P(S{=}H)=0.1$ and $P(S{=}L)=0.9$.

By Bayes' theorem:

$$P(S{=}H\mid R{=}H)=\frac{0.1\times0.3}{0.1\times0.3+0.9\times0.8}=\frac{0.03}{0.03+0.72}=\frac{0.03}{0.75}=\mathbf{0.04}.$$

Checked in a script: 0.04.

The result is small because $L$ is 9 times more likely and also gets received as $H$ more often than $H$ does.

**Key idea:** Bayes' theorem: posterior $\propto$ prior $\times$ likelihood.

</details>
