# 2019 CS2 Q32: Probability two numbers share the same leading bit

## Question

Two numbers are chosen independently and uniformly at random from the set $\lbrace 1, 2, \dots, 13 \rbrace$. The probability (rounded off to 3 decimal places) that their 4-bit (unsigned) binary representations have the same most significant bit is ________.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**0.503**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

In 4 bits the most significant bit is 1 for $8 \le n \le 15$.

| MSB | Numbers in $1..13$ | Count |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1 to 7 | 7 |
| 1 | 8 to 13 | 6 |

Both numbers share the MSB if both are in the first group or both in the second:

$$P = \left(\frac{7}{13}\right)^2 + \left(\frac{6}{13}\right)^2 = \frac{49 + 36}{169} = \frac{85}{169} \approx 0.503.$$

The official key accepts **0.502 to 0.504**. A direct count over all 169 pairs confirms $85/169$.

**Key idea:** split the sample space by the bit value and add the squared probabilities.

</details>
