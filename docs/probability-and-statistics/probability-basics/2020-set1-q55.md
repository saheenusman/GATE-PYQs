# 2020 CS Q55: Parity of a random subset sum

## Question

For $n > 2$, let $a \in \lbrace 0, 1 \rbrace^n$ be a non-zero vector. Suppose that $x$ is chosen uniformly at random from $\lbrace 0, 1 \rbrace^n$. Then, the probability that $\sum_{i=1}^{n} a_i x_i$ is an odd number is ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**0.5**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Pairing argument.** Since $a \neq 0$, pick an index $j$ with $a_j = 1$.

Pair each $x$ with $x'$, which is $x$ with bit $j$ flipped. This pairs up all $2^n$ vectors.

- The sums for $x$ and $x'$ differ by exactly $a_j \times (\pm 1) = \pm 1$.
- So one of them has an odd sum and the other has an even sum.

Hence exactly half of the $2^n$ vectors give an odd sum:

$$P(\text{odd}) = \frac{2^{n-1}}{2^n} = \frac{1}{2}.$$

**Alternative view.** Let $S$ be the set of indices where $a_i = 1$, with $|S| = m \ge 1$. The sum is the number of 1's among $x_i$, $i \in S$. It is odd for $2^{m-1}$ of the $2^m$ patterns, and the other $n - m$ bits are free. The ratio is again $1/2$.

I checked $n = 3, 4, 5$ by enumerating every non-zero $a$ and every $x$. The probability was exactly 0.5 each time.

**Key idea:** flipping one bit with $a_j = 1$ flips the parity of the sum, so odd and even outcomes are equally likely. The value of $n$ does not matter.

</details>
