# 2010 CS Q27: Chance that a divisor of 10^99 is a multiple of 10^96

## Question

What is the probability that a divisor of $10^{99}$ is a multiple of $10^{96}$?

- (A) $1/625$
- (B) $4/625$
- (C) $12/625$
- (D) $16/625$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

$10^{99} = 2^{99} \cdot 5^{99}$, so each divisor is $2^a 5^b$ with $0 \le a, b \le 99$. That gives $100 \times 100 = 10^4$ divisors, all equally likely.

A divisor is a multiple of $10^{96} = 2^{96} 5^{96}$ exactly when $a \ge 96$ and $b \ge 96$. Each of $a$ and $b$ has $4$ choices ($96$ to $99$), so there are $4 \times 4 = 16$ such divisors.

$$P = \frac{16}{10^4} = \frac{1}{625}$$

So (A).

- (D) $16/625$ is $100$ times too large: it uses $16$ favourable cases out of only $625$.
- (B) and (C) correspond to no natural count of favourable divisors over $10^4$.

**Code check:** enumerating all $10^4$ exponent pairs gave $16$ favourable, i.e. $16/10000 = 1/625$.

**Key idea:** divisors of $p^a q^b$ are pairs of exponents; a multiple condition becomes lower bounds on the exponents.

</details>
