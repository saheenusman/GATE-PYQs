# 2023 CS Q53: Independence of coin-toss events

## Question

Consider a random experiment where two fair coins are tossed. Let $A$ be the event that denotes HEAD on both the throws, $B$ be the event that denotes HEAD on the first throw, and $C$ be the event that denotes HEAD on the second throw.

Which of the following statements is/are TRUE?

- (A) $A$ and $B$ are independent.
- (B) $A$ and $C$ are independent.
- (C) $B$ and $C$ are independent.
- (D) $\text{Prob}(B \mid C) = \text{Prob}(B)$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C and D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Probabilities.** The sample space is $\lbrace HH, HT, TH, TT \rbrace$, each with probability $\tfrac14$.

- $P(A) = \tfrac14$, $P(B) = \tfrac12$, $P(C) = \tfrac12$.
- $P(A \cap B) = P(HH) = \tfrac14$ and $P(A \cap C) = \tfrac14$.
- $P(B \cap C) = P(HH) = \tfrac14$.

**Independence test** ($P(X \cap Y) = P(X)P(Y)$):

| Pair | $P(X)P(Y)$ | $P(X \cap Y)$ | Independent? |
|---|---|---|---|
| $A, B$ | $\tfrac18$ | $\tfrac14$ | no |
| $A, C$ | $\tfrac18$ | $\tfrac14$ | no |
| $B, C$ | $\tfrac14$ | $\tfrac14$ | **yes** |

**D.** $P(B \mid C) = \dfrac{P(B \cap C)}{P(C)} = \dfrac{1/4}{1/2} = \tfrac12 = P(B)$. True, and this is just independence of $B$ and $C$ restated.

**Key idea:** the two throws are independent of each other, but "both heads" depends on each throw.

</details>
