# 2014 CS2 Q1: Probability that a 10-computer security system is deemed functional

## Question

The security system at an IT office is composed of 10 computers of which exactly four are working. To check whether the system is functional, the officials inspect four of the computers picked at random (without replacement). The system is deemed functional if at least three of the four computers inspected are working. Let the probability that the system is deemed functional be denoted by $p$. Then $100p =$ _____________.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**11.9**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

There are $\binom{10}{4} = 210$ equally likely ways to pick 4 computers. Of the 10, 4 work and 6 do not.

- Exactly 3 working: $\binom{4}{3}\binom{6}{1} = 4 \times 6 = 24$
- All 4 working: $\binom{4}{4} = 1$

$$p = \frac{24 + 1}{210} = \frac{25}{210} = \frac{5}{42}$$

$$100p = \frac{250}{21} \approx 11.9$$

**Code check:** exact fraction arithmetic gives $250/21 = 11.905$.

**Key idea:** hypergeometric counting, summing the cases with at least 3 working.

</details>
