# 2025 CS1 Q32: Probability that a coin showing two heads is the fake one

## Question

A box contains 5 coins: 4 regular coins and 1 fake coin. When a regular coin is tossed, the probability $P(head) = 0.5$ and for a fake coin, $P(head) = 1$. You pick a coin at random and toss it twice, and get two heads. The probability that the coin you have chosen is the fake coin is _______. (rounded off to two decimal places)

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**0.50**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Use **Bayes' theorem**. Let $F$ = the chosen coin is fake and $HH$ = two heads.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| $P(F)$ | $\frac15$ |
| $P(\text{regular})$ | $\frac45$ |
| $P(HH \mid F)$ | $1 \cdot 1 = 1$ |
| $P(HH \mid \text{regular})$ | $0.5 \times 0.5 = \frac14$ |

$$P(F \mid HH) = \frac{P(HH \mid F)\,P(F)}{P(HH \mid F)\,P(F) + P(HH \mid \text{reg})\,P(\text{reg})} = \frac{\frac15}{\frac15 + \frac14\cdot\frac45} = \frac{0.2}{0.2 + 0.2} = 0.50.$$

**Key idea:** seeing evidence that is more likely under one hypothesis raises its probability. Compare the two "weights" $0.2$ and $0.2$.

</details>
