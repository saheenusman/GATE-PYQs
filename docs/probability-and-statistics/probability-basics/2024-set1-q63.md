# 2024 CS1 Q63: Both balls red given the first is red

## Question

A bag contains 10 red balls and 15 blue balls. Two balls are drawn randomly without replacement. Given that the first ball drawn is red, the probability (rounded off to 3 decimal places) that both balls drawn are red is ________

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**0.375**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Given that the first ball is red, 9 red and 15 blue balls remain, 24 in all. Both are red exactly when the second ball is also red:

$$P(\text{both red} \mid \text{first red}) = \frac{9}{24} = 0.375.$$

**Same result by the formula:**

$$\frac{P(\text{both red})}{P(\text{first red})} = \frac{\tfrac{10}{25}\cdot\tfrac{9}{24}}{\tfrac{10}{25}} = \frac{9}{24}.$$

**Key idea:** conditioning on the first draw leaves a smaller bag. The probability is just the chance the second ball is red.

</details>
