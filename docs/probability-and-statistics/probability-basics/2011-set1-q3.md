# 2011 CS Q3: Both heads given that at least one is a head

## Question

If two fair coins are flipped and at least one of the outcomes is known to be a head, what is the probability that both outcomes are heads?

- (A) 1/3
- (B) 1/4
- (C) 1/2
- (D) 2/3

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

The sample space of two flips is $\lbrace HH, HT, TH, TT \rbrace$, each with probability $\tfrac14$.

The condition "at least one head" removes $TT$, leaving $\lbrace HH, HT, TH \rbrace$.

$$P(HH \mid \text{at least one H}) = \frac{P(HH)}{P(\text{at least one H})} = \frac{1/4}{3/4} = \frac13$$

So the answer is (A).

- (B) $\tfrac14$ is the unconditional probability of $HH$.
- (C) $\tfrac12$ would be right if we knew a *particular* coin (say the first) was a head. "At least one" does not say which.
- (D) $\tfrac23$ is the probability of exactly one head given at least one head.

**Code check:** enumerating the four outcomes gave $P = 1/3$.

**Key idea:** conditioning on 'at least one' is different from conditioning on a specific coin; count outcomes in the reduced sample space.

</details>
