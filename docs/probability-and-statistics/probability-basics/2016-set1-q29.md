# 2016 CS1 Q29: Probability that a repeated coin-flip experiment outputs Y

## Question

Consider the following experiment.

- Step 1. Flip a fair coin twice.
- Step 2. If the outcomes are (TAILS, HEADS) then output Y and stop.
- Step 3. If the outcomes are either (HEADS, HEADS) or (HEADS, TAILS), then output N and stop.
- Step 4. If the outcomes are (TAILS, TAILS), then go to Step 1.

The probability that the output of the experiment is Y is (up to two decimal places) ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**0.33**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Each round has four equally likely outcomes, each with probability $\tfrac14$:

| Outcome | Result |
|---|---|
| TH | output Y, stop |
| HH, HT | output N, stop |
| TT | repeat |

**Condition on the round stopping.** TT only repeats the experiment, so ignore it. Among the three stopping outcomes, one gives Y:

$$P(Y) = \frac{1/4}{1 - 1/4} = \frac{1}{3} \approx 0.33$$

**Same answer by summing the series.** $P(Y) = \tfrac14 \sum_{k\ge 0} \left(\tfrac14\right)^k = \tfrac14 \cdot \tfrac{1}{1 - 1/4} = \tfrac13$.

**Key idea:** repeat-on-TT means $P(Y) = (1/4)/(1 - 1/4) = 1/3$.

</details>
