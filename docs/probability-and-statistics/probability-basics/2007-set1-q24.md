# 2007 CS Q24: Probability that 2 comes before every other even number

## Question

Suppose we uniformly and randomly select a permutation from the $20!$ permutations of $1, 2, 3, \dots, 20$. What is the probability that $2$ appears at an earlier position than any other even number in the selected permutation?

- (A) $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- (B) $\dfrac{1}{10}$
- (C) $\dfrac{9!}{20!}$
- (D) None of the above

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

There are $10$ even numbers $2, 4, \dots, 20$. In a uniformly random permutation, the relative order of these ten numbers is itself uniformly random, so each of them is equally likely to be the first even number to appear.

$$P(2 \text{ is first among the evens}) = \frac{1}{10}.$$

(B). Check by counting: choose positions for the 10 evens ($\binom{20}{10}$), put 2 first in their slots ($9!$ orders for the other evens), arrange the odds ($10!$): $\binom{20}{10}\,9!\,10!/20! = 9!\,/\,10! = 1/10$.

- (A) $1/2$ would be the answer against a single other number, not nine others.
- (C) $9!/20!$ is about $1.5\times10^{-13}$, far too small: the event covers a large fraction of all permutations, not a handful of them.
- (D) is wrong since (B) is correct.

**Code check:** Python Monte Carlo, 300,000 random permutations of $1..20$: observed frequency $0.0995$ against $0.1$.
