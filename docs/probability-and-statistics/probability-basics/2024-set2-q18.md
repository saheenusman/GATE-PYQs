# 2024 CS2 Q18: Six dice all showing different numbers

## Question

When six unbiased dice are rolled simultaneously, the probability of getting all distinct numbers (i.e., 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6) is

- (A) $\dfrac{1}{324}$
- (B) $\dfrac{5}{324}$
- (C) $\dfrac{7}{324}$
- (D) $\dfrac{11}{324}$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Total outcomes:** $6^6 = 46656$.

**Favourable:** the six dice show a permutation of $1..6$, which is $6! = 720$.

$$\frac{720}{46656} = \frac{5}{324}.$$

(Divide both by 144.)

**Key idea:** all-distinct outcomes of $n$ dice with $n$ faces number $n!$ out of $n^n$.

</details>
