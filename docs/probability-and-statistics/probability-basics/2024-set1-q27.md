# 2024 CS1 Q27: Events with P(A)=0.3, P(B)=0.5, P(A∩B)=0.1

## Question

Let $A$ and $B$ be two events in a probability space with $P(A) = 0.3$, $P(B) = 0.5$, and $P(A \cap B) = 0.1$. Which of the following statements is/are TRUE?

- (A) The two events $A$ and $B$ are independent
- (B) $P(A \cup B) = 0.7$
- (C) $P(A \cap B^c) = 0.2$, where $B^c$ is the complement of the event $B$
- (D) $P(A^c \cap B^c) = 0.4$, where $A^c$ and $B^c$ are the complements of the events $A$ and $B$, respectively

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B and C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

- **(A) False.** Independence needs $P(A \cap B) = P(A)P(B) = 0.3 \times 0.5 = 0.15$. Here it is 0.1.
- **(B) True.** $P(A \cup B) = 0.3 + 0.5 - 0.1 = 0.7$.
- **(C) True.** $P(A \cap B^c) = P(A) - P(A \cap B) = 0.3 - 0.1 = 0.2$.
- **(D) False.** $P(A^c \cap B^c) = 1 - P(A \cup B) = 1 - 0.7 = 0.3$, not 0.4.

**Key idea:** use $P(A \cup B)$ by inclusion-exclusion, and $P(A \cap B^c) = P(A) - P(A \cap B)$.

</details>
