# 2025 CS2 Q64: Chance that a square-invariant quadratic has equal roots

## Question

A quadratic polynomial $(x - \alpha)(x - \beta)$ over complex numbers is said to be square invariant if $(x - \alpha)(x - \beta) = (x - \alpha^2)(x - \beta^2)$. Suppose from the set of all square invariant quadratic polynomials we choose one at random.

The probability that the roots of the chosen polynomial are equal is __________. (rounded off to one decimal place)

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**0.5**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

The polynomials are equal iff their roots agree as a multiset: $\lbrace\alpha,\beta\rbrace = \lbrace\alpha^2,\beta^2\rbrace$. Two cases:

**Case 1: $\alpha^2 = \alpha$ and $\beta^2 = \beta$.** Then $\alpha, \beta \in \lbrace 0, 1\rbrace$, giving the polynomials

- $x^2$ (roots 0, 0),
- $x(x-1)$ (roots 0, 1),
- $(x-1)^2$ (roots 1, 1).

**Case 2: $\alpha^2 = \beta$ and $\beta^2 = \alpha$ with $\alpha \ne \beta$.** Then $\alpha^4 = \alpha$, so $\alpha(\alpha^3 - 1) = 0$. With $\alpha \ne 0$ and $\alpha \ne 1$ (those were case 1) we need $\alpha = \omega$, a primitive cube root of unity, and $\beta = \omega^2$. This gives $(x-\omega)(x-\omega^2) = x^2 + x + 1$.

So there are exactly **4** square-invariant quadratics:

| Polynomial | Equal roots? |
|---|---|
| $x^2$ | yes |
| $x^2 - x$ | no |
| $(x-1)^2$ | yes |
| $x^2 + x + 1$ | no |

Probability $= \dfrac{2}{4} = 0.5$.

A computer check over a pool of candidate roots (including cube and fifth roots of unity) found exactly these 4 pairs, 2 with equal roots.

**Key idea:** square-invariance means squaring permutes (or fixes) the roots, which restricts them to $0$, $1$ and the cube roots of unity.

</details>
