# 2012 CS Q33: Probability of a sum of at least 6 with a conditional second roll

## Question

Suppose a fair six-sided die is rolled once. If the value on the die is $1$, $2$, or $3$, the die is rolled a second time. What is the probability that the sum total of values that turn up is at least $6$?

- (A) $10/21$
- (B) $5/12$
- (C) $2/3$
- (D) $1/6$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Split on the first roll.

**First roll $4$ or $5$ or $6$** (probability $\tfrac{1}{2}$): no second roll. The sum is at least $6$ only for a $6$: probability $\tfrac{1}{6}$ of all outcomes.

**First roll $1$, $2$ or $3$** (each with probability $\tfrac{1}{6}$): roll again and need the second roll at least $6 - d_1$.

| First roll | Second roll needed | $P$ |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $\ge 5$ | $2/6$ |
| 2 | $\ge 4$ | $3/6$ |
| 3 | $\ge 3$ | $4/6$ |

Contribution: $\tfrac{1}{6}\cdot\tfrac{2+3+4}{6} = \tfrac{9}{36} = \tfrac{1}{4}$.

Total: $\tfrac{1}{6} + \tfrac{1}{4} = \tfrac{5}{12}$: (B).

**Code check:** exact fractions over all outcomes gave $5/12$.

**Key idea:** condition on the first roll, since only that decides whether there is a second one.

</details>
