# 2017 CS1 Q30: Scalar alpha for which u + alpha v bisects the angle between u and v

## Question

Let $u$ and $v$ be two vectors in $\mathbb{R}^2$ whose Euclidean norms satisfy $\|u\| = 2\|v\|$. What is the value of $\alpha$ such that $w = u + \alpha v$ bisects the angle between $u$ and $v$?

- (A) $2$
- (B) $1/2$
- (C) $1$
- (D) $-1/2$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Bisector direction.** The internal angle bisector of $u$ and $v$ points along the sum of the two *unit* vectors:

$$\frac{u}{\|u\|} + \frac{v}{\|v\|}$$

**Use $\|u\| = 2\|v\|$.**

$$\frac{u}{2\|v\|} + \frac{v}{\|v\|} = \frac{1}{2\|v\|}\,(u + 2v)$$

So the bisector is along $u + 2v$, i.e. $\alpha = 2$: **(A)**.

**Check.** Let $w = u + 2v$. Then

$$\cos\angle(w,u) = \frac{\|u\|^2 + 2\,u\cdot v}{\|u\|\,\|w\|},\qquad \cos\angle(w,v) = \frac{u\cdot v + 2\|v\|^2}{\|v\|\,\|w\|}$$

With $\|u\| = 2\|v\|$, the first becomes $\dfrac{4\|v\|^2 + 2\,u\cdot v}{2\|v\|\,\|w\|}$. The second, multiplied top and bottom by $2$, becomes $\dfrac{2\,u\cdot v + 4\|v\|^2}{2\|v\|\,\|w\|}$. They are equal, so the two angles match.

**Why the others fail.** For $\alpha = 1$, $\tfrac12$ or $-\tfrac12$ the two cosines above are no longer equal (the general formula is $\dfrac{\|u\|^2 + \alpha\,u\cdot v}{\|u\|\|w\|}$ versus $\dfrac{u\cdot v + \alpha\|v\|^2}{\|v\|\|w\|}$), so $w$ is not on the bisector.

**Code check:** for 1000 random pairs with $\|u\|=2\|v\|$, the angles $(w,u)$ and $(w,v)$ at $\alpha=2$ differed by at most $5\times10^{-14}$. At $\alpha = \tfrac12, 1, -\tfrac12$ the angles were clearly different for a sample pair.

**Key idea:** the bisector is $\dfrac{u}{\|u\|} + \dfrac{v}{\|v\|}$; a longer vector must be scaled down.

</details>
