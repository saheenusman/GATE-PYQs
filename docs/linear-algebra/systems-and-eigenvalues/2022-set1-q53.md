# 2022 CS Q53: Eigenvectors of a 4×4 matrix

## Question

Which of the following is/are the eigenvector(s) for the matrix given below?

$$M = \begin{pmatrix} -9 & -6 & -2 & -4 \\ -8 & -6 & -3 & -1 \\ 20 & 15 & 8 & 5 \\ 32 & 21 & 7 & 12 \end{pmatrix}$$

- (A) $(-1,\ 1,\ 0,\ 1)^T$
- (B) $(1,\ 0,\ -1,\ 0)^T$
- (C) $(-1,\ 0,\ 2,\ 2)^T$
- (D) $(0,\ 1,\ -3,\ 0)^T$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A, C and D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Multiply $M$ by each vector** and check whether the result is a scalar multiple of it.

| Vector | $Mv$ | Multiple of $v$? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (A) $(-1,1,0,1)$ | $(-1,1,0,1)$ | yes, $\lambda = 1$ |
| (B) $(1,0,-1,0)$ | $(-7,-5,12,25)$ | no |
| (C) $(-1,0,2,2)$ | $(-3,0,6,6)$ | yes, $\lambda = 3$ |
| (D) $(0,1,-3,0)$ | $(0,3,-9,0)$ | yes, $\lambda = 3$ |

So (A), (C), (D) are eigenvectors.

**Code check:** the eigenvalues of $M$ are $1$, $-2$ and $3$ (twice), consistent with the table; (C) and (D) both belong to $\lambda = 3$.

**Key idea:** $v$ is an eigenvector exactly when $Mv = \lambda v$ for some scalar $\lambda$.

</details>
