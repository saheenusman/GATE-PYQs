# 2017 CS2 Q52: Largest absolute eigenvalue from a characteristic polynomial with one known root

## Question

If the characteristic polynomial of a $3\times3$ matrix $M$ over $\mathbb{R}$ (the set of real numbers) is $\lambda^3 - 4\lambda^2 + a\lambda + 30$, $a \in \mathbb{R}$, and one eigenvalue of $M$ is 2, then the largest among the absolute values of the eigenvalues of $M$ is ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**5**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Find $a$.** An eigenvalue is a root of the characteristic polynomial, so plug in $\lambda = 2$:

$$8 - 16 + 2a + 30 = 0 \;\Rightarrow\; 2a = -22 \;\Rightarrow\; a = -11.$$

**Factor.** $\lambda^3 - 4\lambda^2 - 11\lambda + 30 = (\lambda - 2)(\lambda^2 - 2\lambda - 15) = (\lambda-2)(\lambda-5)(\lambda+3).$

**Eigenvalues:** $2, 5, -3$. Their absolute values are $2, 5, 3$; the largest is **5**.

**Check:** the sum of eigenvalues is $2 + 5 - 3 = 4$, the trace (coefficient of $\lambda^2$ is $-4$); the product is $-30$, matching the constant term ($-\det M = 30$).

**Code check:** with $a = -11$, the polynomial vanishes at $2$, $5$ and $-3$.

**Key idea:** substitute the known eigenvalue to find the unknown coefficient, then factor.

</details>
