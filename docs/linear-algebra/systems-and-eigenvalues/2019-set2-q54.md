# 2019 CS2 Q54: Product of the eigenvalues of a Vandermonde matrix

## Question

Consider the following matrix:

$$R = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 4 & 8 \\ 1 & 3 & 9 & 27 \\ 1 & 4 & 16 & 64 \\ 1 & 5 & 25 & 125 \end{bmatrix}$$

The absolute value of the product of Eigen values of $R$ is ________.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**12**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

The product of the eigenvalues of a square matrix equals its **determinant**. So we need $\lvert\det R\rvert$.

Row $i$ is $[1,\, x,\, x^2,\, x^3]$ for $x = 2, 3, 4, 5$. That is a **Vandermonde matrix**:

$$\det R = \prod_{i<j}(x_j - x_i) = (3-2)(4-2)(5-2)(4-3)(5-3)(5-4).$$

| Pair | 2,3 | 2,4 | 2,5 | 3,4 | 3,5 | 4,5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Difference | 1 | 2 | 3 | 1 | 2 | 1 |

Product $= 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 3 \cdot 1 \cdot 2 \cdot 1 = 12$.

**Code check:** NumPy gives $\det R = 12.0$, and the product of the computed eigenvalues has absolute value $12.000$.

**Key idea:** product of eigenvalues $=$ determinant, and a Vandermonde determinant is a product of differences.

</details>
