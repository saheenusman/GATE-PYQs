# 2024 CS1 Q12: Product of eigenvalues of a singular matrix

## Question

The product of all eigenvalues of the matrix $\begin{bmatrix} 1 & 2 & 3 \\ 4 & 5 & 6 \\ 7 & 8 & 9 \end{bmatrix}$ is

- (A) $-1$
- (B) 0
- (C) 1
- (D) 2

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B (0)**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

The product of the eigenvalues of a square matrix equals its **determinant**.

Here the rows are in arithmetic progression, so $R_3 - R_2 = R_2 - R_1 = (3,3,3)$. The rows are linearly dependent, so $\det = 0$. (Computed: $\det = 0$.)

So at least one eigenvalue is 0 (the eigenvalues are $0$ and $\tfrac{15 \pm 3\sqrt{33}}{2}$), and the product is **0**.

**Why the others fail:** $\pm 1$ and 2 would need a non-singular matrix.

**Key idea:** $\prod \lambda_i = \det A$ and $\sum \lambda_i = \operatorname{tr} A$.

</details>
