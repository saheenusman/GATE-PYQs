# 2025 CS1 Q41: Eigenvalues of the 13th power of a 2x2 matrix

## Question

Let $A$ be a $2 \times 2$ matrix as given.

$$A = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{bmatrix}$$

What are the eigenvalues of the matrix $A^{13}$?

- (A) $1, -1$
- (B) $2\sqrt2,\ -2\sqrt2$
- (C) $4\sqrt2,\ -4\sqrt2$
- (D) $64\sqrt2,\ -64\sqrt2$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Eigenvalues of $A$.**

$$\det(A - \lambda I) = (1-\lambda)(-1-\lambda) - 1 = \lambda^2 - 2 \Rightarrow \lambda = \pm\sqrt2.$$

**Eigenvalues of a power.** If $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $A$, then $\lambda^{13}$ is an eigenvalue of $A^{13}$:

$$(\pm\sqrt2)^{13} = \pm\, 2^{13/2} = \pm\, 2^6\sqrt2 = \pm\,64\sqrt2.$$

**Check using $A^2$.** $A^2 = \begin{bmatrix} 2 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \end{bmatrix} = 2I$, so $A^{13} = A\,(A^2)^6 = 2^6 A = 64A$, whose eigenvalues are $64 \times (\pm\sqrt2)$. A numerical computation gave $\pm 90.51 = \pm 64\sqrt2$.

| Option | Mistake |
|---|---|
| A | Eigenvalues of $A$ with the wrong power |
| B | $\pm 2\sqrt2 = (\pm\sqrt2)^3$ |
| C | $\pm 4\sqrt2 = (\pm\sqrt2)^5$ |

**Key idea:** eigenvalues of $A^k$ are the $k$th powers of the eigenvalues of $A$.

</details>
