# 2015 CS3 Q23: Eigenvectors of a 3 by 3 matrix for eigenvalue 1

## Question

In the given matrix

$$\begin{bmatrix} 1 & -1 & 2 \\ 0 & 1 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 & 1 \end{bmatrix}$$

one of the eigenvalues is 1. The eigenvectors corresponding to the eigenvalue 1 are

- (A) $\lbrace \alpha(4,2,1) \mid \alpha \ne 0,\ \alpha \in \mathbb{R} \rbrace$
- (B) $\lbrace \alpha(-4,2,1) \mid \alpha \ne 0,\ \alpha \in \mathbb{R} \rbrace$
- (C) $\lbrace \alpha(\sqrt2,0,1) \mid \alpha \ne 0,\ \alpha \in \mathbb{R} \rbrace$
- (D) $\lbrace \alpha(-\sqrt2,0,1) \mid \alpha \ne 0,\ \alpha \in \mathbb{R} \rbrace$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Solve $(A - I)v = 0$:

$$\begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 & 2 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \\ 1 & 2 & 0 \end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} x \\ y \\ z \end{bmatrix} = 0$$

- Row 1: $-y + 2z = 0 \Rightarrow y = 2z$.
- Row 3: $x + 2y = 0 \Rightarrow x = -2y = -4z$.

So $v = z\,(-4, 2, 1)$: option (B).

**Check:** $A(-4,2,1)^T = (-4 - 2 + 2,\ 2,\ -4 + 4 + 1)^T = (-4, 2, 1)^T$. ✓

- (A) $(4,2,1)$ fails row 3: $4 + 4 \ne 0$.
- (C) and (D) have $y = 0$ but $z = 1$, which fails row 1: $0 + 2 \ne 0$.

**Code check:** a symbolic null space of $A - I$ is spanned by $(-4, 2, 1)$.

**Key idea:** eigenvectors for $\lambda$ are the non-zero vectors in the null space of $A - \lambda I$.

</details>
