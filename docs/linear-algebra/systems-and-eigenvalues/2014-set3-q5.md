# 2014 CS3 Q5: Smallest dimension of the intersection of two subspaces

## Question

If $V_1$ and $V_2$ are 4-dimensional subspaces of a 6-dimensional vector space $V$, then the smallest possible dimension of $V_1 \cap V_2$ is ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**2**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

For subspaces, $\dim(V_1 + V_2) = \dim V_1 + \dim V_2 - \dim(V_1 \cap V_2)$.

Since $V_1 + V_2 \subseteq V$, we have $\dim(V_1 + V_2) \le 6$. So

$$\dim(V_1 \cap V_2) = 4 + 4 - \dim(V_1+V_2) \ge 8 - 6 = 2.$$

The bound is reached, e.g. $V_1 = \operatorname{span}(e_1,e_2,e_3,e_4)$ and $V_2 = \operatorname{span}(e_3,e_4,e_5,e_6)$ intersect in $\operatorname{span}(e_3,e_4)$, of dimension 2.

**Key idea:** $\dim(V_1\cap V_2) = \dim V_1 + \dim V_2 - \dim(V_1+V_2)$.

</details>
