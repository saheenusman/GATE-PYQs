# 2026 CS1 Q13: Maximum multiplicity of an eigenvalue

## Question

For $n > 1$, the maximum multiplicity of any eigenvalue of an $n \times n$ matrix with elements from $\mathbb{R}$ is

- (A) $n$
- (B) $n-1$
- (C) $1$
- (D) $n+1$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Refresher.** The eigenvalues are the roots of the characteristic polynomial $\det(A - \lambda I)$, which has degree $n$. The **algebraic multiplicity** of an eigenvalue is how many times it appears as a root.

- A degree-$n$ polynomial has $n$ roots counted with multiplicity, so no eigenvalue can repeat more than $n$ times.
- It can reach $n$: take $A = I_n$, whose characteristic polynomial is $(1-\lambda)^n$. The eigenvalue 1 has multiplicity exactly $n$.

So the maximum is $n$.

**Why the others fail:** (B) and (C) are smaller than what $I_n$ achieves. (D) $n+1$ is more than the degree allows.

**Key idea:** multiplicity of an eigenvalue is at most the degree $n$ of the characteristic polynomial; the identity matrix attains it.

</details>
