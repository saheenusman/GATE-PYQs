# 2008 CS Q28: How many matrices have eigenvalue 1

## Question

How many of the following matrices have an eigenvalue $1$?

$$\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix},\quad \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix},\quad \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix} \quad\text{and}\quad \begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$$

- (A) one
- (B) two
- (C) three
- (D) four

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Compute the eigenvalues of each (for the triangular ones they are the diagonal entries):

| Matrix | Eigenvalues | Has 1? |
|---|---|---|
| $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ | $1,\ 0$ | **yes** |
| $\begin{pmatrix} 0 & 1 \\ 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}$ | $0,\ 0$ | no |
| $\begin{pmatrix} 1 & -1 \\ 1 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ | $\lambda^2 - 2\lambda + 2 = 0$, so $1 \pm i$ | no |
| $\begin{pmatrix} -1 & 0 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ | $-1,\ -1$ | no |

Only the first matrix has the eigenvalue $1$: (A).

- (B), (C) and (D) count matrices whose eigenvalues are $0$, $1 \pm i$ or $-1$, none of which equals $1$. Note that the third matrix has diagonal entries $1$, but its eigenvalues are complex.

**Code check:** numerically computed eigenvalues: $(1, 0)$, $(0, 0)$, $(1 \pm i)$, $(-1, -1)$.

**Key idea:** eigenvalues are roots of $\det(A - \lambda I)$; a diagonal entry of $1$ is an eigenvalue only for a triangular matrix.

</details>
