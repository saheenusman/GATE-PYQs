# 2021 CS2 Q47: Maximum number of mutually orthogonal non-zero vectors in R^10

## Question

For two $n$-dimensional real vectors $P$ and $Q$, the operation $s(P,Q)$ is defined as follows:

$$s(P,Q)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\big(P[i]\cdot Q[i]\big)$$

Let $\mathcal{L}$ be a set of 10-dimensional non-zero real vectors such that for every pair of distinct vectors $P,Q\in\mathcal{L}$, $s(P,Q)=0$. What is the maximum cardinality possible for the set $\mathcal{L}$?

- (A) 9
- (B) 10
- (C) 11
- (D) 100

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

$s(P,Q)$ is the dot product, so $\mathcal L$ is a set of non-zero, pairwise orthogonal vectors in $\mathbb R^{10}$.

**Upper bound.** Non-zero orthogonal vectors are linearly independent. Suppose $\sum_i c_iv_i=0$. Taking the dot product with $v_j$ kills every term except $c_j\,(v_j\cdot v_j)$, so $c_j\|v_j\|^2=0$ and $c_j=0$.

$\mathbb R^{10}$ has dimension 10, so at most 10 vectors can be independent.

**Achieved.** The standard basis $e_1,\dots,e_{10}$ is such a set.

So the maximum is 10, (B).

- (A) 9 is not the maximum, because 10 is achievable.
- (C) 11 and (D) 100 would be more than 10 independent vectors, which is impossible.

**Key idea:** non-zero pairwise orthogonal vectors are linearly independent, so a set of them in $\mathbb R^n$ has at most $n$ elements.

</details>
