# 2012 CS Q11: Eigenvalues of the 19th power of a 2 by 2 matrix

## Question

Let $A$ be the $2 \times 2$ matrix with elements $a_{11} = a_{12} = a_{21} = +1$ and $a_{22} = -1$. Then the eigenvalues of the matrix $A^{19}$ are

- (A) $1024$ and $-1024$
- (B) $1024\sqrt{2}$ and $-1024\sqrt{2}$
- (C) $4\sqrt{2}$ and $-4\sqrt{2}$
- (D) $512\sqrt{2}$ and $-512\sqrt{2}$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

$A = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 1 \\ 1 & -1 \end{pmatrix}$ has trace $0$ and determinant $-2$, so $\lambda^2 - 2 = 0$ and the eigenvalues are $\pm\sqrt{2}$.

The eigenvalues of $A^{19}$ are the 19th powers:

$$(\pm\sqrt{2})^{19} = \pm 2^{19/2} = \pm 2^{9}\sqrt{2} = \pm 512\sqrt{2}.$$

Check by the matrix: $A^2 = 2I$, so $A^{19} = (A^2)^9 A = 512A$, whose eigenvalues are $512 \times (\pm\sqrt{2})$.

- (A), (B) use $2^{10}$ instead of $2^9$.
- (C) is a different power.

**Code check:** exact integer powering gave $A^{19} = 512A$.

**Key idea:** the eigenvalues of $A^k$ are the $k$th powers of the eigenvalues of $A$.

</details>
