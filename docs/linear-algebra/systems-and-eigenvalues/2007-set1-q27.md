# 2007 CS Q27: A subspace of R^3 and its basis

## Question

Consider the set of (column) vectors defined by $X = \lbrace x \in R^3 \mid x_1 + x_2 + x_3 = 0 \rbrace$, where $x^T = [x_1, x_2, x_3]^T$. Which of the following is **TRUE**?

- (A) $\lbrace [1,-1,0]^T, [1,0,-1]^T \rbrace$ is a basis for the subspace $X$.
- (B) $\lbrace [1,-1,0]^T, [1,0,-1]^T \rbrace$ is a linearly independent set, but it does not span $X$ and therefore is not a basis of $X$.
- (C) $X$ is not a subspace of $R^3$.
- (D) None of the above

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

$X$ is the null space of $[1\ 1\ 1]$, so it is a subspace of $R^3$ of dimension $3 - 1 = 2$.

Both given vectors are in $X$ ($1-1+0 = 0$ and $1+0-1 = 0$), and they are linearly independent (neither is a multiple of the other). Two independent vectors in a 2-dimensional space span it, so they form a basis: (A).

- (B) is false: independence plus the right count (2 vectors in a 2-D space) already gives a spanning set.
- (C) is false: $X$ is closed under addition and scalar multiplication, and contains $0$.
- (D) is false because (A) holds.

**Code check:** numpy: the matrix with these two columns has rank 2; both column sums are 0.

**Key idea:** a basis of a $d$-dimensional space is any $d$ independent vectors in it; here $d=2$.

</details>
