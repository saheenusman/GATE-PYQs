# 2024 CS2 Q47: Effects of swapping two rows

## Question

Let $A$ be an $n \times n$ matrix over the set of all real numbers $\mathbb{R}$. Let $B$ be a matrix obtained from $A$ by swapping two rows. Which of the following statements is/are TRUE?

- (A) The determinant of $B$ is the negative of the determinant of $A$
- (B) If $A$ is invertible, then $B$ is also invertible
- (C) If $A$ is symmetric, then $B$ is also symmetric
- (D) If the trace of $A$ is zero, then the trace of $B$ is also zero

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A and B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

- **(A) True.** Swapping two rows changes the sign of the determinant.
- **(B) True.** $\det B = -\det A \ne 0$ whenever $\det A \ne 0$.
- **(C) False.** $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1&2 \\ 2&3 \end{bmatrix}$ is symmetric, but swapping its rows gives $\begin{bmatrix} 2&3 \\ 1&2 \end{bmatrix}$, which is not.
- **(D) False.** $A = \begin{bmatrix} 1&2 \\ 3&-1 \end{bmatrix}$ has trace 0. Swapping rows gives $\begin{bmatrix} 3&-1 \\ 1&2 \end{bmatrix}$ with trace 5.

**Key idea:** a row swap is an elementary operation that flips the determinant's sign but moves entries off the diagonal, so symmetry and trace are not preserved.

</details>
