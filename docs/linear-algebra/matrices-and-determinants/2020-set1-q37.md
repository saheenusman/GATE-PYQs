# 2020 CS Q37: Which rank and determinant identities hold for n x n matrices

## Question

Let $A$ and $B$ be two $n \times n$ matrices over real numbers. Let $\text{rank}(M)$ and $\det(M)$ denote the rank and determinant of a matrix $M$, respectively. Consider the following statements.

- I. $\text{rank}(AB) = \text{rank}(A)\,\text{rank}(B)$
- II. $\det(AB) = \det(A)\,\det(B)$
- III. $\text{rank}(A+B) \le \text{rank}(A) + \text{rank}(B)$
- IV. $\det(A+B) \le \det(A) + \det(B)$

Which of the above statements are TRUE?

- (A) I and II only
- (B) I and IV only
- (C) II and III only
- (D) III and IV only

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

| Statement | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| I | **False** | Take $A = B = I_2$. Then $\text{rank}(AB) = 2$ but $\text{rank}(A)\,\text{rank}(B) = 4$. (A product of ranks can even exceed $n$, which a rank never does.) |
| II | **True** | The determinant is multiplicative for square matrices. |
| III | **True** | Every column of $A+B$ is a sum of a column of $A$ and a column of $B$, so the column space of $A+B$ lies inside $\text{col}(A) + \text{col}(B)$, whose dimension is at most $\text{rank}(A) + \text{rank}(B)$. |
| IV | **False** | Take $A = B = I_2$. Then $\det(A+B) = \det(2I_2) = 4$, but $\det(A) + \det(B) = 2$. |

True: II and III.

**Why the other options fail:** (A) and (B) include I, and (B) and (D) include IV.

**Key idea:** rank is subadditive and determinant is multiplicative. Neither rank nor determinant behaves well in the other direction (product of ranks, determinant of a sum).

</details>
