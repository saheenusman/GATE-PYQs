# 2026 CS1 Q20: Consequences of a vector in the null space

## Question

Let $n > 1$. Consider an $n \times n$ matrix $M$ with its elements from $\mathbb{R}$. Let the vector $(0, 1, 0, 0, \dots, 0) \in \mathbb{R}^n$ be in the null space of $M$.

Which of the following options is/are always correct?

- (A) Determinant of $M$ is 1
- (B) Determinant of $M$ is 0
- (C) Rank of $M$ is 1
- (D) There are at least two non-zero vectors in the null space of $M$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B and D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Given:** $M e_2 = 0$ for the non-zero vector $e_2 = (0,1,0,\dots,0)$.

- **(A) False, (B) True.** A square matrix with a non-zero vector in its null space is singular, so $\det M = 0$. It cannot be 1.
- **(C) False.** By rank-nullity, $\text{rank}(M) = n - \dim(\text{null space}) \le n - 1$. It could be $n-1$, $n-2$, ... or 0. Nothing forces it to be 1.
- **(D) True.** The null space is a subspace. If $e_2$ is in it, so is $2e_2$, $-e_2$, ... Every non-zero multiple is a different non-zero vector. So there are infinitely many, in particular at least two.

**Example for (C):** $M = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & 0 & 2 \\ 3 & 0 & 4 \\ 5 & 0 & 6 \end{pmatrix}$ sends $e_2$ to 0 and has rank 2, not 1.

**Key idea:** a non-zero null vector means $\det = 0$, and a subspace with one non-zero vector has infinitely many.

</details>
