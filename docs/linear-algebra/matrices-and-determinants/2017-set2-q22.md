# 2017 CS2 Q22: Rank of the sum of two 3x3 matrices

## Question

Let $P = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 1 & -1 \\ 2 & -3 & 4 \\ 3 & -2 & 3 \end{bmatrix}$ and $Q = \begin{bmatrix} -1 & -2 & -1 \\ 6 & 12 & 6 \\ 5 & 10 & 5 \end{bmatrix}$ be two matrices.

Then the rank of $P + Q$ is ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**2**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Add the matrices.**

$$P + Q = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & -1 & -2 \\ 8 & 9 & 10 \\ 8 & 8 & 8 \end{bmatrix}$$

**Row operation.** Subtract row 2 from row 3: $R_3 - R_2 = (0,\,-1,\,-2)$, which is exactly row 1. So the three rows are linearly dependent, and the rank is at most 2.

**Rows 1 and 2 are independent:** $(0,-1,-2)$ and $(8,9,10)$ are not multiples of each other.

So $\operatorname{rank}(P+Q) = 2$.

**Code check:** exact Gaussian elimination (fractions) gave rank 2 for $P+Q$. (For comparison $\operatorname{rank} P = 2$ and $\operatorname{rank} Q = 1$, so rank is not additive.)

**Key idea:** add first, then row-reduce. The rank of a sum is not the sum of the ranks.

</details>
