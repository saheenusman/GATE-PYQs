# 2022 CS Q20: Trace of a product

## Question

Consider the following two statements with respect to the matrices $A_{m \times n}$, $B_{n \times m}$, $C_{n \times n}$ and $D_{n \times n}$.

Statement 1: $\operatorname{tr}(AB) = \operatorname{tr}(BA)$

Statement 2: $\operatorname{tr}(CD) = \operatorname{tr}(DC)$

where $\operatorname{tr}(\cdot)$ represents the trace of a matrix. Which one of the following holds?

- (A) Statement 1 is correct and Statement 2 is wrong.
- (B) Statement 1 is wrong and Statement 2 is correct.
- (C) Both Statement 1 and Statement 2 are correct.
- (D) Both Statement 1 and Statement 2 are wrong.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C (both statements are correct)**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Statement 1.** $AB$ is $m \times m$ and $BA$ is $n \times n$, so both traces are defined, and

$$\operatorname{tr}(AB) = \sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n} A_{ij}B_{ji} = \sum_{j=1}^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{m} B_{ji}A_{ij} = \operatorname{tr}(BA)$$

**Statement 2** is the special case $m = n$ with $A = C$, $B = D$. It is also true, even though $CD \ne DC$ in general.

Both statements are correct: option C.

**Key idea:** the trace is cyclic, $\operatorname{tr}(XY) = \operatorname{tr}(YX)$, even for non-square factors and non-commuting matrices.

</details>
