# 2019 CS2 Q19: Invertible matrix and non-zero determinant

## Question

Let $X$ be a square matrix. Consider the following two statements on $X$.

- I. $X$ is invertible.
- II. Determinant of $X$ is non-zero.

Which one of the following is TRUE?

- (A) I implies II; II does not imply I.
- (B) II implies I; I does not imply II.
- (C) I does not imply II; II does not imply I.
- (D) I and II are equivalent statements.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

For a square matrix the two statements are equivalent.

- **I implies II.** If $X^{-1}$ exists, then $\det(X)\det(X^{-1}) = \det(I) = 1$, so $\det(X) \neq 0$.
- **II implies I.** If $\det(X) \neq 0$, then $X^{-1} = \mathrm{adj}(X)/\det(X)$ exists.

So (A), (B) and (C) each claim a missing implication that actually holds.

**Key idea:** a square matrix is invertible exactly when its determinant is non-zero.

</details>
