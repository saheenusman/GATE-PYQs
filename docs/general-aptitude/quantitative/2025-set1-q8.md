# 2025 CS1 Q8: Exactly one six in three rolls

## Question

A fair six-faced dice, with the faces labelled '1', '2', '3', '4', '5', and '6', is rolled thrice. What is the probability of rolling '6' exactly once?

- (A) $\dfrac{75}{216}$
- (B) $\dfrac{1}{6}$
- (C) $\dfrac{1}{18}$
- (D) $\dfrac{25}{216}$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

This is a binomial probability with $n = 3$ rolls and success probability $p = \frac16$:

$$P(\text{exactly one six}) = \binom{3}{1}\cdot\frac{1}{6}\cdot\left(\frac{5}{6}\right)^2 = 3 \cdot \frac{25}{216} = \frac{75}{216}.$$

The factor 3 counts *which* roll is the six: 6xx, x6x, xx6, each with probability $\frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{5}{6}\cdot\frac{5}{6} = \frac{25}{216}$.

| Option | Mistake |
|---|---|
| B, $\frac16$ | Probability for a single roll |
| C, $\frac1{18}$ | $= \frac{12}{216}$, not derived from any count here |
| D, $\frac{25}{216}$ | Forgets to multiply by 3 positions |

**Key idea:** $P = \binom{n}{k}p^k(1-p)^{n-k}$.

</details>
