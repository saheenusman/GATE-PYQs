# 2017 CS2 Q64: Number of roots of e^x + 0.5x^2 - 2 = 0 in [-5, 5]

## Question

The number of roots of $e^{x} + 0.5x^{2} - 2 = 0$ in the range $[-5, 5]$ is

- (A) 0
- (B) 1
- (C) 2
- (D) 3

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Let $f(x) = e^x + 0.5x^2 - 2$.

**Shape of $f$.** $f'(x) = e^x + x$. This is strictly increasing ($f'' = e^x + 1 > 0$), with $f'(-1) = e^{-1} - 1 < 0$ and $f'(0) = 1 > 0$, so $f'$ has exactly one zero, near $x_0 \approx -0.567$. Thus $f$ **decreases** up to $x_0$ and **increases** after it, with a single minimum there.

**Values.**

- At the minimum, $f(x_0) \approx -1.27 < 0$ (also $f(0) = -1$).
- At the ends, $f(-5) = e^{-5} + 12.5 - 2 \approx 10.5 > 0$ and $f(5) = e^{5} + 12.5 - 2 \approx 158.9 > 0$.

So $f$ goes from positive, down through zero to the negative minimum, then back up through zero to positive: it crosses zero exactly **twice**, once on each side of $x_0$.

Answer: **(C)**.

**Code check:** scanning $[-5,5]$ with step $10^{-4}$ found exactly two sign changes, near $x \approx -1.926$ and $x \approx 0.599$, and $f'(x_0)\approx 0$ with $f(x_0) \approx -1.27$.

**Key idea:** one minimum below zero and positive ends give exactly two roots.

</details>
