# 2024 CS1 Q2: Ratio x/y when x+y is proportional to x−y

## Question

If two distinct non-zero real variables $x$ and $y$ are such that $(x+y)$ is proportional to $(x-y)$, then the value of $\dfrac{x}{y}$ ________.

- (A) depends on $xy$
- (B) depends only on $x$ and not on $y$
- (C) depends only on $y$ and not on $x$
- (D) is a constant

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D (is a constant)**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Write the proportionality.** For some constant $k$ (with $k \ne 1$, since $x \ne 0$ and $y \ne 0$):

$$x + y = k(x - y)$$

**Solve for the ratio.**

$$x(1-k) = -y(1+k) \;\Rightarrow\; \frac{x}{y} = \frac{k+1}{k-1}$$

The right side involves only the constant $k$. So $x/y$ is a **constant**, however $x$ and $y$ themselves change.

**Why the others fail:** the ratio does not depend on $xy$, on $x$ alone, or on $y$ alone.

**Key idea:** "proportional to" means "equal to a fixed constant times". Divide through to get a ratio of constants.

</details>
