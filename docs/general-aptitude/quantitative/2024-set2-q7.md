# 2024 CS2 Q7: Two items sold at the same price with 10% profit and loss

## Question

A person sold two different items at the same price. He made 10% profit in one item, and 10% loss in the other item. In selling these two items, the person made a total of

- (A) 1% profit
- (B) 2% profit
- (C) 1% loss
- (D) 2% loss

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C (1% loss)**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Let each item sell for $p$.

- Item with 10% profit: cost $= p/1.1$.
- Item with 10% loss: cost $= p/0.9$.

Total cost $= p\left(\frac{1}{1.1} + \frac{1}{0.9}\right) = p \times 2.0202$. Total revenue $= 2p$.

$$\text{Loss} = \frac{2.0202 - 2}{2.0202} \approx 1\%.$$

Equivalent short form: equal-price sales with $x\%$ gain and $x\%$ loss always lose $x^2/100\% = 1\%$.

**Why the others fail:** the 10% on cost is not the same base for both items, so the gain and loss do not cancel.

**Key idea:** percentages apply to the cost price, which differs for the two items.

</details>
