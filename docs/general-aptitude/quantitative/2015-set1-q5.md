# 2015 CS1 Q5: Probability that two chosen numbers sum to 16

## Question

Given Set $A = \lbrace 2, 3, 4, 5 \rbrace$ and Set $B = \lbrace 11, 12, 13, 14, 15 \rbrace$, two numbers are randomly selected, one from each set. What is the probability that the sum of the two numbers equals 16?

- (A) 0.20
- (B) 0.25
- (C) 0.30
- (D) 0.33

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

There are $4 \times 5 = 20$ equally likely pairs.

Pairs with sum 16: $(2,14),\ (3,13),\ (4,12),\ (5,11)$. That is 4 pairs.

$$P = \frac{4}{20} = 0.20$$

Option (A). The others (0.25, 0.30, 0.33) would need 5, 6 or about 7 favourable pairs, and no such pairs exist.

**Key idea:** count favourable pairs over the product of the set sizes.

</details>
