# 2010 CS Q65: Four-digit numbers above 3000 from a given set of digits

## Question

Given digits $2, 2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 4, 4$, how many distinct $4$-digit numbers greater than $3000$ can be formed?

- (A) $50$
- (B) $51$
- (C) $52$
- (D) $54$

*Printed as is: the supplied PDF lists eight digits, $2,2,3,3,4,4,4,4$. Other published copies of this paper list nine digits, $2,2,3,3,3,4,4,4,4$ (three 3s). I could not check the original paper itself.*

<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Status: disputed.** The official key is (B) $51$. With the digits **as printed in the supplied PDF** I get $44$, which is not an option. With a third $3$ I get $51$, which is the key.

A number above $3000$ starts with $3$ or $4$. Count the distinct three-digit tails left over, by leading digit.

**As printed ($2,2,3,3,4,4,4,4$).**

- Leading $3$: tail from $\lbrace 2,2,3,4,4,4,4 \rbrace$ gives $19$.
- Leading $4$: tail from $\lbrace 2,2,3,3,4,4,4 \rbrace$ gives $25$.
- Total $19 + 25 = 44$, which matches none of the options.

**With three 3s ($2,2,3,3,3,4,4,4,4$).**

- Leading $3$: tail from $\lbrace 2,2,3,3,4,4,4,4 \rbrace$ gives $25$.
- Leading $4$: tail from $\lbrace 2,2,3,3,3,4,4,4 \rbrace$ gives $26$.
- Total $25 + 26 = 51$: (B), the key.

Each tail count adds the arrangements for every allowed mix of digits; for example, a tail with all three digits different has $3! = 6$ arrangements, a tail with two equal digits has $3$, and a tail with all equal digits has $1$.

Since the question as printed has no matching option and the key matches the three-3s reading, the printed digit list looks like a transcription error. I record the key, (B), and flag the mismatch.

- (A) $50$, (C) $52$ and (D) $54$ do not come out of either reading.

**Code check:** a Python enumeration of all distinct ordered selections of $4$ digits (no leading zero, value above $3000$) gave $44$ for the printed digits and $51$ for the three-3s digits.

**Key idea:** split by the leading digit and count the distinct arrangements of the remaining digits, taking care with repeated digits.
