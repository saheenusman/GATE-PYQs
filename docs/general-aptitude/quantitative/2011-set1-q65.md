# 2011 CS Q65: Spirit left after repeated replacement with water

## Question

A container originally contains 10 litres of pure spirit. From this container 1 litre of spirit is replaced with 1 litre of water. Subsequently, 1 litre of the mixture is again replaced with 1 litre of water and this process is repeated one more time. How much spirit is now left in the container?

- (A) 7.58 litres
- (B) 7.84 litres
- (C) 7 litres
- (D) 7.29 litres

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Each replacement removes $\tfrac1{10}$ of whatever is in the container, so $\tfrac{9}{10}$ of the spirit remains after each step.

The text describes three replacements: the first, "subsequently ... again", and "repeated one more time".

$$10 \times \left(\frac{9}{10}\right)^3 = 10 \times 0.729 = 7.29 \text{ litres}$$

So (D).

- Two replacements would leave $10 \times 0.81 = 8.1$ litres, which is not an option, so the three-step reading is the one that matches the choices.

**Code check:** $10 \times 0.9^3 = 7.29$ and $10 \times 0.9^2 = 8.1$.

**Key idea:** each replacement keeps 9/10 of the spirit, so the spirit left is 10 x (9/10)^k.

</details>
