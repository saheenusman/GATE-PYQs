# 2021 CS2 Q8: Total number of students from changed ratios

## Question

The number of students in three classes is in the ratio 3:13:6. If 18 students are added to each class, the ratio changes to 15:35:21.

The total number of students in all the three classes in the beginning was:

- (A) 22
- (B) 66
- (C) 88
- (D) 110

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Let the classes have $3k,\ 13k,\ 6k$ students.

After adding 18 to each: $\dfrac{3k+18}{13k+18}=\dfrac{15}{35}=\dfrac37$.

$7(3k+18)=3(13k+18)\Rightarrow 21k+126=39k+54\Rightarrow 18k=72\Rightarrow k=4$.

Initial sizes: 12, 52, 24. Check after adding 18: 30, 70, 42, which is $15:35:21$ (all multiplied by 2). Total at the start $=12+52+24=88$.

(C). The option 22 is $22k$ with $k=1$, and 66 and 110 are other multiples of 22 ($k=3,5$) that do not fit.

**Key idea:** write the classes as $3k, 13k, 6k$ and match one of the new ratios.

</details>
