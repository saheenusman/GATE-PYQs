# 2017 CS1 Q64: Ways to give four people shirts they do not dislike

## Question

Arun, Gulab, Neel and Shweta must choose one shirt each from a pile of four shirts coloured red, pink, blue and white respectively. Arun dislikes the colour red and Shweta dislikes the colour white. Gulab and Neel like all the colours. In how many different ways can they choose the shirts so that no one has a shirt with a colour he or she dislikes?

- (A) 21
- (B) 18
- (C) 16
- (D) 14

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Count by inclusion-exclusion.** Each person gets a different shirt, so there are $4! = 24$ assignments in all.

- Arun gets red (a bad outcome): the other three share the 3 remaining shirts, $3! = 6$ ways.
- Shweta gets white (bad): $3! = 6$ ways.
- Both bad (Arun red and Shweta white): the other two share 2 shirts, $2! = 2$ ways.

$$\text{Good} = 24 - 6 - 6 + 2 = 14$$

So **(D)**.

**Check by direct count.** Arun can take pink, blue or white. Count Shweta's options for each:

| Arun takes | Shweta can take | Gulab and Neel | Ways |
|---|---|---|---|
| pink | red, blue (not white, not pink) | 2 ways | $2 \times 2 = 4$ |
| blue | red, pink | 2 ways | $2 \times 2 = 4$ |
| white | red, pink, blue | 2 ways | $3 \times 2 = 6$ |

$4 + 4 + 6 = 14$.

**Code check:** enumerating all 24 assignments and keeping those with Arun not red and Shweta not white gave 14.

**Key idea:** count the total, subtract the arrangements that break a condition, and add back the ones subtracted twice.

</details>
