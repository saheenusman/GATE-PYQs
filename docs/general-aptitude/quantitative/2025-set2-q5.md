# 2025 CS2 Q5: Which statement about two primes holds always

## Question

Let $p_1$ and $p_2$ denote two arbitrary prime numbers. Which one of the following statements is correct for all values of $p_1$ and $p_2$?

- (A) $p_1 + p_2$ is not a prime number.
- (B) $p_1 p_2$ is not a prime number.
- (C) $p_1 + p_2 + 1$ is a prime number.
- (D) $p_1 p_2 + 1$ is a prime number.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**(B) is always true.** A product of two primes has at least the divisors $1, p_1, p_2, p_1p_2$ (with $p_1$ or $p_2$ a proper divisor), so it is composite.

Counterexamples for the others:

| Option | $p_1, p_2$ | Value | Prime? |
|---|---|---|---|
| A: "sum is not prime" | 2, 3 | 5 | **is** prime, so A fails |
| C: "sum + 1 is prime" | 3, 5 | 9 | not prime, so C fails |
| D: "product + 1 is prime" | 3, 5 | 16 | not prime, so D fails |

**Key idea:** to refute "for all", one counterexample is enough. To prove it, use the definition of prime.

</details>
