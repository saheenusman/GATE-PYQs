# 2017 CS1 Q59: Smallest y such that y times 162 is a perfect cube

## Question

Find the smallest number $y$ such that $y \times 162$ is a perfect cube.

- (A) 24
- (B) 27
- (C) 32
- (D) 36

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Factor.** $162 = 2 \times 81 = 2^{1} \times 3^{4}$.

**Make every exponent a multiple of 3.**

- $2^{1}$ needs $2^{2}$ more to reach $2^{3}$.
- $3^{4}$ needs $3^{2}$ more to reach $3^{6}$.

$$y = 2^{2} \times 3^{2} = 36$$

Check: $36 \times 162 = 5832 = 18^{3}$. So **(D)**.

**Why the others fail.** (A) $24 = 2^3\cdot3$ gives $2^4 3^5$; (B) $27 = 3^3$ gives $2\cdot3^7$; (C) $32 = 2^5$ gives $2^6\cdot3^4$. None has all exponents divisible by 3.

**Code check:** the smallest $y$ with $162y$ a perfect cube, found by search, is $36$ ($5832 = 18^3$).

**Key idea:** a number is a perfect cube when every prime exponent is a multiple of 3.

</details>
