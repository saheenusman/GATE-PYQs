# 2020 CS Q8: Unpainted area in an annulus filled with touching circles

## Question

The figure below shows an annular ring with outer and inner radii as $b$ and $a$, respectively. The annular space has been painted in the form of blue colour circles touching the outer and inner periphery of annular space. If maximum $n$ number of circles can be painted, then the unpainted area available in annular space is ______.

![An annular ring with outer radius b and inner radius a. Blue circles sit in the ring one after another, each touching both the outer and the inner boundary and touching its neighbours. A dotted line of small circles indicates that the pattern continues around the ring.](../../assets/2020/set1-q8.png)

- (A) $\pi\left[(b^2-a^2) - \frac{n}{4}(b-a)^2\right]$
- (B) $\pi\left[(b^2-a^2) - n(b-a)^2\right]$
- (C) $\pi\left[(b^2-a^2) + \frac{n}{4}(b-a)^2\right]$
- (D) $\pi\left[(b^2-a^2) + n(b-a)^2\right]$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Size of each circle.** A circle touches both the inner and the outer boundary, so its diameter is the width of the ring:

$$2r = b - a \quad\Rightarrow\quad r = \frac{b-a}{2}.$$

**Area of one circle:**

$$\pi r^2 = \frac{\pi (b-a)^2}{4}.$$

**Unpainted area** = area of the ring minus the area of the $n$ circles:

$$\pi(b^2-a^2) - n\cdot\frac{\pi(b-a)^2}{4} = \pi\left[(b^2-a^2) - \frac{n}{4}(b-a)^2\right].$$

**Why the others fail:**

- **(B)** subtracts $n(b-a)^2$, which treats each circle as having radius $b-a$ instead of $(b-a)/2$.
- **(C) and (D)** add the circle area. The unpainted area must be smaller than the ring.

**Key idea:** the circle's diameter equals the ring width $b-a$. Then area is $\pi r^2$ with $r = (b-a)/2$.

</details>
