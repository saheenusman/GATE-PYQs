# 2021 CS2 Q3: Angle between the longest diagonal of a cube and an edge

## Question

If $\theta$ is the angle, in degrees, between the longest diagonal of the cube and any one of the edges of the cube, then $\cos\theta=$

- (A) $\dfrac{1}{2}$
- (B) $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$
- (C) $\dfrac{1}{\sqrt2}$
- (D) $\dfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Take a cube of side 1 with a corner at the origin. The longest (space) diagonal is $\vec d=(1,1,1)$ and an edge at that corner is $\vec e=(1,0,0)$.

$$\cos\theta=\frac{\vec d\cdot\vec e}{|\vec d||\vec e|}=\frac{1}{\sqrt3\cdot1}=\frac1{\sqrt3}.$$

This is the same for every edge, by symmetry. (B).

- (A) $\tfrac12$ would be $60^\circ$.
- (C) $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}$ is the angle between an edge and a face diagonal ($45^\circ$).
- (D) $\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}$ is $30^\circ$.

**Key idea:** use the dot product with the diagonal $(1,1,1)$ and an edge $(1,0,0)$.

</details>
