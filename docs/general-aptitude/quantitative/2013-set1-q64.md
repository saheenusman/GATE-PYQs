# 2013 CS Q64: Average speed over three modes of travel

## Question

A tourist covers half of his journey by train at 60 km/h, half of the remainder by bus at 30 km/h and the rest by cycle at 10 km/h. The average speed of the tourist in km/h during his entire journey is

- (A) 36
- (B) 30
- (C) 24
- (D) 18

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Let the total distance be 1 unit.

| Mode | Distance | Speed | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| Train | $1/2$ | 60 | $1/120$ |
| Bus | $1/4$ | 30 | $1/120$ |
| Cycle | $1/4$ | 10 | $3/120$ |
| Total | 1 | | $5/120 = 1/24$ |

$$\text{Average speed} = \frac{\text{total distance}}{\text{total time}} = \frac{1}{1/24} = 24 \text{ km/h}$$

**Why the others fail:** None of 36, 30 or 18 equals total distance over total time. The typical trap is averaging the three speeds, which gives $(60+30+10)/3 \approx 33.3$.

**Code check:** the computation gave 24.

**Key idea:** average speed is total distance over total time, never the mean of the speeds.

</details>
