# 2018 CS Q3: Area of the circle on a square's diagonal

## Question

The area of a square is $d$. What is the area of the circle which has the diagonal of the square as its diameter?

- (A) $\pi d$
- (B) $\pi d^2$
- (C) $\frac{1}{4}\pi d^2$
- (D) $\frac{1}{2}\pi d$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Step 1: side and diagonal.** Side $= \sqrt{d}$. Diagonal $= \sqrt{2}\cdot\sqrt{d}$, so its square is $2d$.

**Step 2: circle.** Diameter $=\sqrt{2d}$, so radius squared is $r^2 = \dfrac{2d}{4} = \dfrac{d}{2}$.

$$\text{Area} = \pi r^2 = \frac{\pi d}{2}$$

**Why the others fail:**
- **A** would need $r^2 = d$, which is a circle of radius equal to the square's side.
- **B** and **C** contain $d^2$. An area must scale like $d$ (the square's area), not like $d^2$.

**Key idea:** the diagonal of a square is $\sqrt{2}$ times its side, so the circle's area is half the square's area times $\pi$.

</details>
