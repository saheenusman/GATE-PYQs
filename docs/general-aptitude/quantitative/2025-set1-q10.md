# 2025 CS1 Q10: Three scoops from four flavours

## Question

A shop has 4 distinct flavors of ice-cream. One can purchase any number of scoops of any flavor. The order in which the scoops are purchased is inconsequential. If one wants to purchase 3 scoops of ice-cream, in how many ways can one make that purchase?

- (A) 4
- (B) 20
- (C) 24
- (D) 48

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Order does not matter and repeats are allowed, so we count **multisets** of size 3 from 4 flavours. By stars and bars, with $n = 4$ types and $r = 3$ scoops:

$$\binom{n + r - 1}{r} = \binom{6}{3} = 20.$$

By cases: all three the same (4 ways), two of one and one of another ($4 \times 3 = 12$ ways), all different ($\binom43 = 4$ ways). Total $4 + 12 + 4 = 20$.

| Option | Mistake |
|---|---|
| A, 4 | Counts only "all the same flavour" |
| C, 24 | $4 \times 3 \times 2$: ordered, all different |
| D, 48 | Not obtained by any valid count |

Enumerating all multisets by computer also gives 20.

**Key idea:** choosing $r$ items with repetition from $n$ types gives $\binom{n+r-1}{r}$.

</details>
