# 2024 CS2 Q3: Students liking core and other branches

## Question

In an engineering college of 10,000 students, 1,500 like neither their core branches nor other branches. The number of students who like their core branches is $1/4$th of the number of students who like other branches. The number of students who like both their core and other branches is 500.

The number of students who like their core branches is

- (A) 1,800
- (B) 3,500
- (C) 1,600
- (D) 1,500

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A (1,800)**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Let $n$ = students who like other branches. Then core = $n/4$.

**Students liking at least one** $= 10000 - 1500 = 8500$.

**Inclusion-exclusion:**

$$\frac{n}{4} + n - 500 = 8500 \Rightarrow \frac{5n}{4} = 9000 \Rightarrow n = 7200.$$

Core $= 7200/4 = 1800$.

**Why the others fail:** 1,600 and 1,500 come from forgetting to add back the 500 in both. 3,500 does not satisfy the $1/4$ ratio.

**Key idea:** $|A \cup B| = |A| + |B| - |A \cap B|$.

</details>
