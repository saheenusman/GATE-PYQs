# 2017 CS2 Q61: Which word reveals the author's belief that ideology matters less than literature

## Question

"We lived in a culture that denied any merit to literary works, considering them important only when they were handmaidens to something seemingly more urgent – namely ideology. This was a country where all gestures, even the most private, were interpreted in political terms."

The author's belief that ideology is not as important as literature is revealed by the word:

- (A) 'culture'
- (B) 'seemingly'
- (C) 'urgent'
- (D) 'political'

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**What the author is doing.** The author describes a culture that valued literature only as a servant ("handmaiden") of ideology, which it thought *more urgent*. The author does not share that view.

**Which word shows the author's own stance?**

- (A) *culture* names the society being described, with no opinion.
- (B) *seemingly* means "apparently, but perhaps not really". By saying ideology was only **seemingly** more urgent, the author signals doubt: it is how the culture saw it, not how the author does. This reveals the belief that ideology is *not* more important.
- (C) *urgent* belongs to the culture's own claim; used straight, it would endorse it.
- (D) *political* is a plain description of how gestures were read.

Answer: **(B)**.

**Status note:** reading-comprehension question (no program applies); the answer matches the official key.

**Key idea:** hedging words like *seemingly* mark the author's distance from a claim.

</details>
