# 2024 CS2 Q1: Analogy of increasing intensity (bothered, ..., daunted)

## Question

If '→' denotes increasing order of intensity, then the meaning of the words [walk → jog → sprint] is analogous to [bothered → ________ → daunted].

Which one of the given options is appropriate to fill the blank?

- (A) phased
- (B) phrased
- (C) fazed
- (D) fused

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C (fazed)**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

The three words must rise in intensity of being disturbed: *bothered* (mildly), **fazed** (disconcerted), *daunted* (seriously discouraged or intimidated).

**Why the others fail:** *phased* means done in stages, *phrased* means worded, and *fused* means joined or melted. None of them is a level of being disturbed. *Fazed* and *phased* sound alike, which is the trap.

**Key idea:** pick the word that fits the same theme at the middle intensity.

</details>
