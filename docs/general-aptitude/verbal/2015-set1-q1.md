# 2015 CS1 Q1: Choosing the right quantifier in a negative question

## Question

Didn't you buy ______ paper when you went shopping?

- (A) any paper
- (B) much paper
- (C) no paper
- (D) a few paper

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

The sentence is a negative question, so the blank needs a word that works with negation.

- (A) *any paper*: "any" is the standard quantifier in negatives and questions. Correct.
- (B) *much paper*: grammatical, but it asks about the quantity bought, whereas the sentence asks whether *any* paper was bought.
- (C) *no paper*: a double negative with "Didn't".
- (D) *a few paper*: "a few" needs a plural countable noun, and "paper" is uncountable here.

**Key idea:** "any" is used with uncountable nouns in negative questions.

</details>
