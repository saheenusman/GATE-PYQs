# 2014 CS1 Q61: Inference from the Palghat Gap passage

## Question

(GA Set-7, Q6) The Palghat Gap (or Palakkad Gap), a region about 30 km wide in the southern part of the Western Ghats in India, is lower than the hilly terrain to its north and south. The exact reasons for the formation of this gap are not clear. It results in the neighbouring regions of Tamil Nadu getting more rainfall from the South West monsoon and the neighbouring regions of Kerala having higher summer temperatures.

What can be inferred from this passage?

- (A) The Palghat gap is caused by high rainfall and high temperatures in southern Tamil Nadu and Kerala
- (B) The regions in Tamil Nadu and Kerala that are near the Palghat Gap are low-lying
- (C) The low terrain of the Palghat Gap has a significant impact on weather patterns in neighbouring parts of Tamil Nadu and Kerala
- (D) Higher summer temperatures result in higher rainfall near the Palghat Gap area

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

The passage says the gap *results in* more rain in Tamil Nadu and hotter summers in Kerala. So the low terrain affects the weather.

- (A) reverses cause and effect, and the passage says the causes of the gap are not clear.
- (B) the gap is lower than the terrain to its north and south, but nothing says the neighbouring regions are low-lying.
- (D) rain and heat are two separate effects of the gap; the passage does not link them.

**Key idea:** an inference must follow from the text and keep cause and effect the right way round.

</details>
