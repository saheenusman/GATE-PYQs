# 2020 CS Q4: What can be inferred from the melting-glaciers passage

## Question

The dawn of the 21st century witnessed the melting glaciers oscillating between giving too much and too little to billions of people who depend on them for fresh water. The UN climate report estimates that without deep cuts to man-made emissions, at least 30% of the northern hemisphere's surface permafrost could melt by the end of the century. Given this situation of imminent global exodus of billions of people displaced by rising seas, nation-states need to rethink their carbon footprint for political concerns, if not for environmental ones.

Which one of the following statements can be inferred from the given passage?

- (A) Nation-states do not have environmental concerns.
- (B) Nation-states are responsible for providing fresh water to billions of people.
- (C) Billions of people are responsible for man-made emissions.
- (D) Billions of people are affected by melting glaciers.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

An inference must follow directly from what the passage says. Nothing outside it may be added.

- **(D) is stated.** The passage says billions of people depend on glaciers for fresh water, and that the melting swings between "too much and too little". So billions are affected.
- **(A) is not supported.** The passage says states should rethink their carbon footprint "for political concerns, *if not* for environmental ones". That suggests environmental concern is not enough, not that it is absent.
- **(B) is not supported.** The passage never says who must provide fresh water.
- **(C) is not supported.** It links emissions to the cuts needed, but never puts the blame on those billions of people.

**Key idea:** pick the option the text states or directly implies, and reject options that need an extra assumption.

</details>
