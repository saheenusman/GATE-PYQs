# 2024 CS1 Q6: Idioms with 'head'

## Question

In the given text, the blanks are numbered (i)–(iv). Select the best match for all the blanks.

Steve was advised to keep his head **(i)** before heading **(ii)** to bat; for, while he had a head **(iii)** batting, he could only do so with a cool head **(iv)** his shoulders.

- (A) (i) down (ii) down (iii) on (iv) for
- (B) (i) on (ii) down (iii) for (iv) on
- (C) (i) down (ii) out (iii) for (iv) on
- (D) (i) on (ii) out (iii) on (iv) for

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Read each blank as a fixed phrase:

- **keep his head down**: stay focused and avoid distraction. (i) = *down*.
- **heading out to bat**: going out onto the field. (ii) = *out*.
- **a head for batting**: a natural aptitude for it. (iii) = *for*.
- **a cool head on his shoulders**: a calm, sensible person. (iv) = *on*.

Full sentence: *keep his head down before heading out to bat; for, while he had a head for batting, he could only do so with a cool head on his shoulders.*

**Why the others fail:** each uses at least one of "head on batting", "head for his shoulders" or "heading down to bat", none of which is an idiom.

**Key idea:** these blanks test fixed idioms. Test each option by reading the phrase aloud.

</details>
