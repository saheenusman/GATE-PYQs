# 2016 CS2 Q62: Valid inferences from a passage on computers and the internet

## Question

(GA Set-6, Q7) Computers were invented for performing only high-end useful computations. However, it is no understatement that they have taken over our world today. The internet, for example, is ubiquitous. Many believe that the internet itself is an unintended consequence of the original invention. With the advent of mobile computing on our phones, a whole new dimension is now enabled. One is left wondering if all these developments are good or, more importantly, required.

Which of the statement(s) below is/are logically valid and can be inferred from the above paragraph?

- (i) The author believes that computers are not good for us.
- (ii) Mobile computers and the internet are both intended inventions

- (A) (i) only
- (B) (ii) only
- (C) both (i) and (ii)
- (D) neither (i) nor (ii)

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

- **(i) cannot be inferred.** The author only "is left wondering if all these developments are good". Wondering is not believing they are bad.
- **(ii) cannot be inferred.** The passage says many believe the internet is an *unintended* consequence, which contradicts "intended" for the internet; nothing says mobile computing was intended either.

Neither follows: **(D)**.

**Key idea:** the author wonders but does not conclude; the internet is called unintended.

</details>
