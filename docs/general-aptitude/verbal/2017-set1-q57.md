# 2017 CS1 Q57: Besides versus beside in a sentence about why people work

## Question

Research in the workplace reveals that people work for many reasons ______.

- (A) money beside
- (B) beside money
- (C) money besides
- (D) besides money

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Meaning needed.** The sentence says people work for many reasons *in addition to* money. The word for "in addition to / apart from" is **besides**. It is a preposition, so it comes before its object: **besides money**.

**Why the others fail.**

- (B) *beside* means "next to" (as in "sit beside me"), not "in addition to".
- (A) and (C) put *money* first, which leaves the preposition without an object after it and does not read as a sentence.

So **(D)**.

**Status note:** no program applies; my reading matches the official key.

**Key idea:** *besides* = in addition to; *beside* = next to. A preposition is followed by its object.

</details>
