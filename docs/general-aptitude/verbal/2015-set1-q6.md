# 2015 CS1 Q6: Meaning of the idiom 'took to their heels'

## Question

Select the alternative meaning of the underlined part of the sentence.

The chain snatchers <u>took to their heels</u> when the police party arrived.

- (A) took shelter in a thick jungle
- (B) open indiscriminate fire
- (C) took to flight
- (D) unconditionally surrendered

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

*To take to one's heels* means to run away.

- (C) *took to flight* is the same idea. Correct.
- (A) hiding in a jungle is not stated.
- (B) firing is the opposite of fleeing.
- (D) surrendering is also the opposite of running away.

**Key idea:** "took to their heels" is an idiom meaning "fled".

</details>
