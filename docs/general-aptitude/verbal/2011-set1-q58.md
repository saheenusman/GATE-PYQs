# 2011 CS Q58: Verb form after contemplated

## Question

Choose the most appropriate word(s) from the options given below to complete the following sentence.

**I contemplated ______ Singapore for my vacation but decided against it.**

- (A) to visit
- (B) having to visit
- (C) visiting
- (D) for a visit

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

The verb *contemplate* takes a gerund (an *-ing* form) as its object: "contemplated visiting".

- (A) "contemplated to visit" is not idiomatic: *contemplate* is not followed by an infinitive.
- (B) "having to visit" means being obliged to visit, which changes the meaning.
- (D) "contemplated for a visit" is ungrammatical.

**Key idea:** contemplate is followed by an -ing form, not by an infinitive.

</details>
