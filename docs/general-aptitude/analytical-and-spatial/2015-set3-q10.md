# 2015 CS3 Q10: Valid distribution of portfolios among six members

## Question

The head of a newly formed government desires to appoint five of the six selected members P, Q, R, S, T, and U to portfolios of Home, Power, Defense, Telecom, and Finance. U does not want any portfolio if S gets one of the five. R wants either Home or Finance or no portfolio. Q says that if S gets either Power or Telecom, then she must get the other one. T insists on a portfolio if P gets one.

Which is the valid distribution of portfolios?

- (A) P-Home, Q-Power, R-Defense, S-Telecom, T-Finance
- (B) R-Home, S-Power, P-Defense, Q-Telecom, T-Finance
- (C) P-Home, Q-Power, T-Defense, S-Telecom, U-Finance
- (D) Q-Home, U-Power, T-Defense, R-Telecom, P-Finance

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Test each option against the four conditions.

| Option | Problem |
|---|---|
| A | R gets Defense, but R wants only Home, Finance, or nothing. ✗ |
| **B** | R: Home ✓. S: Power, so Q must get Telecom ✓. P is in, so T is in ✓. S is in, so U is out ✓. **Valid** |
| C | S is in and U is also in (Finance). ✗ |
| D | R gets Telecom. ✗ |

Option (B).

**Code check:** a brute force over all assignments of five of the six people to the five portfolios, with these four rules, confirms that only (B) among the options is valid.

**Key idea:** eliminate options one rule at a time; one violation is enough.

</details>
