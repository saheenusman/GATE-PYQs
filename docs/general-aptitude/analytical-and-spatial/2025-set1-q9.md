# 2025 CS1 Q9: Unfolding a paper folded twice and cut

## Question

A square paper, shown in figure (I), is folded along the dotted lines as shown in the figures (II) and (III). Then a few cuts are made as shown in figure (IV). Which one of the following patterns will be obtained when the paper is unfolded?

Note: The figures shown are representative.

![Four steps. (I) a plain square. (II) the square with a dotted diagonal from top left to bottom right and a fold arrow. (III) the resulting right triangle, with a second dotted line from the top right corner to the middle of the long side and a second fold arrow. (IV) the final small triangle with a flat top edge and a point at the bottom. It has a small black triangle cut at the middle of the top edge, a black right-angled triangle cut on its left slanted edge, and a small black bar inside near the right slanted edge.](../../assets/2025/set1-q9-a.png)

The four options, from top to bottom:

![Options A, B, C and D. Each is a square with cuts. A: black triangles pointing outward at the middle of all four sides, black squares at the top left and bottom right corners, and L-shaped marks at the top right and bottom left corners. B: the same but with diamonds instead of squares. C: the squares and L-shapes as in A, but the top triangle points inward and the bottom one points inward. D: a single black square at the centre, triangles at the four sides and L-shapes at the top right and bottom left.](../../assets/2025/set1-q9-b.png)

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**What the folds do.** The paper is folded along **both diagonals** (first the top-left to bottom-right one, then the other one through the centre). The result in (IV) is one quarter of the square: a triangle whose flat top edge is a **side of the square** and whose bottom point is the **centre**. Unfolding reflects the quarter across both diagonals, so the final pattern is **mirror-symmetric about both diagonals**.

**Where each cut sits in the quarter (figure IV):**

| Cut in (IV) | Position | What it becomes after unfolding |
|---|---|---|
| Small triangle, point up, at the middle of the top edge | On a side of the square | A triangle at the **middle of each of the four sides**, all pointing the same way relative to the edge (toward the edge) |
| Right-angled triangle on the left slanted edge | On a diagonal fold, halfway to the centre | Reflected across that diagonal it becomes an **axis-aligned square**, centred on that diagonal, once near each of **two opposite corners** |
| Small bar inside, next to the right slanted edge | Next to the other diagonal | Its mirror images join into an **L shape** near each of the **other two corners** |

**Eliminating options.**

| Option | Problem |
|---|---|
| B | Has **diamonds** (squares turned 45°) at the corners. A cut on a diagonal fold gives an axis-aligned square, not a diamond |
| C | The side triangles at top and bottom point **inward** while those at left and right point outward. Mirror symmetry about the diagonals would turn each side triangle into the next one, so all four must point the same way |
| D | Puts a single square at the **centre**. The cut is halfway along a diagonal, not at the centre, so squares must appear near the corners |
| **A** | Four outward triangles, squares at one pair of opposite corners, and L-shapes at the other pair: it is exactly the pattern above |

This is a pencil-and-paper symmetry argument. The cut positions are read from the printed figure, which is marked "representative".

**Key idea:** two diagonal folds make the final pattern symmetric about both diagonals, so reflect each cut across them and compare features.

</details>
