# 2022 CS Q9: Corners and mid-points of a triangle

## Question

The corners and mid-points of the sides of a triangle are named using the distinct letters P, Q, R, S, T and U, but not necessarily in the same order. Consider the following statements:

- The line joining P and R is parallel to the line joining Q and S.
- P is placed on the side opposite to the corner T.
- S and U cannot be placed on the same side.

Which one of the following statements is correct based on the above information?

- (A) P cannot be placed at a corner
- (B) S cannot be placed at a corner
- (C) U cannot be placed at a mid-point
- (D) R cannot be placed at a corner

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B (S cannot be placed at a corner)**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Setup.** Call the corners $A, B, C$ and the mid-points $a$ (of $BC$), $b$ (of $CA$), $c$ (of $AB$). A "side" holds a corner pair and the mid-point between them, so three points per side.

**Which lines can be parallel?** Lines through two of the six points are sides, mid-lines (two mid-points) and medians (a corner and the opposite mid-point). The only distinct parallel pairs are a side and the mid-line parallel to it, for example $BC \parallel bc$.

**Case I: $\lbrace Q, S \rbrace$ is the mid-line.** Then $S$ is a mid-point.

**Case II: $\lbrace P, R \rbrace$ is the mid-line and $\lbrace Q, S \rbrace$ lies on the parallel side.** Take that side as $BC$, so $P, R = b, c$. Then $Q, S$ are two of $B, a, C$, and $T, U$ are $A$ and the leftover point $x$ of $B, a, C$.
- If $U = x$, then $S$ and $U$ share side $BC$. Not allowed. So $U = A$ and $T = x$.
- $T$ must be a corner, so $x$ is $B$ or $C$. Then $\lbrace Q, S \rbrace = \lbrace a,\ \text{the other corner} \rbrace$.
- The other corner shares a side with $A = U$, so $S$ cannot be that corner. Hence $S = a$, a mid-point.

So $S$ is a mid-point in every valid placement. (B) is correct.

**The other options are not forced.** One valid placement: $P = A,\ Q = c,\ R = C,\ S = a,\ T = B,\ U = b$.
- $PR$ is side $AC$ and $QS$ is the mid-line $ca$, which is parallel to $AC$. ✓
- $P = A$ lies on $CA$, the side opposite $T = B$. ✓
- $S = a$ is on $BC$ and $U = b$ is on $CA$, so they do not share a side. ✓

Here $P$ is at a corner, $U$ is at a mid-point and $R$ is at a corner. That rules out (A), (C) and (D).

**Code check:** a brute force over all $720$ labelings found exactly $30$ that satisfy the three conditions. $S$ is a mid-point in all of them, while $P$, $U$ and $R$ each take both corner and mid-point positions.

**Key idea:** list the geometric facts (parallel pairs, sides) first, then test each option against them.

</details>
