# 2021 CS1 Q9: Valid conclusions from two All-statements

## Question

Given below are two statements 1 and 2, and two conclusions I and II.

Statement 1: All bacteria are microorganisms.

Statement 2: All pathogens are microorganisms.

Conclusion I: Some pathogens are bacteria.

Conclusion II: All pathogens are not bacteria.

Based on the above statements and conclusions, which one of the following options is logically CORRECT?

- (A) Only conclusion I is correct
- (B) Only conclusion II is correct
- (C) Either conclusion I or II is correct.
- (D) Neither conclusion I nor II is correct.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C or D (the official key accepts both)**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Two sets (bacteria, pathogens) sit inside "microorganisms". Nothing forces them to overlap or to be disjoint, so neither conclusion is forced. (A) and (B) are out.

The remaining choice depends on how "All pathogens are not bacteria" is read.

- **Reading 1: "No pathogen is bacteria".** I and II are then contradictory: exactly one of them is true. Neither is forced, but one of them must hold, so **(C)** "either I or II" is correct.
- **Reading 2: "Not all pathogens are bacteria"** (some pathogen is not a bacterium). I and II can both be true (partial overlap), and II is not forced either. Then neither is forced, so **(D)**.

The wording is ambiguous, and the official key accepts **both C and D**. This is not a case where my answer disagrees with the key: I get C under reading 1 and D under reading 2, and both are on the key.

**Key idea:** "All X are not Y" can mean "no X is Y" or "not all X are Y". Settle which before testing conclusions.

</details>
