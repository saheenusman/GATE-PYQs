# 2017 CS2 Q65: Which region has the fastest-changing air pressure on a contour plot

## Question

An air pressure contour line joins locations in a region having the same atmospheric pressure. The following is an air pressure contour plot of a geographical region. Contour lines are shown at 0.05 bar intervals in this plot.

![Air pressure contour plot of a rectangular region, with contour lines labelled 0.65, 0.7, 0.75, 0.8, 0.9 and 0.95. Four red circles mark the places P, Q, R and S. R is at the top left, where the 0.65 and 0.7 lines run close together and several lines pass through the circle. P is at the left inside the 0.9 line, with a closed 0.95 line beside it. Q is at the bottom centre, between the 0.8 and 0.75 lines, where the lines are far apart. S is at the right inside the 0.9 line, with a small 0.95 line. A scale bar shows 0, 1 and 2 km.](../../assets/2017/set2-q65.png)

If the possibility of a thunderstorm is given by how fast air pressure rises or drops over a region, which of the following regions is most likely to have a thunderstorm?

- (A) P
- (B) Q
- (C) R
- (D) S

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**How to read a contour plot.** Neighbouring lines differ by a fixed 0.05 bar. So where lines are **closest together**, the pressure changes over the shortest distance: the pressure gradient is steepest. The question defines the thunderstorm risk by how fast pressure rises or drops, so we want the place with the densest contour lines.

**Compare the four regions** (visual reading of the figure):

| Region | What the lines look like |
|---|---|
| P | sits near a pressure peak (the 0.95 loop), where the surface is flat; only a few, well-spaced lines |
| Q | between the 0.8 and 0.75 lines, which are far apart: a gentle slope |
| S | near the 0.95 loop on the right; only two lines (0.9 and 0.95) cross the area, so the change is modest |
| R | at the top left, where the 0.65 and 0.7 lines (and others) are packed tightly together, several lines inside the circle |

Pressure changes fastest at **R**: **(C)**.

**Status note:** this is a figure-reading question, so no program applies. The choice rests on comparing line spacing in the image; it matches the official key.

**Key idea:** closely spaced contour lines mean a steep gradient, i.e. rapid change.

</details>
