# 2021 CS2 Q10: Height ordering of six students

## Question

Six students P, Q, R, S, T and U, with distinct heights, compare their heights and make the following observations.

Observation I: S is taller than R.

Observation II: Q is the shortest of all.

Observation III: U is taller than only one student.

Observation IV: T is taller than S but is not the tallest.

The number of students that are taller than R is the same as the number of students shorter than ______.

- (A) T
- (B) R
- (C) S
- (D) P

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

- II: Q is the shortest.
- III: U is taller than exactly one student, which must be Q. So U is second shortest.
- I and IV: $R<S<T$, and T is not the tallest, so P is the tallest.
- The four remaining students above U are R, S, T, P. With $R<S<T$ and P on top, the order is fixed.

Shortest to tallest: **Q < U < R < S < T < P**.

Students taller than R: S, T, P, which is 3. Students shorter than S: Q, U, R, which is also 3. So the blank is **S**, option (C).

Checked by brute force over all $6!$ orderings: only one satisfies all four observations.

**Key idea:** place the extremes first (shortest, second shortest, tallest), then fit the chain R < S < T.

</details>
