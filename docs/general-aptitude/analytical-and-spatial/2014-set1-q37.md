# 2014 CS1 Q37: Coins from five bags: product of labels with 11 gm coins

## Question

There are 5 bags labeled 1 to 5. All the coins in a given bag have the same weight. Some bags have coins of weight 10 gm, others have coins of weight 11 gm. I pick 1, 2, 4, 8, 16 coins respectively from bags 1 to 5. Their total weight comes out to 323 gm. Then the product of the labels of the bags having 11 gm coins is ___.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**12**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

If all 31 coins were 10 gm, the total would be $31 \times 10 = 310$ gm. The extra weight is $323 - 310 = 13$ gm, and each 11 gm coin adds 1 gm.

So the number of coins from the 11 gm bags is 13. The counts are $1, 2, 4, 8, 16$ (powers of two), and $13 = 1 + 4 + 8$ in binary is unique. So the 11 gm bags are the ones giving 1, 4 and 8 coins: bags **1, 3 and 4**.

$$1 \times 3 \times 4 = 12$$

**Code check:** trying all $2^5$ weight assignments gives one solution, bags 1, 3, 4.

**Classification flag:** this is a reasoning puzzle in the CS paper. It is filed under `analytical-and-spatial` (it could also be `combinatorics`).

**Key idea:** powers-of-two counts make the weight excess a binary code for the bags.

</details>
