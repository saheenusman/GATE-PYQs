# 2017 CS1 Q62: Number of women among six people at a circular table

## Question

Six people are seated around a circular table. There are at least two men and two women. There are at least three right-handed persons. Every woman has a left-handed person to her immediate right. None of the women are right-handed. The number of women at the table is

- (A) 2
- (B) 3
- (C) 4
- (D) Cannot be determined

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Facts.** Women $\ge 2$, men $\ge 2$, and no woman is right-handed, so **every woman is left-handed**. The $\ge 3$ right-handed people therefore must all be men, so there are **at least 3 men**. With 6 people in all, women $\le 3$. So there are 2 or 3 women.

**3 women (and 3 men) is impossible.** Then all 3 men are right-handed, so the left-handed people are exactly the 3 women. Each woman's immediate right neighbour must be left-handed, so it must be a woman. Following "the person on the right" from a woman would then reach only women, all around the circle. That makes all six women, contradicting the presence of men.

**2 women (and 4 men) works.** Going around: W1, W2, M1 (left-handed), M2, M3, M4 (right-handed).

- W1's right neighbour is W2, who is left-handed.
- W2's right neighbour is M1, who is left-handed.
- There are 3 right-handed persons (M2, M3, M4).

So the number of women is **2**: **(A)**.

**Why the others fail.** (B) is shown impossible above. (C) 4 women would leave 2 men, but at least 3 right-handed men are needed. (D) The cases leave only one value.

**Code check:** enumerating all $4^6$ assignments of (gender, handedness) to the six seats and applying the five conditions left only configurations with exactly 2 women.

**Key idea:** use the constraints to count (right-handed people must be men), then test the boundary cases.

</details>
