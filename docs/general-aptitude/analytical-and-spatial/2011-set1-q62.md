# 2011 CS Q62: Which microbe to target with a vaccine first

## Question

P, Q, R and S are four types of dangerous microbes recently found in a human habitat. The area of each circle with its diameter printed in brackets represents the growth of a single microbe surviving human immunity system within 24 hours of entering the body. The danger to human beings varies proportionately with the toxicity, potency and growth attributed to a microbe shown in the figure below:

![A bubble chart. The horizontal axis is potency (probability that the microbe will overcome the human immunity system), from 0.2 to 1. The vertical axis is toxicity (milligrams of microbe required to destroy half the body mass in kilograms), from 0 to 1000. Circle P (50 mm) is centred near potency 0.4 and toxicity 800. Circle Q (40 mm) is near potency 0.5 and toxicity 600. Circle R (30 mm) is near potency 0.4 and toxicity 300. Circle S (20 mm) is near potency 0.8 and toxicity 200.](../../assets/2011/set1-q62.png)

A pharmaceutical company is contemplating the development of a vaccine against the most dangerous microbe. Which microbe should the company target in its first attempt?

- (A) P
- (B) Q
- (C) R
- (D) S

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Reading the axes.** Toxicity is the number of milligrams needed to destroy half the body mass, so a **smaller** number means a **more** toxic microbe. Growth is the circle's area, which is proportional to the square of its diameter.

So the danger grows with potency, growth ($\propto d^2$), and $1 / \text{toxicity value}$. Taking centres read by eye from the chart:

| Microbe | Potency | Toxicity (mg) | Diameter | Danger index $= \dfrac{\text{potency} \times d^2}{\text{mg}}$ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P | $0.4$ | $800$ | $50$ | $0.4 \times 2500 / 800 = 1.25$ |
| Q | $0.5$ | $600$ | $40$ | $0.5 \times 1600 / 600 \approx 1.33$ |
| R | $0.4$ | $300$ | $30$ | $0.4 \times 900 / 300 = 1.20$ |
| S | $0.8$ | $200$ | $20$ | $0.8 \times 400 / 200 = 1.60$ |

S is the most dangerous: it has by far the highest potency and needs the least mass to be lethal, which outweighs its small growth: (D).

**Reading note.** The question does not say how the three factors combine, so the index above is one reasonable model, and the centre values are read by eye from the printed chart. S leads Q by about $20\%$ here, but the result depends on S's toxicity being near $200$ mg. This agrees with the key.

**Key idea:** danger rises with potency and growth, and falls as the lethal-dose value rises; compare the factors together.

</details>
