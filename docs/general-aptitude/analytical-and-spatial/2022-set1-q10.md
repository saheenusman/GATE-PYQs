# 2022 CS Q10: Dividing a plot into four similar plots

## Question

A plot of land must be divided between four families. They want their individual plots to be similar in shape, not necessarily equal in area. The land has equally spaced poles, marked as dots in the below figure. Two ropes, R1 and R2, are already present and cannot be moved.

![A 7 by 7 grid of poles. The plot is the square with two corners cut off, at the top right and the bottom left. Rope R2 runs vertically down two units from the top edge at the fifth column. Rope R1 runs horizontally two units in from the left edge at the fifth row.](../../assets/2022/set1-q10.png)

What is the least number of **additional** straight ropes needed to create the desired plots? A single rope can pass through three poles that are aligned in a straight line.

- (A) 2
- (B) 4
- (C) 5
- (D) 3

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D (3)**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Place the ropes.** Number the poles by (column, row) from the top-left, with $(0,0)$ at the top-left pole. The boundary corners are $(0,0), (5,0), (6,1), (6,6), (1,6), (0,5)$. R1 joins $(0,4)$ to $(2,4)$, and R2 joins $(4,0)$ to $(4,2)$.

**Three extra ropes work:**
1. $(2,4)$ to $(2,6)$: from the end of R1 straight down to the bottom edge.
2. $(2,4)$ to $(4,2)$: the diagonal joining the end of R1 to the end of R2.
3. $(4,2)$ to $(6,2)$: from the end of R2 across to the right edge.

The four plots are pentagons. Two have area 14 and two have area 3.5, which is the same shape at half the scale, so all four are similar.

**Why fewer ropes do not work.** This was checked by computer search, not by a hand proof. Candidate ropes are all straight segments through three consecutive collinear poles, in the four grid directions (horizontal, vertical and both diagonals), lying inside the plot. Together with R1 and R2, no choice of 0, 1 or 2 extra ropes gives four plots of the same shape. With 3 extra ropes there is exactly one solution, the one above.

**Key idea:** the new ropes must cut the land into four regions of the same shape, so look for cuts that continue R1 and R2 to the boundary or join their ends.

**Note:** the search assumes ropes of length 2 grid units. That is how the question defines a rope ("passes through three poles"). The figure was read from the PDF.

</details>
