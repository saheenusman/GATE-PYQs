# 2026 CS2 Q5: What follows from 'When it is raining, peacocks dance'

## Question

'When it is raining, peacocks dance.'

Based only on this sentence, which one of the following options is necessarily true?

- (A) Peacocks dance only when it is raining.
- (B) When peacocks dance, it is raining.
- (C) When peacocks are not dancing, it is not raining.
- (D) When it is not raining, peacocks do not dance.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Let $R$ = "it is raining" and $D$ = "peacocks dance". The sentence is $R \Rightarrow D$.

- **(C)** "Not $D$ implies not $R$" is the contrapositive. It is always equivalent. **True.**
- **(A)** "Only when raining" means $D \Rightarrow R$. That is the converse. Not given.
- **(B)** Also $D \Rightarrow R$. Not given.
- **(D)** "Not $R \Rightarrow$ not $D$" is the inverse. Not given.

**Key idea:** an implication is equivalent only to its contrapositive. The converse and inverse can fail.

</details>
