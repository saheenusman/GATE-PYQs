# 2013 CS Q46: Decoders needed for a 16K x 16 RAM

## Question

A RAM chip has a capacity of 1024 words of 8 bits each ($1K \times 8$). The number of $2 \times 4$ decoders with enable line needed to construct a $16K \times 16$ RAM from $1K \times 8$ RAM is

- (A) 4
- (B) 5
- (C) 6
- (D) 7

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Chips.** $16K\times16$ needs $\frac{16K}{1K}\times\frac{16}{8} = 16 \times 2 = 32$ chips. Each row of 2 chips gives the 16-bit word, and there are 16 such rows.

**Address bits.** $16K = 2^{14}$ addresses. The low 10 bits go to every chip; the top 4 bits must select one of 16 rows, which needs a $4\times16$ decoder.

**Building $4\times16$ from $2\times4$ decoders with enable:**

- 1 decoder takes the top 2 bits and produces 4 enables.
- Each of its 4 outputs enables one second-level $2\times4$ decoder (fed by the other 2 bits), giving $4\times4 = 16$ row selects.

Total: $1 + 4 = 5$ decoders.

**Why the others fail:** (A) four second-level decoders give 16 outputs, but nothing would choose which one is enabled, so a first-level decoder is needed. (C) and (D) add decoders that are not needed.

**Code check:** a count script gave 32 chips and 5 decoders.

**Note:** this is also a memory-organisation question, so `cache-and-memory` is listed as an additional topic.

**Key idea:** a 4-to-16 decoder = 1 + 4 two-to-four decoders in a tree.

</details>
