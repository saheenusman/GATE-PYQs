# 2021 CS1 Q38: Next states of a 3-bit counter with T flip-flops

## Question

Consider a 3-bit counter, designed using T flip-flops, as shown below:

![Three T flip-flops TP, TQ, TR in a row with outputs P, Q, R and complements P', Q', R'. One clock pulse line feeds the clock input of all three. The T input of TP is connected to R. The T input of TQ is connected to P'. The T input of TR is connected to Q'.](../../assets/2021/set1-q38.png)

Assuming the initial state of the counter given by PQR as 000, what are the next three states?

- (A) 011, 101, 000
- (B) 001, 010, 111
- (C) 011, 101, 111
- (D) 001, 010, 000

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Wiring (read from the figure).** All three flip-flops share the clock, so the counter is synchronous. The inputs are $T_P=R$, $T_Q=P'$, $T_R=Q'$. A T flip-flop toggles when $T=1$ and holds when $T=0$.

| State PQR | $T_P=R$ | $T_Q=\overline P$ | $T_R=\overline Q$ | Next PQR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 000 | 0 | 1 | 1 | 011 |
| 011 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 101 |
| 101 | 1 | 0 | 1 | 000 |

The next three states are **011, 101, 000**, so (A).

(B) and (D) start with 001, which would need only $R$ to toggle. (C) ends in 111, but from 101 the toggles are $P$ and $R$, giving 000.

Simulated in Python: `['011', '101', '000']`.

**Key idea:** next state $=$ current state XOR the T inputs.

</details>
