# 2021 CS2 Q62: Literals in the minimal SOP of a partly specified function

## Question

Consider a Boolean function $f(w,x,y,z)$ such that

$$f(w,0,0,z)=1$$
$$f(1,x,1,z)=x+z$$
$$f(w,1,y,z)=wz+y$$

The number of literals in the minimal sum-of-products expression of $f$ is ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**6**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Build the truth table (minterm index $=8w+4x+2y+z$).

- $x=0,y=0$: $f=1$, giving minterms 0, 1, 8, 9.
- $w=1,y=1$: $f=x+z$, giving minterms 11, 14, 15 (minterm 10 is 0).
- $x=1$: $f=wz+y$, giving minterms 6, 7, 13, 14, 15.

The three rules agree wherever they overlap. The only unspecified cells are $w=0,x=0,y=1$ (minterms 2 and 3), which act as don't-cares.

$$f=\Sigma m(0,1,6,7,8,9,11,13,14,15)$$

Minimal cover with three prime implicants:

- $\bar x\bar y$ covers 0, 1, 8, 9.
- $xy$ covers 6, 7, 14, 15.
- $wz$ covers 9, 11, 13, 15.

$$f=\bar x\bar y+xy+wz$$

That is $2+2+2=6$ literals.

I confirmed with a brute-force search over all implicant covers: the minimum is 6 whether the two unspecified cells are treated as don't-cares or as 0 (treating them as 1 would give 8).

**Key idea:** reduce the piecewise definition to a truth table, then find the smallest cover with K-map style grouping.

</details>
