# 2026 CS1 Q48: Minimal sum-of-products from a K-map

## Question

Consider a Boolean function $F$ with the following minterm expression:

$$F(P,Q,R,S) = \sum m(1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 10, 12, 13, 14)$$

Which of the following options is/are the minimal sum-of-products expression(s) of $F$?

- (A) $\bar{P}S + Q\bar{R} + \bar{P}\bar{Q}R + \bar{Q}R\bar{S}$
- (B) $\bar{P}S + Q\bar{R} + \bar{P}\bar{Q}R + PR\bar{S}$
- (C) $\bar{P}S + Q\bar{R} + PQ\bar{S} + PR\bar{S}$
- (D) $\bar{P}S + Q\bar{R} + PQ\bar{S} + \bar{Q}R\bar{S}$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B and D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**K-map** (rows $PQ$, columns $RS$, in Gray order):

| $PQ \backslash RS$ | 00 | 01 | 11 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **00** | 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| **01** | 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |
| **11** | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| **10** | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 |

**Prime implicants** (all groups that cannot grow):

| Group | Minterms | Term |
|---|---|---|
| Essential | 1, 3, 5, 7 | $\bar{P}S$ |
| Essential | 4, 5, 12, 13 | $Q\bar{R}$ |
| | 2, 3 | $\bar{P}\bar{Q}R$ |
| | 2, 10 | $\bar{Q}R\bar{S}$ |
| | 12, 14 | $PQ\bar{S}$ |
| | 10, 14 | $PR\bar{S}$ |

Minterms 2, 10 and 14 are not covered by the two essentials. Two more terms are needed to cover them:

- Cover 2 with $\bar{P}\bar{Q}R$ or $\bar{Q}R\bar{S}$.
- Cover 14 with $PQ\bar{S}$ or $PR\bar{S}$.
- Cover 10 by one of $\bar{Q}R\bar{S}$ or $PR\bar{S}$.

Minimum is 4 terms, and the valid 4-term covers are:

1. $\bar{P}S + Q\bar{R} + \bar{Q}R\bar{S} + PR\bar{S}$ (not in the options)
2. $\bar{P}S + Q\bar{R} + \bar{Q}R\bar{S} + PQ\bar{S}$ **= (D)**
3. $\bar{P}S + Q\bar{R} + \bar{P}\bar{Q}R + PR\bar{S}$ **= (B)**

All have 10 literals. I confirmed by script that these are the only minimum covers, and that (A) and (C) do **not** equal $F$:

- (A) leaves minterm 14 uncovered.
- (C) leaves minterm 2 uncovered.

**Key idea:** take the essential prime implicants first, then pick the cheapest cover for what is left. More than one minimal cover can exist.

</details>
