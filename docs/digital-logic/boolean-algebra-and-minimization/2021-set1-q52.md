# 2021 CS1 Q52: Expressions equivalent to the complement of a product of sums

## Question

Consider the following Boolean expression.

$$F=(X+Y+Z)(\overline X+Y)(\overline Y+Z)$$

Which of the following Boolean expressions is/are equivalent to $\overline F$ (complement of $F$)?

- (A) $(\overline X+\overline Y+\overline Z)(X+\overline Y)(Y+\overline Z)$
- (B) $X\overline Y+\overline Z$
- (C) $(X+\overline Z)(\overline Y+\overline Z)$
- (D) $X\overline Y+Y\overline Z+\overline X\,\overline Y\,\overline Z$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B, C and D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Apply De Morgan to get $\overline F$ as a sum of products:

$$\overline F=\overline X\,\overline Y\,\overline Z+X\overline Y+Y\overline Z$$

Its minterms (XYZ) are 000, 010, 100, 101 and 110, so $\overline F=1$ exactly on these five inputs.

- (A) Not equal. It is $F$ with every literal complemented, not $\overline F$. At $XYZ=010$ it gives $(1)(0)(\cdot)=0$, but $\overline F=1$.
- (B) $X\overline Y+\overline Z$: $X\overline Y$ covers 100 and 101, and $\overline Z$ covers 000, 010, 100, 110. Together these are the five minterms. **Equivalent.**
- (C) $(X+\overline Z)(\overline Y+\overline Z)=\overline Z+X\overline Y$ (distributive law). Same as (B). **Equivalent.**
- (D) This is exactly the De Morgan form above. **Equivalent.**

All four options were evaluated on all 8 inputs: B, C and D match $\overline F$ everywhere, and A does not.

Answer: **B, C and D**.

**Key idea:** $\overline{A\cdot B\cdot C}=\overline A+\overline B+\overline C$. Complementing a product of sums gives a sum of products of the complemented terms.

</details>
