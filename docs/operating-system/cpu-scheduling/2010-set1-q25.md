# 2010 CS Q25: Starvation and response time in common scheduling policies

## Question

Which of the following statements are true?

- I. Shortest remaining time first scheduling may cause starvation
- II. Preemptive scheduling may cause starvation
- III. Round robin is better than FCFS in terms of response time

- (A) I only
- (B) I and III only
- (C) II and III only
- (D) I, II and III

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

- **I is true.** In shortest remaining time first, a long job keeps losing the CPU as shorter jobs arrive, so it can starve.
- **II is true.** Preemptive priority scheduling lets a steady stream of higher-priority processes keep a low-priority process waiting indefinitely. SRTF is itself an example of a preemptive policy with starvation.
- **III is true.** Round robin gives each process a turn within one cycle of the queue, so the first response comes soon. Under FCFS, a short job can wait behind a long one, so average response time is worse.

All three hold: (D).

- (A), (B) and (C) each leave out one of the true statements.

**Key idea:** preemptive policies that favour short or high-priority jobs can starve others; round robin trades throughput for quick first responses.

</details>
