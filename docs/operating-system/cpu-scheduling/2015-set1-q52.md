# 2015 CS1 Q52: Completion time of a task under rate-monotonic scheduling

## Question

Consider a uniprocessor system executing three tasks $T_1$, $T_2$ and $T_3$, each of which is composed of an infinite sequence of jobs (or instances) which arrive periodically at intervals of 3, 7 and 20 milliseconds, respectively. The priority of each task is the inverse of its period, and the available tasks are scheduled in order of priority, with the highest priority task scheduled first. Each instance of $T_1$, $T_2$ and $T_3$ requires an execution time of 1, 2 and 4 milliseconds, respectively. Given that all tasks initially arrive at the beginning of the $1^{\text{st}}$ millisecond and task preemptions are allowed, the first instance of $T_3$ completes its execution at the end of ______ milliseconds.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**12**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Shortest period means highest priority: $T_1 > T_2 > T_3$. New jobs arrive at $T_1$: $0, 3, 6, 9, \dots$ and $T_2$: $0, 7, 14, \dots$. Preemption is allowed.

| Time | Running | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 0 - 1 | $T_1$ | highest priority; finishes (1 ms) |
| 1 - 3 | $T_2$ | runs its 2 ms |
| 3 - 4 | $T_1$ | new $T_1$ job at 3 |
| 4 - 6 | $T_3$ | gets 2 of its 4 ms |
| 6 - 7 | $T_1$ | new $T_1$ job at 6 preempts $T_3$ |
| 7 - 9 | $T_2$ | new $T_2$ job at 7 outranks $T_3$ |
| 9 - 10 | $T_1$ | new $T_1$ job at 9 |
| 10 - 12 | $T_3$ | the last 2 ms; **done at 12** |

**Code check:** a millisecond-by-millisecond simulator of this fixed-priority schedule also finished the first $T_3$ job at 12.

**Key idea:** rate-monotonic scheduling: shorter period means higher priority, and a higher-priority arrival preempts.

</details>
