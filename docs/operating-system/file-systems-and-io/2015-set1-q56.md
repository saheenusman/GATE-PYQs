# 2015 CS1 Q56: Extra head movement of SSTF compared to SCAN

## Question

Suppose the following disk request sequence (track numbers) for a disk with 100 tracks is given: 45, 20, 90, 10, 50, 60, 80, 25, 70. Assume that the initial position of the R/W head is on track 50. The additional distance that will be traversed by the R/W head when the Shortest Seek Time First (SSTF) algorithm is used compared to the SCAN (Elevator) algorithm (assuming that SCAN algorithm moves towards 100 when it starts execution) is ______ tracks.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**10**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**SSTF** (always go to the nearest pending request), starting at 50:

| Move | Distance |
|---|---|
| 50 to 50 (already there) | 0 |
| 50 to 45 | 5 |
| 45 to 60 | 15 |
| 60 to 70 | 10 |
| 70 to 80 | 10 |
| 80 to 90 | 10 |
| 90 to 25 | 65 |
| 25 to 20 | 5 |
| 20 to 10 | 10 |

SSTF total $= 130$.

**SCAN** (towards 100 first), servicing 60, 70, 80, 90 on the way up and then reversing to take 45, 25, 20, 10:

$$(90 - 50) + (90 - 10) = 40 + 80 = 120$$

Difference: $130 - 120 = 10$.

**Assumption to note.** This takes the head to turn around at the **last request** (90). If SCAN is taken to run to the physical end of the disk (track 99 or 100) before reversing, SCAN costs $138$ or $140$ and the difference would be negative. The question gives no such detail, and the official key $10$ is the turn-at-last-request reading.

**Code check:** a simulator gave SSTF $= 130$, SCAN (turn at 90) $= 120$.

**Key idea:** SSTF is greedy by distance; SCAN sweeps in one direction and then back.

</details>
