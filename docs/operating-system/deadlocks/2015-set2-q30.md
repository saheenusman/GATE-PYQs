# 2015 CS2 Q30: Values of N that could lead to a deadlock with 6 identical resources

## Question

A system has 6 identical resources and $N$ processes competing for them. Each process can request at most 2 resources. Which one of the following values of $N$ could lead to a deadlock?

- (A) 1
- (B) 2
- (C) 3
- (D) 4

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Status: disputed.** The official key is (D), $N = 4$. I could not derive it: by the standard analysis no option can lead to a deadlock. The reasoning follows.

**Deadlock-free condition.** A deadlock needs all resources held while every process is still waiting for more. A process holding its maximum (2) is not waiting and will finish and release, so a deadlock needs every process to hold **fewer than 2**, i.e. at most 1 each, with all 6 resources in use. That needs $N \ge 6$.

Equivalently, with maximum demand $m_i = 2$ and $R = 6$ resources, the system is deadlock-free when

$$R \ge \sum_i (m_i - 1) + 1 = N + 1 \;\Longleftrightarrow\; N \le 5.$$

**Test the options.**

- $N = 1, 2, 3$: even in the worst split, some process can always get its second resource.
- $N = 4$: to use all 6 resources, at least two processes hold 2 each. Those two are done and release, so there is no deadlock.
- $N = 6$ is the smallest value that can deadlock (each holds 1, none free).

**Code check:** a brute-force enumeration of allocations for $N = 1,\dots,7$ found a deadlock state only for $N \ge 6$.

**Conclusion.** I record the official key (D), but the options as printed ($1, 2, 3, 4$) contain no value that can deadlock. I read the option "4" clearly in the paper. The question may have been misprinted (a value of 6 would make (D) correct), but I cannot confirm that from the PDF. That is why this is marked `disputed`.

**Key idea:** with $R$ resources and a maximum of 2 per process, deadlock needs $N \ge R$.

</details>
