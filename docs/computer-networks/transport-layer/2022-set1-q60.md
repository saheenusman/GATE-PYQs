# 2022 CS Q60: Minimum TCP sequence number bits

## Question

Consider the data transfer using TCP over a 1 Gbps link. Assuming that the maximum segment lifetime (MSL) is set to 60 seconds, the minimum number of bits required for the sequence number field of the TCP header, to prevent the sequence number space from wrapping around during the MSL is ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**33**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**TCP numbers bytes.** In one MSL the sender can send

$$\frac{10^9 \text{ bits/s}}{8} \times 60 \text{ s} = 7.5 \times 10^9 \text{ bytes}$$

The sequence space must be at least this large:

$$2^{32} \approx 4.29 \times 10^9 < 7.5 \times 10^9 \le 2^{33} \approx 8.59 \times 10^9$$

So at least **33** bits are needed.

**Why not 32:** a 32-bit space (as in real TCP) would wrap around in about 34 seconds at 1 Gbps, which is why TCP needs extensions such as timestamps for fast links.

**Key idea:** the sequence space must exceed the bytes transmitted within one segment lifetime.

</details>
