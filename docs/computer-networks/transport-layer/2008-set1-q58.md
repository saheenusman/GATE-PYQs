# 2008 CS Q58: Maximum transmission time with a token bucket

## Question

A computer on a 10 Mbps network is regulated by a token bucket. The token bucket is filled at a rate of 2 Mbps. It is initially filled to capacity with 16 Megabits. What is the maximum duration for which the computer can transmit at the full 10 Mbps?

- (A) 1.6 seconds
- (B) 2 seconds
- (C) 5 seconds
- (D) 8 seconds

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

While transmitting at the full rate $M = 10$ Mbps for $t$ seconds, the computer sends $10t$ megabits. Tokens available in that time: the bucket's $16$ Mb plus the $2t$ Mb that arrive meanwhile. Transmission can continue while

$$10\,t \le 16 + 2\,t \;\Longrightarrow\; 8\,t \le 16 \;\Longrightarrow\; t \le 2 \text{ s}.$$

This is the standard burst formula $S = \dfrac{C}{M - R} = \dfrac{16}{10 - 2} = 2$ seconds: (B).

- (A) 1.6 s is $16/10$, which ignores the tokens that keep arriving.
- (D) 8 s is $16/2$, which divides the bucket by the token rate alone; (C) 5 s does not correspond to the formula.

**Code check:** the calculation gives $2.0$ seconds.

**Key idea:** the burst length is the bucket size divided by the difference between the sending rate and the token rate.

</details>
