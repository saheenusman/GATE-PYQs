# 2012 CS Q45: TCP congestion window at the end of the tenth transmission

## Question

Consider an instance of TCP's Additive Increase Multiplicative Decrease (AIMD) algorithm where the window size at the start of the slow start phase is $2$ MSS and the threshold at the start of the first transmission is $8$ MSS. Assume that a timeout occurs during the fifth transmission. Find the congestion window size at the end of the tenth transmission.

- (A) 8 MSS
- (B) 14 MSS
- (C) 7 MSS
- (D) 12 MSS

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Status: disputed.** The official key is MTA. Under the standard textbook model I get a single answer, (C), so I do not just accept "no answer". Both follow.

**My derivation** (window used in each transmission):

| Transmission | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Window (MSS) | 2 | 4 | 8 | 9 | 10 |

Slow start doubles until the threshold of $8$, then adds $1$ per round. A timeout during transmission 5 sets the threshold to half the window, $10/2 = 5$, and restarts slow start from $1$:

| Transmission | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Window (MSS) | 1 | 2 | 4 | 5 | 6 |

(From $4$ slow start would double to $8$, but it stops at the threshold $5$.) After the ACKs of the tenth transmission the window grows once more, to **7 MSS**. That is (C).

If the threshold is taken as half of $9$ instead of $10$, it is $4$, and the windows and the final value are the same.

**Why the key may say MTA.** The wording leaves some conventions open: whether "at the end of the tenth transmission" includes the growth after its ACKs (7) or means the window used in it (6), and which window the timeout halves. The key gives no reason, so I record MTA and flag my answer, (C).

**Code check:** a round-by-round simulation of both thresholds (5 and 4) gave windows $1, 2, 4, 5, 6$ for transmissions 6 to 10 and $7$ after the tenth round's ACKs.

**Key idea:** a timeout sets the threshold to half the current window and the window to 1; then slow start up to the threshold, then +1 per round.
