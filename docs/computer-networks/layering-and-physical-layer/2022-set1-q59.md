# 2022 CS Q59: Time to receive a packet over a satellite link

## Question

Consider a 100 Mbps link between an earth station (sender) and a satellite (receiver) at an altitude of 2100 km. The signal propagates at a speed of $3 \times 10^8$ m/s. The time taken (in milliseconds, rounded off to two decimal places) for the receiver to completely receive a packet of 1000 bytes transmitted by the sender is ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**7.08 (accepted range 7.07 to 7.09)**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Total time = transmission time + propagation time.**

- Transmission: $\dfrac{1000 \times 8 \text{ bits}}{100 \times 10^6 \text{ bits/s}} = 80\ \mu s = 0.08$ ms.
- Propagation: $\dfrac{2100 \times 10^3 \text{ m}}{3 \times 10^8 \text{ m/s}} = 7$ ms.

$$t = 0.08 + 7 = 7.08 \text{ ms}$$

The receiver has the whole packet when the last bit arrives: the last bit leaves after 0.08 ms and then travels for 7 ms.

**Key idea:** delay to receive a packet completely is transmission time (to push all bits out) plus propagation time (for the last bit to travel).

</details>
