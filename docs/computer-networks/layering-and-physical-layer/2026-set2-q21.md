# 2026 CS2 Q21: File transfer time over links of different bandwidths

## Question

A file of 4 million bytes is transferred between two hosts over a path of three consecutive links with bandwidths 2 Mbps, 500 kbps and 1 Mbps. All processing and propagation delays are negligible. There is no other traffic and no extra overhead.

What is the total time (in seconds) to transfer the file? (1M $= 10^6$, 1k $= 10^3$)

- (A) 731
- (B) 64
- (C) 8
- (D) 16

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Idea.** With negligible delays, the data flows through the path like water through pipes. The speed is set by the **slowest link** (the bottleneck): 500 kbps.

File size in bits: $4 \times 10^6 \times 8 = 32 \times 10^6$ bits.

$$t = \frac{32 \times 10^6}{500 \times 10^3} = 64\ \text{s}.$$

**Why the others fail:**

- **16 s** is the time if the whole path ran at 2 Mbps (the fastest link). A chain is never faster than its slowest link.
- **8 s** would need a 4 Mbps link, which is not in the path.
- **731 s** is far beyond even the slowest link alone (64 s), so it is not a transfer time on this path.

**Key idea:** end-to-end throughput equals the bottleneck bandwidth when everything else is negligible.

</details>
