# 2026 CS2 Q43: CRC remainder

## Question

Data bits **110001011** are sent over a link that uses a Cyclic Redundancy Check. The generator pattern is **1001**. Which option shows the remainder bits appended to the data before transmission?

- (A) 011
- (B) 101
- (C) 000
- (D) 100

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Refresher.** The generator 1001 has 4 bits, so append $4 - 1 = 3$ zeros to the data. Divide by the generator using XOR (modulo-2 division). The 3-bit remainder is the CRC.

Dividend: `110001011000`. At each step, if the leading bit is 1, XOR the generator under it.

| Step | Bits before | XOR 1001 at position | Bits after |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 110001011000 | 0 | 010101011000 |
| 2 | 010101011000 | 1 | 000111011000 |
| 3 | 000111011000 | 3 | 000011111000 |
| 4 | 000011111000 | 4 | 000001101000 |
| 5 | 000001101000 | 5 | 000000100000 |
| 6 | 000000100000 | 6 | 000000000100 |

The last 3 bits are **100**. That is the remainder, which is **(D)**.

The transmitted bits are `110001011` followed by `100`.

**Verified** with a modulo-2 division program: remainder 100.

**Key idea:** append $r$ zeros (generator length minus 1), divide with XOR, and the leftover $r$ bits are the CRC.

</details>
