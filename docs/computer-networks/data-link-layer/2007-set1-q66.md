# 2007 CS Q66: 1-bit delay in a token ring

## Question

In a token ring network the transmission speed is $10^7$ bps and the propagation speed is 200 metres/$\mu$s. The 1-bit delay in this network is equivalent to:

- (A) 500 metres of cable.
- (B) 200 metres of cable.
- (C) 20 metres of cable.
- (D) 50 metres of cable.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

One bit takes $1/10^7\ \text{s} = 0.1\ \mu\text{s}$ to transmit. In that time a signal travels
$$0.1\ \mu\text{s} \times 200\ \text{m}/\mu\text{s} = 20\ \text{m}.$$

So the 1-bit delay equals $20$ metres of cable: (C).

- (B) 200 m is the distance covered in $1\ \mu$s, which is the delay of 10 bits at this rate, not 1 bit. (A) 500 m and (D) 50 m correspond to $2.5\ \mu$s and $0.25\ \mu$s.

**Code check:** Python: $1/10^7 = 10^{-7}$ s; $200\times 10^6 \times 10^{-7} = 20$ m.

**Key idea:** distance equivalent of one bit $=$ propagation speed $\times$ bit time.
