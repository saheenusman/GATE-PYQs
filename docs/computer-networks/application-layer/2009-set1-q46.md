# 2009 CS Q46: Correct equations of the RSA cryptosystem

## Question

In the RSA public key cryptosystem, the private and public keys are $(e, n)$ and $(d, n)$ respectively, where $n = p * q$ and $p$ and $q$ are large primes. Besides, $n$ is public and $p$ and $q$ are private. Let $M$ be an integer such that $0 < M < n$ and $\phi(n) = (p-1)(q-1)$. Now consider the following equations.

- I. $M' = M^e \bmod n$ and $M = (M')^d \bmod n$
- II. $ed \equiv 1 \bmod n$
- III. $ed \equiv 1 \bmod \phi(n)$
- IV. $M' = M^e \bmod \phi(n)$ and $M = (M')^d \bmod \phi(n)$

Which of the above equations correctly represent RSA cryptosystem?

- (A) I and II
- (B) I and III
- (C) II and IV
- (D) III and IV

*Printed as is: the paper calls $(e, n)$ the private key and $(d, n)$ the public key, which is the reverse of the usual naming. It does not affect the answer, since the question is about which equations hold.*

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

RSA works as follows. Pick $n = pq$ and $\phi(n) = (p-1)(q-1)$. Choose $e$ and $d$ with

$$ed \equiv 1 \pmod{\phi(n)}.$$

Encrypt with $M' = M^e \bmod n$ and decrypt with $M = (M')^d \bmod n$. This works because $M^{ed} = M^{1 + k\phi(n)} \equiv M \pmod n$ (Euler's theorem).

- **I is correct:** encryption and decryption are done modulo $n$.
- **III is correct:** $e$ and $d$ are inverses modulo $\phi(n)$.
- **II is wrong:** inverses modulo $n$ do not give $M^{ed} \equiv M \pmod n$.
- **IV is wrong:** the arithmetic is done modulo $n$, not modulo $\phi(n)$. Reducing modulo $\phi(n)$ loses information about $M$.

So I and III: (B).

- (A) contains II. (C) contains II and IV. (D) contains IV.

**Code check:** a toy RSA with $p = 61$, $q = 53$ ($n = 3233$, $\phi(n) = 3120$), $e = 17$, $d = 2753$:

- $ed \bmod \phi(n) = 1$ (III holds), while $ed \bmod n = 1539$.
- Equation I recovered $M$ for all $3232$ messages. Equation IV recovered it for only $1754$ of $3232$.
- Using $d' = e^{-1} \bmod n$ (equation II) in place of $d$, decryption recovered $M$ for only $3$ of the $3232$ messages.

**Classification note:** RSA is network security, which has no folder of its own in the topic list. I filed it under `application-layer`.

**Key idea:** the exponents are inverses modulo $\phi(n)$, but the messages are reduced modulo $n$.

</details>
