# 2024 CS2 Q19: The ACID property that survives crashes

## Question

Once the DBMS informs the user that a transaction has been successfully completed, its effect should persist even if the system crashes before all its changes are reflected on disk. This property is called

- (A) durability
- (B) atomicity
- (C) consistency
- (D) isolation

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A (durability)**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Durability** means that once a transaction commits, its changes are permanent even after a crash. The DBMS achieves this with a write-ahead log: the log is forced to disk at commit, and recovery redoes committed work.

**Why the others fail:**

- **Atomicity:** all or nothing (a transaction is not left half done).
- **Consistency:** the database moves from one valid state to another.
- **Isolation:** concurrent transactions do not interfere.

**Key idea:** "persists even after a crash" is the signature of durability.

</details>
