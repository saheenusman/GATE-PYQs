# 2008 CS Q41: Maximum number of node splits in a B-tree of order 4

## Question

A B-tree of order 4 is built from scratch by 10 successive insertions. What is the maximum number of node splitting operations that may take place?

- (A) 3
- (B) 4
- (C) 5
- (D) 6

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Order 4: a node holds at most 3 keys, so inserting a 4th key into a node splits it, and the middle key moves up. A split of the root adds a level.

**Ascending insertion 1, 2, ..., 10** (promoting the left of the two middle keys):

| Insert | What happens | Splits so far |
|---|---|---|
| 1, 2, 3 | root leaf $[1,2,3]$ | 0 |
| 4 | $[1,2,3,4]$ splits; 2 goes up | 1 |
| 5 | goes into $[3,4]$ | 1 |
| 6 | $[3,4,5,6]$ splits; 4 goes up | 2 |
| 7 | goes into $[5,6]$ | 2 |
| 8 | $[5,6,7,8]$ splits; 6 goes up; root $[2,4,6]$ | 3 |
| 9 | goes into $[7,8]$ | 3 |
| 10 | $[7,8,9,10]$ splits, 8 goes up into the full root, which splits too (a new root) | 5 |

So $5$ splits are possible; the exhaustive check below shows it cannot be more.

**Code check:** I tried all $10! = 3{,}628{,}800$ insertion orders with a simulator, for both conventions (promote the left or the right middle key). The maximum is **5 splits** in both. The number of orders giving 2, 3 and 5 splits were 870912, 2716416 and 41472 respectively (the same for both conventions), and no order gives 6. (Which order gives the maximum depends on the convention, but the maximum does not.)

- (A) 3 and (B) 4 are reached by many orders but are not the maximum.
- (D) 6 is never reached.

**Key idea:** in a B-tree of order 4 a split happens whenever a node overflows, and a cascade up to the root counts several splits at once.

</details>
