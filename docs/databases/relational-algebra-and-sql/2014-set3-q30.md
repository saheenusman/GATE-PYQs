# 2014 CS3 Q30: Relational algebra: employees older than all their dependents

## Question

Consider the relational schema given below, where `eId` of the relation `dependent` is a foreign key referring to `empId` of the relation `employee`. Assume that every employee has at least one associated dependent in the `dependent` relation.

```
employee (empId, empName, empAge)
dependent (depId, eId, depName, depAge)
```

(Keys: `empId` for employee; `depId, eId` for dependent.)

Consider the following relational algebra query:

$$\Pi_{empId}(employee) - \Pi_{empId}\big(employee \bowtie_{(empId = eId) \wedge (empAge \le depAge)} dependent\big)$$

The above query evaluates to the set of `empId`s of employees whose age is greater than that of

- (A) some dependent.
- (B) all dependents.
- (C) some of his/her dependents.
- (D) all of his/her dependents.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

The join pairs each employee with **his or her own** dependents (`empId = eId`) whose age is at least the employee's age (`empAge ≤ depAge`). The projection gives the employees who have **at least one dependent at least as old**.

Subtracting this set from all employees leaves the employees who have **no** such dependent, which means all of their dependents are strictly younger: the employee is older than **all of his/her dependents**. The assumption that every employee has a dependent makes "all" non-vacuous.

- (A), (B) compare with dependents in general, but the join is restricted to the employee's own (`empId = eId`).
- (C) "some of his/her dependents" is too weak: the query also removes every employee who has even one dependent that is as old or older.

**Code check:** in SQLite with sample data, the query returns only the employee whose dependents are all younger. An employee with one older dependent, or one of equal age, is excluded.

**Key idea:** $R - \pi(\text{join with } \le)$ expresses "for all ... strictly greater".

</details>
