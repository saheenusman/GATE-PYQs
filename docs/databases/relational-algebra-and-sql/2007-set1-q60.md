# 2007 CS Q60: Tuple calculus: employees with no female subordinates

## Question

Consider the relation $employee(name, sex, supervisorName)$ with $name$ as the key. $supervisorName$ gives the name of the supervisor of the employee under consideration. What does the following Tuple Relational Calculus query produce?

$$\lbrace e.name \mid employee(e) \wedge (\forall x)[\neg employee(x) \vee x.supervisorName \ne e.name \vee x.sex = \text{"male"}] \rbrace$$

- (A) Names of employees with a male supervisor.
- (B) Names of employees with no immediate male subordinates.
- (C) Names of employees with no immediate female subordinates.
- (D) Names of employees with a female supervisor.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

The bracket says: for every $x$, either $x$ is not an employee, or $x$'s supervisor is not $e$, or $x$ is male. Rewrite as an implication:
$$(x \text{ is an employee} \wedge x.supervisorName = e.name) \Rightarrow x.sex = \text{"male"}.$$

So every employee whose supervisor is $e$ (an **immediate subordinate** of $e$) must be male. That means $e$ has **no immediate female subordinates**: (C).

- (A) and (D) talk about the supervisor of $e$, but the condition is on employees whose supervisor is $e$.
- (B) reverses the sex: the condition forces subordinates to be male, so it excludes those with female subordinates, not male ones.

**Code check:** Python: evaluated the formula on 3000 random employee tables and compared with "has no immediate female subordinate": 0 mismatches.
