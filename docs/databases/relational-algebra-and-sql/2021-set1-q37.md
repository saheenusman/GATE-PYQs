# 2021 CS1 Q37: Meaning of a self-join on age

## Question

The following relation records the age of 500 employees of a company, where $empNo$ (indicating the employee number) is the key:

$empAge(\underline{empNo}, age)$

Consider the following relational algebra expression:

$$\Pi_{empNo}\left(empAge \bowtie_{(age>age1)} \rho_{empNo1,\,age1}(empAge)\right)$$

What does the above expression generate?

- (A) Employee numbers of only those employees whose age is the maximum.
- (B) Employee numbers of only those employees whose age is more than the age of exactly one other employee.
- (C) Employee numbers of all employees whose age is not the minimum.
- (D) Employee numbers of all employees whose age is the minimum.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

The join pairs every employee $e$ with every employee $e'$ (renamed $empNo1, age1$) such that $e.age>e'.age$. The projection keeps $e.empNo$.

So $e$ survives exactly when **some** employee is younger than $e$, i.e. when $e$'s age is **not the minimum**.

- (A) The maximum-age employees survive, but so do many others. Wrong.
- (B) "Exactly one other" is not required: one younger employee is enough (and projection removes duplicates).
- (C) **Correct.**
- (D) The minimum-age employees are exactly the ones **removed**.

**Key idea:** a self-join with $>$ followed by a projection keeps everything except the minimum (or maximum, if reversed).

</details>
