# 2010 CS Q43: Maximum size of a natural join under functional dependencies

## Question

The following functional dependencies hold for relations $R(A, B, C)$ and $S(B, D, E)$:

$B \to A,\quad A \to C$

The relation $R$ contains $200$ tuples and the relation $S$ contains $100$ tuples. What is the maximum number of tuples possible in the natural join $R \bowtie S$?

- (A) $100$
- (B) $200$
- (C) $300$
- (D) $2000$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

From $B \to A$ and $A \to C$ we get $B \to C$, so $B \to ABC$: **$B$ is a key of $R$**. (Printed as is: the paper writes "200tuples" and "100tuples" without a space; the numbers are $200$ and $100$.)

The natural join matches on the common attribute $B$. Since $B$ is unique in $R$, each tuple of $S$ matches **at most one** tuple of $R$. So the join has at most $|S| = 100$ tuples.

The bound is reached when every $B$ value in $S$ also appears in $R$. So the maximum is $100$: (A).

- (B) $200$ is $|R|$; the join cannot exceed $|S|$, since each tuple of $S$ pairs with at most one tuple of $R$.
- (C) $300$ and (D) $2000$ exceed $|S|$ as well.

**Classification note:** the question is about the size of a join, so I filed it under `relational-algebra-and-sql`, with `functional-dependencies-and-normalization` as an extra topic.

**Code check:** $20{,}000$ random small instances were generated with $B$ a key of $R$ and the dependencies $B \to A$, $A \to C$ holding. The join never had more tuples than $S$, and it had exactly $|S|$ tuples in $1251$ of them.

**Key idea:** if the join attribute is a key of one relation, the join has at most as many tuples as the other relation.

</details>
