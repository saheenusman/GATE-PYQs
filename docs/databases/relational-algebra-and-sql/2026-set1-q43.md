# 2026 CS1 Q43: From tuple relational calculus to relational algebra

## Question

Consider relations $R(P, Q)$ and $S(X, Y)$. Let

$$E = \lbrace \langle u \rangle \mid \exists v\ \exists w\ \ \langle u, v \rangle \in R \wedge \langle v, w \rangle \in S \rbrace$$

be a tuple relational calculus expression. Which relational algebra expression is equivalent to $E$?

- (A) $\Pi_P(R \bowtie_{R.P = S.X} S)$
- (B) $\Pi_P(S \bowtie_{S.X = R.Q} R)$
- (C) $\Pi_P(R \bowtie_{R.P = S.Y} S)$
- (D) $\Pi_P(S \bowtie_{S.Y = R.Q} R)$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Read the calculus carefully.**

- $\langle u, v \rangle \in R$ means $u$ is the **first** attribute $P$ of $R$ and $v$ is the **second** attribute $Q$.
- $\langle v, w \rangle \in S$ means $v$ is the **first** attribute $X$ of $S$ and $w$ is $Y$.

The same $v$ appears in both, so the join condition is

$$R.Q = S.X.$$

We output $u$, which is $R.P$, so the projection is $\Pi_P$.

$$E = \Pi_P\big(R \bowtie_{R.Q = S.X} S\big).$$

Joining is symmetric, so $S \bowtie_{S.X = R.Q} R$ is the same thing. That is **(B)**.

**Why the others fail:**

- **(A)** joins $R.P = S.X$: it joins on the wrong attribute of $R$.
- **(C)** joins $R.P = S.Y$: wrong attribute of both.
- **(D)** joins $S.Y = R.Q$: wrong attribute of $S$.

**Verified.** I compared the four expressions with the calculus on 3000 random pairs of small relations: only (B) always matched.

**Key idea:** a variable that appears in two predicates ($v$ here) becomes the join condition, and only the free variable ($u$) is projected.

</details>
