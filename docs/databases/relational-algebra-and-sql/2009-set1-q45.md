# 2009 CS Q45: Equivalent relational algebra, calculus and SQL queries

## Question

Let $R$ and $S$ be relational schemes such that $R = \lbrace a, b, c \rbrace$ and $S = \lbrace c \rbrace$. Now consider the following queries on the database:

- I. $\pi_{R-S}(r) - \pi_{R-S}\big(\pi_{R-S}(r) \times s - \pi_{R-S,S}(r)\big)$
- II. $\lbrace t \mid t \in \pi_{R-S}(r) \wedge \forall u \in s\ (\exists v \in r\ (u = v[s] \wedge t = v[R-S])) \rbrace$
- III. $\lbrace t \mid t \in \pi_{R-S}(r) \wedge \forall v \in r\ (\exists u \in s\ (u = v[s] \wedge t = v[R-S])) \rbrace$
- IV. `Select R.a, R.b from R, S where R.c = S.c`

Which of the above queries are equivalent?

- (A) I and II
- (B) I and III
- (C) II and IV
- (D) III and IV

*Note (mine, not printed in the paper): $r$ and $s$ are the relations on schemes $R$ and $S$, and $\pi_{R-S}$ keeps the attributes $a, b$.*

<summary>Solution</summary>

**Official key:** the key table in the supplied PDF has no entry for Q45, so I cannot check against it. The answer below is my own derivation.

Here $R - S = \lbrace a, b \rbrace$ and $\pi_{R-S,S}(r) = r$.

- **I is relational division $r \div s$.** $\pi_{R-S}(r) \times s$ lists every $(a,b)$ paired with every $c$ in $s$. Removing the pairs that really occur in $r$ leaves the missing pairs. Projecting to $(a,b)$ gives the $(a,b)$ values that miss some $c$ of $s$. Subtracting those from $\pi_{R-S}(r)$ leaves the $(a,b)$ values that occur with **every** $c$ in $s$.
- **II is also division.** It keeps $t$ in $\pi_{R-S}(r)$ such that for **every** $u \in s$ there is a tuple of $r$ with $c = u$ and $(a,b) = t$. That is the definition of division.
- **III is not division.** Its quantifier is $\forall v \in r$: $t$ must equal $v[R-S]$ for **every** tuple $v$ of $r$. So it can only return something when all tuples of $r$ have the same $(a,b)$ value.
- **IV is a join.** It returns the $(a,b)$ of tuples whose $c$ occurs **somewhere** in $s$ ("exists"), not "for all".

So I and II are equivalent: (A).

- (B) fails because III is stricter than I.
- (C) fails because IV is an "exists" query and II is a "for all" query.
- (D) fails for both reasons.

**Example:** $r = \lbrace (0,0,0), (0,1,1), (0,1,2), (1,0,1), (1,1,2), (2,0,1) \rbrace$ and $s = \lbrace 1, 2 \rbrace$. Query I returns $\lbrace (0,1) \rbrace$ (only $(0,1)$ appears with both $c = 1$ and $c = 2$). Query III returns nothing, and query IV returns $(0,1), (1,0), (1,1), (2,0)$.

**Status note:** marked `unverified` because the key gives no answer to compare with. The reasoning was checked by program: on $3000$ random pairs of relations $(r, s)$, including empty $s$, I and II returned the same result every time ($3000$ of $3000$), while I matched III only $1441$ times and matched IV only $1401$ times.

**Key idea:** "$\forall u \in s$ there is a matching tuple in $r$" is division; "$\forall v \in r$" or a plain join is not.
