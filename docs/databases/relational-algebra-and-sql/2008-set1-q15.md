# 2008 CS Q15: Equivalent tuple relational calculus expression for a universal condition

## Question

Which of the following tuple relational calculus expression(s) is/are equivalent to $\forall t \in r\ (P(t))$?

- I. $\neg\, \exists\, t \in r\ (P(t))$
- II. $\exists\, t \notin r\ (P(t))$
- III. $\neg\, \exists\, t \in r\ (\neg P(t))$
- IV. $\exists\, t \notin r\ (\neg P(t))$

Options:

- (A) I only
- (B) II only
- (C) III only
- (D) III and IV only

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

"Every tuple of $r$ satisfies $P$" is the same as "**no** tuple of $r$ fails $P$", by the negation of a quantifier:

$$\forall t \in r\ (P(t)) \;\equiv\; \neg\, \exists\, t \in r\ (\neg P(t)).$$

This is III.

- I says that **no** tuple satisfies $P$, which is much stronger (it would also demand every tuple fails $P$).
- II and IV talk about tuples **not in** $r$. They say nothing about the tuples of $r$ at all.

So only III is equivalent: (C).

**Key idea:** $\forall x\, P \equiv \neg \exists x\, \neg P$.

</details>
