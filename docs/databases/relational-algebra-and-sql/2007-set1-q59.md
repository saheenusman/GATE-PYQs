# 2007 CS Q59: Courses where only some female students enrolled

## Question

Information about a collection of students is given by the relation $studInfo(\underline{studId}, name, sex)$. The relation $enroll(\underline{studId, courseId})$ gives which student has enrolled for (or taken) what course(s). Assume that every course is taken by at least one male and at least one female student. What does the following relational algebra expression represent?

$$\Pi_{courseId}\big( (\Pi_{studId}(\sigma_{sex = \text{"female"}}(studInfo)) \times \Pi_{courseId}(enroll)) - enroll \big)$$

- (A) Courses in which all the female students are enrolled.
- (B) Courses in which a proper subset of female students are enrolled.
- (C) Courses in which only male students are enrolled.
- (D) None of the above.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Step by step:

1. $\Pi_{studId}(\sigma_{sex=\text{"female"}}(studInfo))$ is the set of female students.
2. Crossing with $\Pi_{courseId}(enroll)$ gives **every** (female student, course) pair.
3. Subtracting $enroll$ removes the pairs that actually exist, leaving pairs (female student, course) where she has **not** enrolled in that course.
4. Projecting on $courseId$ keeps the courses in which at least one female student is missing.

Because every course has at least one female student, a course with a missing female student has some, but not all, of the female students: a **proper subset** of them. (B).

- (A) is the complement: such courses are exactly those *not* in the result.
- (C) contradicts the assumption that every course has a female student.
- (D) is wrong since (B) fits.

**Code check:** Python random instances (students, courses, enrolments satisfying the assumption): the expression always equalled the set of courses with a proper subset of the female students enrolled (0 mismatches; the result was non-empty in 490 instances).

**Key idea:** $(\text{all pairs}) - (\text{actual pairs})$ finds the *missing* pairs; projecting them gives courses with at least one non-enrolled female student.
