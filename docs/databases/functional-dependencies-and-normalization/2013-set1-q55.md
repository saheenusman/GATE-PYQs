# 2013 CS Q55: Normal form of R

## Question

**Statement for Linked Answer Questions 54 and 55.** Relation $R$ has eight attributes $ABCDEFGH$. Fields of $R$ contain only atomic values. $F = \lbrace CH \to G,\ A \to BC,\ B \to CFH,\ E \to A,\ F \to EG \rbrace$ is a set of functional dependencies (FDs) so that $F^+$ is exactly the set of FDs that hold for $R$.

The relation $R$ is

- (A) in 1NF, but not in 2NF.
- (B) in 2NF, but not in 3NF.
- (C) in 3NF, but not in BCNF.
- (D) in BCNF.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

From Q54, the candidate keys are $AD, BD, DE, DF$. Prime attributes: $A, B, D, E, F$. Non-prime: $C, G, H$.

**1NF:** all fields are atomic (given). Satisfied.

**2NF:** no non-prime attribute may depend on a proper part of a candidate key.
Take the key $AD$. The FD $A \to BC$ gives $A \to C$, where $C$ is non-prime and $A$ is a proper subset of $AD$. That is a **partial dependency**, so $R$ is **not in 2NF**.

Not in 2NF means it cannot be in 3NF or BCNF either. The highest normal form is 1NF: (A).

**Why the others fail:** (B), (C), (D) all assume 2NF, which the partial dependency $A \to C$ breaks.

**Code check:** the closure script confirmed $A \to C$ with $A$ a proper subset of the key $AD$.

**Key idea:** a non-prime attribute depending on part of a candidate key violates 2NF.

</details>
