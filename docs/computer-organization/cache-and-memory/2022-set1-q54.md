# 2022 CS Q54: Direct-mapped cache with four accessed words

## Question

Consider a system with 2 KB direct mapped data cache with a block size of 64 bytes. The system has a physical address space of 64 KB and a word length of 16 bits. During the execution of a program, four data words P, Q, R, and S are accessed in that order 10 times (i.e., PQRSPQRS...). Hence, there are 40 accesses to data cache altogether. Assume that the data cache is initially empty and no other data words are accessed by the program. The addresses of the first bytes of P, Q, R, and S are 0xA248, 0xC28A, 0xCA8A, and 0xA262, respectively. For the execution of the above program, which of the following statements is/are TRUE with respect to the data cache?

- (A) Every access to S is a hit.
- (B) Once P is brought to the cache it is never evicted.
- (C) At the end of the execution only R and S reside in the cache.
- (D) Every access to R evicts Q from the cache.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A, B and D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Address split (16-bit addresses).** Block 64 B gives 6 offset bits. The cache has $2048/64 = 32$ lines, so 5 index bits. The tag has $16 - 6 - 5 = 5$ bits.

| Word | Address | Block number (addr >> 6) | Index | Tag |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| P | 0xA248 | 649 | 9 | 20 |
| Q | 0xC28A | 778 | 10 | 24 |
| R | 0xCA8A | 810 | 10 | 25 |
| S | 0xA262 | 649 | 9 | 20 |

P and S are in the **same block** (0xA240 to 0xA27F). Q and R **conflict** on line 10.

**Trace one round:** P miss (loads the P/S block in line 9); Q miss (line 10); R miss (evicts Q); S **hit** (same block as P). Next rounds: P hit, Q miss (evicts R), R miss (evicts Q), S hit.

- **(A) True.** S always hits, because P loaded its block first and nothing evicts line 9.
- **(B) True.** Line 9 is never used by Q or R, so P's block is never evicted.
- **(C) False.** At the end, line 9 holds the P/S block (so P is also in the cache) and line 10 holds R. The cache holds P, S and R, not just R and S.
- **(D) True.** Q is in line 10 just before every access to R, so every access to R evicts Q.

**Code check:** a direct-mapped cache simulation of the 40 accesses agreed.

**Key idea:** work out index and tag from the address; words in the same block share a line, and different tags on the same index evict each other.

</details>
