# 2009 CS Q7: Number of 32K x 1 RAM chips for 256 KB

## Question

How many $32\text{K} \times 1$ RAM chips are needed to provide a memory capacity of 256 K-bytes?

- (A) 8
- (B) 32
- (C) 64
- (D) 128

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Capacity needed: $256\ \text{KB} = 256\text{K} \times 8\ \text{bits} = 2048\text{K bits}$.

Capacity of one chip: $32\text{K} \times 1 = 32\text{K bits}$.

$$\frac{2048\text{K}}{32\text{K}} = 64\ \text{chips}.$$

Layout: $8$ chips side by side give one $8$-bit word ($32\text{K}$ words), and $8$ such banks give $256\text{K}$ words: $8 \times 8 = 64$: (C).

- (A) $8$ chips give only $32\ \text{KB}$.
- (B) $32$ chips give $128\ \text{KB}$.
- (D) $128$ chips give $512\ \text{KB}$.

**Code check:** $256 \cdot 1024 \cdot 8 / (32 \cdot 1024) = 64$.

**Key idea:** divide the total bits needed by the bits in one chip.

</details>
