# 2009 CS Q5: Octal number (1217)8 in other bases

## Question

$(1217)_8$ is equivalent to

- (A) $(1217)_{16}$
- (B) $(028F)_{16}$
- (C) $(2297)_{10}$
- (D) $(0B17)_{16}$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Write each octal digit as 3 bits:

$$1\,2\,1\,7 \to 001\ 010\ 001\ 111.$$

Regroup the $12$ bits in fours: $0010\ 1000\ 1111 = 2\,8\,F$. So $(1217)_8 = (028F)_{16}$: (B).

In decimal: $1 \cdot 512 + 2 \cdot 64 + 1 \cdot 8 + 7 = 655$.

- (A) $(1217)_{16} = 4631$, not $655$.
- (C) $(2297)_{10}$ is not $655$.
- (D) $(0B17)_{16} = 2839$, not $655$.

**Code check:** `int("1217", 8)` gave $655 = \text{0x28f}$. The options evaluated to $4631$ (A), $655$ (B) and $2839$ (D).

**Classification note:** number-base conversion is sometimes filed under digital logic. I filed it under `number-representation-and-alu`.

**Key idea:** convert octal to binary three bits at a time and regroup the bits in fours to get hexadecimal.

</details>
