# 2019 CS2 Q18: Bits needed to avoid overflow in sign-magnitude subtraction

## Question

Consider $Z = X - Y$, where $X$, $Y$ and $Z$ are all in sign-magnitude form. $X$ and $Y$ are each represented in $n$ bits. To avoid overflow, the representation of $Z$ would require a minimum of:

- (A) $n$ bits
- (B) $n - 1$ bits
- (C) $n + 1$ bits
- (D) $n + 2$ bits

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

In $n$-bit sign-magnitude the sign takes 1 bit and the magnitude takes $n - 1$ bits, so each of $X$ and $Y$ lies in

$$-(2^{n-1}-1) \;\text{ to }\; +(2^{n-1}-1).$$

The extreme difference is $X - Y = (2^{n-1}-1) - (-(2^{n-1}-1)) = 2^n - 2$. That magnitude needs $n$ bits, plus 1 sign bit, so **$n+1$ bits**.

**Code check ($n = 4$):** the largest $\lvert X - Y\rvert$ is 14. It needs 4 magnitude bits plus a sign bit, which is 5 $= n + 1$.

**Why the others fail:** $n$ bits overflow for $X = $ max and $Y = -$max. $n-1$ bits is smaller still. $n+2$ bits is more than needed.

**Key idea:** subtracting two numbers of opposite sign adds magnitudes, which needs one extra magnitude bit.

</details>
