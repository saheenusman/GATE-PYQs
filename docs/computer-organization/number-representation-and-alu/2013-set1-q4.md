# 2013 CS Q4: Smallest 8-bit two's complement integer

## Question

The smallest integer that can be represented by an 8-bit number in 2's complement form is

- (A) $-256$
- (B) $-128$
- (C) $-127$
- (D) $0$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

An $n$-bit two's complement number ranges over $-2^{n-1}$ to $2^{n-1}-1$.

For $n=8$: $-128$ to $+127$. The smallest is $-128$, written $1000\,0000$.

**Why the others fail:**

- (A) $-256$ needs 9 bits.
- (C) $-127$ is the smallest in sign-magnitude or 1's complement, not 2's complement.
- (D) $0$ is neither the smallest nor negative.

**Key idea:** two's complement has one extra negative value, $-2^{n-1}$.

</details>
