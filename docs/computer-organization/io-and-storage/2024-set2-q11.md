# 2024 CS2 Q11: DMA data transfer rate with cycle stealing

## Question

Consider a computer with a 4 MHz processor. Its DMA controller can transfer 8 bytes in 1 cycle from a device to main memory through cycle stealing at regular intervals. Which one of the following is the data transfer rate (in bits per second) of the DMA controller if 1% of the processor cycles are used for DMA?

- (A) 2,56,000
- (B) 3,200
- (C) 25,60,000
- (D) 32,000

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C (25,60,000)**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**DMA cycles per second.** The processor runs $4 \times 10^6$ cycles per second, and 1% of them go to DMA:

$$0.01 \times 4 \times 10^6 = 4 \times 10^4 \text{ cycles/s}.$$

**Bits per DMA cycle:** $8 \text{ bytes} = 64 \text{ bits}$.

$$4 \times 10^4 \times 64 = 2{,}560{,}000 \text{ bits/s} = 25{,}60{,}000 \text{ (Indian digit grouping).}$$

**Why the others fail:** 2,56,000 is a factor of 10 short (using 0.1%). 3,200 and 32,000 are in bytes or use a wrong cycle count.

**Key idea:** rate = (number of DMA cycles per second) × (bits moved per cycle).

</details>
