# 2008 CS Q32: Why seek latency is not linear in seek distance

## Question

For a magnetic disk with concentric circular tracks, the seek latency is not linearly proportional to the seek distance due to

- (A) non-uniform distribution of requests
- (B) arm starting and stopping inertia
- (C) higher capacity of tracks on the periphery of the platter
- (D) use of unfair arm scheduling policies

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Moving the arm means **accelerating**, **coasting** and then **decelerating** and settling on the target track. Short seeks are dominated by the fixed start and stop cost; longer seeks spend more time at constant speed. So the time is not proportional to the distance: (B).

- (A) and (D) concern *which* requests arrive and in what order, not the time for one seek.
- (C) concerns how much data a track holds, which does not change the time to move the arm.

**Key idea:** seek time includes fixed acceleration and settling time, so it grows less than linearly with distance.

</details>
