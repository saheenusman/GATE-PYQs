# 2007 CS Q37: Cycles for an ADD, MUL, SUB sequence in a 4-stage pipeline

## Question

Consider a pipelined processor with the following four stages:

- IF: Instruction Fetch
- ID: Instruction Decode and Operand Fetch
- EX: Execute
- WB: Write Back

The IF, ID and WB stages take one clock cycle each to complete the operation. The number of clock cycles for the EX stage depends on the instruction. The ADD and SUB instructions need 1 clock cycle and the MUL instruction needs 3 clock cycles in the EX stage. Operand forwarding is used in the pipelined processor. What is the number of clock cycles taken to complete the following sequence of instructions?

```
ADD R2, R1, R0    R2 <- R1 + R0
MUL R4, R3, R2    R4 <- R3 * R2
SUB R6, R5, R4    R6 <- R5 - R4
```

- (A) 7
- (B) 8
- (C) 10
- (D) 14

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

With forwarding, a result is usable by the next instruction's EX stage in the cycle right after the producing EX ends. Only one instruction can be in EX at a time.

| Instr | IF | ID | EX | WB |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ADD | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
| MUL | 2 | 3 | 4-6 | 7 |
| SUB | 3 | 4 | 7 | 8 |

- MUL needs $R2$ from ADD: forwarded at the end of cycle 3, so MUL starts EX in cycle 4 with no stall.
- SUB needs $R4$ from MUL: available only after MUL's EX ends in cycle 6, and the EX unit is busy until then, so SUB's EX is cycle 7.
- SUB writes back in cycle 8.

Total $= 8$ cycles: (B).

- (A) 7 ignores that MUL occupies EX for three cycles and SUB depends on it.
- (C) 10 assumes extra stalls that forwarding removes.
- (D) 14 is the unpipelined total: $4 + 6 + 4$.

**Code check:** Python in-order pipeline model (IF, ID, EX with 1/3/1 cycles, WB, forwarding, one EX unit): last writeback at cycle 8.

**Key idea:** forwarding removes RAW stalls between EX stages, but a multi-cycle EX still delays the dependent instruction until the result is ready.

</details>
