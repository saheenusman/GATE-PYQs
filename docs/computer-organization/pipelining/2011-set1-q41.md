# 2011 CS Q41: Speedup of a four-stage pipeline with unequal stages

## Question

Consider an instruction pipeline with four stages (S1, S2, S3 and S4) each with combinational circuit only. The pipeline registers are required between each stage and at the end of the last stage. Delays for the stages and for the pipeline registers are as given in the figure.

![A four-stage pipeline drawn left to right: Stage S1 with delay 5 ns, a pipeline register with delay 1 ns, Stage S2 with delay 6 ns, a pipeline register with delay 1 ns, Stage S3 with delay 11 ns, a pipeline register with delay 1 ns, Stage S4 with delay 8 ns, and a final pipeline register with delay 1 ns.](../../assets/2011/set1-q41.png)

What is the approximate speed up of the pipeline in steady state under ideal conditions when compared to the corresponding non-pipeline implementation?

- (A) 4.0
- (B) 2.5
- (C) 1.1
- (D) 3.0

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Non-pipelined:** no pipeline registers are needed, so one instruction takes the sum of the stage delays: $5 + 6 + 11 + 8 = 30$ ns.

**Pipelined:** in steady state one instruction finishes per clock. The clock must fit the slowest stage plus its register: $\max(5, 6, 11, 8) + 1 = 12$ ns.

$$\text{Speedup} = \frac{30}{12} = 2.5$$

So (B).

- (A) $4.0$ is the ideal speedup for $4$ balanced stages with no register overhead.
- (C) $1.1$ and (D) $3.0$ would need clock periods of about $27$ ns and $10$ ns. The clock cannot be shorter than $11 + 1 = 12$ ns.

**Code check:** $(5 + 6 + 11 + 8) / (11 + 1) = 2.5$.

**Key idea:** pipelined clock = slowest stage + register delay; speedup = non-pipelined latency / that clock.

</details>
