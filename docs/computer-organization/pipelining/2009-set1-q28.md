# 2009 CS Q28: Cycles for a 2-iteration loop on a 4-stage pipeline

## Question

Consider a 4 stage pipeline processor. The number of cycles needed by the four instructions I1, I2, I3, I4 in stages S1, S2, S3, S4 is shown below:

| | S1 | S2 | S3 | S4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I1 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| I2 | 1 | 3 | 2 | 2 |
| I3 | 2 | 1 | 1 | 3 |
| I4 | 1 | 2 | 2 | 2 |

What is the number of cycles needed to execute the following loop?

`for (i = 1 to 2) { I1; I2; I3; I4; }`

- (A) 16
- (B) 23
- (C) 28
- (D) 30

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Status: disputed.** The supplied key says (D) 30. My own analysis of an ordinary pipeline gives (B) 23. The answer depends on an assumption the question does not state. Both follow.

Cycle numbers below are the cycle in which each stage finishes. An instruction enters a stage when it has finished the previous stage and the previous instruction has left that stage. Stages can hold finished instructions (buffers between stages).

**One iteration (I1 to I4):**

| Instr | S1 | S2 | S3 | S4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| I2 | 3 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
| I3 | 5 | 7 | 9 | 13 |
| I4 | 6 | 9 | 11 | 15 |

One iteration takes $15$ cycles.

**Two iterations, with the second iteration's I1 following I4 straight into the pipeline:**

| Instr | S1 | S2 | S3 | S4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I1 (2nd) | 8 | 10 | 12 | 16 |
| I2 (2nd) | 9 | 13 | 15 | 18 |
| I3 (2nd) | 11 | 14 | 16 | 21 |
| I4 (2nd) | 12 | 16 | 18 | 23 |

Total: $23$ cycles, so (B).

**What the key says.** (D) 30, which is $2 \times 15$: each iteration runs through the pipeline on its own, and the next one starts only after the previous one has finished. That assumes the loop's iterations do not overlap in the pipeline, which the question does not say, and no branch or dependency is described that would force it. I record the key's answer and flag the conflict.

**Other readings.**

- If an instruction cannot leave a stage until the next stage is free (no buffers), the two iterations take $25$ cycles. This is not an option.
- (A) $16$ and (C) $28$ do not come out of any of the models I tried.

**Code check:** a program computed the finishing times. One iteration: $15$ cycles. Two overlapped iterations: $23$ cycles with buffers between stages, $25$ with none. Two iterations run one after the other: $30$.

**Key idea:** in a pipeline, a stage can start the next instruction as soon as it is free; the total is set by the slowest stages, not by the sum of the stage times.
