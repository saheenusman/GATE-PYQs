# 2016 CS2 Q2: Degree of g(x) - g(-x) for the derivative g of a polynomial f

## Question

Let $f(x)$ be a polynomial and $g(x) = f'(x)$ be its derivative. If the degree of $(f(x) + f(-x))$ is 10, then the degree of $(g(x) - g(-x))$ is ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**9**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Split $f = E + O$ into its even part $E$ and odd part $O$.

- $f(x) + f(-x) = 2E(x)$, so $E$ has degree $10$.
- $g = f' = E' + O'$. The derivative of an even function is odd, and of an odd function is even, so $E'$ is odd and $O'$ is even.
- $g(x) - g(-x) = 2E'(x)$, the odd part of $g$.

$E$ has degree $10$, so $E'$ has degree $9$. The answer is $9$.

**Key idea:** $g(x)-g(-x)$ is twice the derivative of the even part of $f$, so degree $10 - 1 = 9$.

</details>
