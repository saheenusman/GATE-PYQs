# 2026 CS1 Q46: Local extrema and smoothness of a piecewise function

## Question

Let $f: \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ be defined as follows:

$$f(x) = \left(\frac{|x|}{2} - x\right)\left(x - \frac{|x|}{2}\right)$$

Which of the following statements is/are true?

- (A) $f$ has a local maximum
- (B) $f$ has a local minimum
- (C) $f'$ is continuous over $\mathbb{R}$
- (D) $f'$ is not differentiable over $\mathbb{R}$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A, C and D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Simplify.** Let $u = x - \frac{|x|}{2}$. Then $f = (-u)(u) = -u^2$.

| Range | $u$ | $f(x)$ | $f'(x)$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| $x \ge 0$ | $x - \frac{x}{2} = \frac{x}{2}$ | $-\frac{x^2}{4}$ | $-\frac{x}{2}$ |
| $x < 0$ | $x + \frac{x}{2} = \frac{3x}{2}$ | $-\frac{9x^2}{4}$ | $-\frac{9x}{2}$ |

- **(A) True.** $f(x) = -u^2 \le 0$ and $f(0) = 0$, so $x=0$ is a (global) maximum.
- **(B) False.** Away from 0, $f$ keeps decreasing on both sides. There is no other turning point, so no local minimum.
- **(C) True.** At 0 the left derivative is $-\frac{9 \cdot 0}{2} = 0$ and the right derivative is $-\frac{0}{2} = 0$. Both equal 0, so $f'(0)=0$. $f'$ is $-\frac{x}{2}$ for $x>0$ and $-\frac{9x}{2}$ for $x<0$, both tending to 0. So $f'$ is continuous.
- **(D) True.** The derivative of $f'$ is $-\frac12$ for $x>0$ and $-\frac92$ for $x<0$. These differ at 0, so $f'$ has a corner there and is **not differentiable at $x=0$**. So $f'$ is not differentiable over all of $\mathbb{R}$.

I also checked these numerically (max at 0, one-sided slopes of $f'$ are $-0.5$ and $-4.5$).

**Key idea:** a piecewise-quadratic can join smoothly ($f'$ continuous) but still have a kink in $f'$, so $f''$ does not exist at the joint.

</details>
