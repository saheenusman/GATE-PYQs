# 2014 CS1 Q6: Rolle's theorem on a 3x3 determinant function

## Question

Let the function

$$f(\theta) = \begin{vmatrix} \sin\theta & \cos\theta & \tan\theta \\ \sin(\pi/6) & \cos(\pi/6) & \tan(\pi/6) \\ \sin(\pi/3) & \cos(\pi/3) & \tan(\pi/3) \end{vmatrix}$$

where $\theta \in [\pi/6, \pi/3]$ and $f'(\theta)$ denote the derivative of $f$ with respect to $\theta$. Which of the following statements is/are **TRUE**?

- (I) There exists $\theta \in (\pi/6, \pi/3)$ such that $f'(\theta) = 0$.
- (II) There exists $\theta \in (\pi/6, \pi/3)$ such that $f'(\theta) \ne 0$.

- (A) I only
- (B) II only
- (C) Both I and II
- (D) Neither I nor II

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Statement I.** At $\theta = \pi/6$ the first row equals the second row, so $f(\pi/6) = 0$. At $\theta = \pi/3$ it equals the third row, so $f(\pi/3) = 0$. $f$ is continuous and differentiable on the interval, so by **Rolle's theorem** some $\theta$ in $(\pi/6, \pi/3)$ has $f'(\theta)=0$. I is true.

**Statement II.** If $f'$ were $0$ everywhere on the interval, $f$ would be constant, hence $0$. But expanding along the first row, $f(\theta) = A\sin\theta + B\cos\theta + C\tan\theta$ with $A, B, C$ not all zero, and it is not identically zero. So $f'(\theta) \ne 0$ somewhere. II is true.

**Code check:** `sympy` gives $f(\pi/6) = f(\pi/3) = 0$, $f(\pi/4) \approx 0.0977 \ne 0$, and $f'(\pi/6) \approx 0.565$, $f'(\pi/3) \approx -1.077$. The sign change confirms a zero of $f'$ in between.

- (A) misses II.
- (B) misses I.
- (D) is false because both hold.

**Key idea:** equal end values give a stationary point (Rolle); a non-constant function has a non-zero derivative somewhere.

</details>
