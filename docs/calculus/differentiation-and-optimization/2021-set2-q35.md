# 2021 CS2 Q35: Largest possible f(3) given a bound on f'

## Question

Suppose that $f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}$ is a continuous function on the interval $[-3,3]$ and a differentiable function in the interval $(-3,3)$ such that for every $x$ in the interval, $f'(x)\le2$. If $f(-3)=7$, then $f(3)$ is at most ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**19**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

By the Mean Value Theorem there is a $c\in(-3,3)$ with

$$f(3)-f(-3)=f'(c)\,(3-(-3))=6f'(c)\le6\cdot2=12.$$

So $f(3)\le7+12=19$.

The bound is reached by $f(x)=2x+13$: it has $f(-3)=7$, $f'(x)=2$ and $f(3)=19$.

**Key idea:** the Mean Value Theorem turns a bound on $f'$ into a bound on the total change of $f$.

</details>
