# 2021 CS1 Q30: Limit of a square-root expression at x = -3

## Question

Consider the following expression.

$$\lim_{x\to -3}\frac{\sqrt{2x+22}-4}{x+3}$$

The value of the above expression (rounded to 2 decimal places) is ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**0.25**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

At $x=-3$: $\sqrt{-6+22}=4$, so the form is $0/0$.

**Method 1 (derivative).** The limit is the derivative of $g(x)=\sqrt{2x+22}$ at $x=-3$:

$g'(x)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2x+22}} \Rightarrow g'(-3)=\dfrac14=0.25$.

**Method 2 (rationalize).**

$\dfrac{(2x+22)-16}{(x+3)(\sqrt{2x+22}+4)}=\dfrac{2}{\sqrt{2x+22}+4}\to\dfrac{2}{8}=0.25$.

Checked numerically at $x=-3+10^{-7}$: 0.2499999.

**Key idea:** a $0/0$ limit of the form $\dfrac{g(x)-g(a)}{x-a}$ is $g'(a)$.

</details>
