# 2017 CS1 Q28: Limit of a 0/0 rational function at x = 1

## Question

The value of $\displaystyle \lim_{x \to 1} \frac{x^{7} - 2x^{5} + 1}{x^{3} - 3x^{2} + 2}$

- (A) is $0$
- (B) is $-1$
- (C) is $1$
- (D) does not exist

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Check the form.** At $x=1$ the numerator is $1-2+1 = 0$ and the denominator is $1-3+2 = 0$. So the form is $0/0$.

**Apply L'Hôpital's rule once.**

$$\lim_{x\to1}\frac{7x^{6}-10x^{4}}{3x^{2}-6x} = \frac{7-10}{3-6} = \frac{-3}{-3} = 1$$

The limit is $1$: **(C)**.

**Check by factoring.** $x=1$ is a root of both, so each has a factor $(x-1)$. Dividing out gives numerator $\to (x-1)(x^6+x^5-x^4-x^3-x^2-x-1)$ and denominator $(x-1)(x^2-2x-2)$. At $x=1$ the quotient is $\dfrac{1+1-1-1-1-1-1}{1-2-2} = \dfrac{-3}{-3} = 1$.

**Why the others fail.** (A) and (B) come from mistakes in the derivative. (D) the limit exists: both one-sided limits equal $1$.

**Code check:** evaluating the quotient at $x = 1\mp10^{-6}$ gave $1.00000033$ and $0.99999967$.

**Key idea:** for a $0/0$ form, differentiate numerator and denominator, then substitute.

</details>
