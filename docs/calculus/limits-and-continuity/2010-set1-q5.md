# 2010 CS Q5: Limit of (1 - 1/n) to the power 2n

## Question

What is the value of $\displaystyle \lim_{n \to \infty} \left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right)^{2n}$?

- (A) $0$
- (B) $e^{-2}$
- (C) $e^{-1/2}$
- (D) $1$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Use $\displaystyle \lim_{n \to \infty} \left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right)^{n} = e^{-1}$.

$$\left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right)^{2n} = \left[\left(1 - \frac{1}{n}\right)^{n}\right]^{2} \to \left(e^{-1}\right)^{2} = e^{-2}$$

So the answer is $e^{-2}$: (B).

- (A) $0$ and (D) $1$ are the limits of $(1-\tfrac1n)^{n^2}$-like and $(1-\tfrac1n)^{\sqrt n}$-like forms; here the exponent grows only linearly, so the limit is a finite number strictly between them.
- (C) $e^{-1/2}$ is the limit of $(1 - \tfrac1n)^{n/2}$, which has half the exponent instead of double.

**Code check:** at $n = 10^7$ the expression is $0.1353352698$, and $e^{-2} = 0.1353352832$.

**Key idea:** $(1 + a/n)^{bn} \to e^{ab}$; here $a = -1$ and $b = 2$.

</details>
