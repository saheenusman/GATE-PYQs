# 2016 CS1 Q3: Limit of sin(x-4)/(x-4) as x tends to 4

## Question

$$\lim_{x \to 4} \frac{\sin(x-4)}{x-4} = \_\_\_\_\_\_.$$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**1**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Substitute $t = x - 4$. As $x \to 4$, $t \to 0$:

$$\lim_{t \to 0} \frac{\sin t}{t} = 1$$

This is the standard limit. Direct substitution gives $0/0$, so the standard limit (or L'Hôpital's rule) is needed: $\dfrac{\cos t}{1} \to 1$.

**Key idea:** $\lim_{t \to 0} \sin t / t = 1$ after the substitution $t = x - 4$.

</details>
