# 2019 CS2 Q23: Limit of a rational function at 3

## Question

Compute $\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 3} \frac{x^4 - 81}{2x^2 - 5x - 3}$

- (A) 1
- (B) 53/12
- (C) 108/7
- (D) Limit does not exist

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

At $x = 3$ both numerator and denominator are $0$, a $0/0$ form. Factor and cancel $(x-3)$.

$$x^4 - 81 = (x-3)(x+3)(x^2+9), \qquad 2x^2 - 5x - 3 = (2x+1)(x-3).$$

$$\lim_{x\to 3}\frac{(x+3)(x^2+9)}{2x+1} = \frac{6 \cdot 18}{7} = \frac{108}{7}.$$

**Check with L'Hopital:** $\dfrac{4x^3}{4x - 5}$ at $x = 3$ is $\dfrac{108}{7}$.

**Code check:** a symbolic limit gives $108/7$.

**Why the others fail:** (A) and (B) come from arithmetic slips. (D) is wrong because the zero cancels, so the limit is finite.

**Key idea:** for $0/0$, factor out the common zero or apply L'Hopital.

</details>
