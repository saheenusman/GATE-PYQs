# 2022 CS Q34: A limit with a square root

## Question

The value of the following limit is ______.

$$\lim_{x \to 0^+} \frac{\sqrt{x}}{1 - e^{2\sqrt{x}}}$$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**-0.5**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Substitute $t = \sqrt{x}$.** As $x \to 0^+$, $t \to 0^+$, and the limit becomes

$$\lim_{t \to 0^+} \frac{t}{1 - e^{2t}}$$

This is of the form $0/0$. By L'Hôpital's rule:

$$\lim_{t \to 0} \frac{1}{-2e^{2t}} = -\frac12$$

(Or use $1 - e^{2t} \approx -2t$ for small $t$, so the ratio tends to $-1/2$.)

**Key idea:** substitute to remove the square root, then apply L'Hôpital or a first-order expansion of $e^{2t}$.

</details>
