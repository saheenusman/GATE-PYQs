# 2014 CS2 Q5: Sign of f(0)f(4) for a cubic with roots 1, 2, 3

## Question

A non-zero polynomial $f(x)$ of degree 3 has roots at $x = 1$, $x = 2$ and $x = 3$. Which one of the following must be TRUE?

- (A) $f(0)f(4) < 0$
- (B) $f(0)f(4) > 0$
- (C) $f(0) + f(4) > 0$
- (D) $f(0) + f(4) < 0$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Three roots and degree 3 give $f(x) = a(x-1)(x-2)(x-3)$ with $a \ne 0$.

$$f(0) = a(-1)(-2)(-3) = -6a, \qquad f(4) = a(3)(2)(1) = 6a$$

$$f(0)f(4) = -36a^2 < 0$$

- (B) is the opposite sign.
- (C) and (D): $f(0) + f(4) = 0$, which is neither $> 0$ nor $< 0$.

**Classification flag:** this is polynomial algebra. It is filed under `limits-and-continuity` as the nearest calculus topic (the sign change between $0$ and $4$).

**Key idea:** write the polynomial from its roots and compare the values on either side.

</details>
