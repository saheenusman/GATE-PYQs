# 2013 CS Q23: Trapezoidal rule on tabulated data

## Question

Function $f$ is known at the following points:

| $x$ | 0 | 0.3 | 0.6 | 0.9 | 1.2 | 1.5 | 1.8 | 2.1 | 2.4 | 2.7 | 3.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| $f(x)$ | 0 | 0.09 | 0.36 | 0.81 | 1.44 | 2.25 | 3.24 | 4.41 | 5.76 | 7.29 | 9.00 |

The value of $\int_0^3 f(x)\,dx$ computed using the trapezoidal rule is

- (A) 8.983
- (B) 9.003
- (C) 9.017
- (D) 9.045

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

With step $h = 0.3$ and 11 points:

$$\int_0^3 f\,dx \approx h\left[\frac{f_0+f_{10}}{2} + \sum_{i=1}^{9} f_i\right]$$

- Interior sum: $0.09+0.36+0.81+1.44+2.25+3.24+4.41+5.76+7.29 = 25.65$
- End term: $(0 + 9)/2 = 4.5$
- Total: $0.3 \times (4.5 + 25.65) = 0.3 \times 30.15 = 9.045$

The data are $f(x)=x^2$, whose exact integral is $9$. The trapezoidal rule overestimates for a convex function, so a value above 9 is expected.

**Why the others fail:** (A), (B), (C) are close to 9 but do not come from the trapezoidal formula (for instance, Simpson's rule gives exactly 9 here).

**Flag:** numerical integration is not in the current GATE CS syllabus; filed under `integration` as the nearest topic.

**Code check:** a Python run gave $9.045$.

**Key idea:** trapezoidal rule weights the two end points by $\tfrac12$ and the interior points by 1.

</details>
