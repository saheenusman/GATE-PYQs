# 2014 CS2 Q17: Associativity of the plus and times operators from a grammar

## Question

Consider the grammar defined by the following production rules, with two operators $*$ and $+$

$$S \to T * P$$
$$T \to U \mid T * U$$
$$P \to Q + P \mid Q$$
$$Q \to Id$$
$$U \to Id$$

Which one of the following is TRUE?

- (A) $+$ is left associative, while $*$ is right associative
- (B) $+$ is right associative, while $*$ is left associative
- (C) Both $+$ and $*$ are right associative
- (D) Both $+$ and $*$ are left associative

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Associativity follows from where the recursion sits.

- $T \to T * U$ recurses on the **left**, so $Id * Id * Id$ groups as $(Id * Id) * Id$: $*$ is **left** associative.
- $P \to Q + P$ recurses on the **right**, so $Id + Id + Id$ groups as $Id + (Id + Id)$: $+$ is **right** associative.

**Code check:** a small parser listing all parse trees gives exactly one tree each, `((Id*Id)*Id)*Id` for the $*$ chain and `Id*(Id+(Id+Id))` for the $+$ chain.

So (B). (A) swaps the two, (C) and (D) get one of the two wrong.

**Key idea:** left recursion gives left associativity; right recursion gives right associativity.

</details>
