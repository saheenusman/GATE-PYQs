# 2008 CS Q11: What a handle is in LR parsing

## Question

Which of the following describes a handle (as applicable to LR-parsing) appropriately?

- (A) It is the position in a sentential form where the next shift or reduce operation will occur
- (B) It is a non-terminal whose production will be used for reduction in the next step
- (C) It is a production that may be used for reduction in a future step along with a position in the sentential form where the next shift or reduce operation will occur
- (D) It is the production $p$ that will be used for reduction in the next step along with a position in the sentential form where the right hand side of the production may be found

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

A **handle** of a right-sentential form is a production $A \to \beta$ together with the position where $\beta$ occurs, such that replacing $\beta$ by $A$ is the **next** step of a rightmost derivation traced backwards. That is exactly the **next reduction**, pairing the production with where its right side is found: (D).

- (A) names only a position, not the production.
- (B) names only the non-terminal, not the production and the position.
- (C) speaks of a *future* reduction. A handle is for the reduction that happens in the next step.

**Key idea:** a handle = the production for the next reduction plus the location of its right-hand side in the sentential form.

</details>
