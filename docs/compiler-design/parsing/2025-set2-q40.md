# 2025 CS2 Q40: Is a grammar SLR(1), LALR(1) or CLR(1)

## Question

Given a Context-Free Grammar $G$ as follows:

$$S \to Aa \mid bAc \mid dc \mid bda$$
$$A \to d$$

Which ONE of the following statements is TRUE?

- (A) $G$ is neither LALR(1) nor SLR(1)
- (B) $G$ is CLR(1), not LALR(1)
- (C) $G$ is LALR(1), not SLR(1)
- (D) $G$ is LALR(1), also SLR(1)

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Compute $Follow(A)$ first. $A$ appears in $Aa$ (followed by $a$) and in $bAc$ (followed by $c$), so $Follow(A) = \lbrace a, c\rbrace$.

**SLR(1) fails.** Consider the two states reached after a $d$:

| State reached by | Items | Conflict? |
|---|---|---|
| $d$ from the start | $A \to d\cdot$, $S \to d\cdot c$ | SLR reduces $A \to d$ on all of $Follow(A)=\lbrace a,c\rbrace$, but also shifts $c$: **shift/reduce conflict on $c$** |
| $b$ then $d$ | $A \to d\cdot$, $S \to bd\cdot a$ | SLR reduces on $a$ and shifts $a$: **shift/reduce conflict on $a$** |

So $G$ is **not SLR(1)**.

**LR(1) lookaheads are exact.**

| State | Item | Lookahead | Conflicts with |
|---|---|---|---|
| after $d$ | $A \to d\cdot$ | only $a$ (because $A$ here is followed by $a$) | shift $c$: different symbol, **no conflict** |
| after $bd$ | $A \to d\cdot$ | only $c$ (because $A$ here is followed by $c$) | shift $a$: different symbol, **no conflict** |

So $G$ is **LR(1) (CLR(1))**.

**LALR(1) merges states with the same core.** The two states above have **different** cores (for example $\lbrace A \to d\cdot, S \to d\cdot c\rbrace$ versus $\lbrace A \to d\cdot, S \to bd\cdot a\rbrace$), so they are not merged, and merging elsewhere creates no new conflict. So $G$ is also **LALR(1)**.

I built the LR(1) automaton, merged same-core states to get the LALR(1) automaton, and built the SLR(1) table in code: both LR(1) and LALR(1) had 11 states and **no conflicts**, and SLR(1) had the **two conflicts** above.

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| A | $G$ is LALR(1), so false |
| B | $G$ is LALR(1), so false |
| **C** | Correct |
| D | $G$ is not SLR(1), so false |

**Key idea:** SLR uses the coarse $Follow$ set as lookahead, LR(1) uses exact per-state lookaheads, and LALR keeps those exact lookaheads whenever merging same-core states causes no conflict.

</details>
