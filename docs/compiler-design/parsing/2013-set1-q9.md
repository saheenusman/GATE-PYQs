# 2013 CS Q9: Maximum reduce moves

## Question

What is the maximum number of reduce moves that can be taken by a bottom-up parser for a grammar with no epsilon- and unit-production (i.e., of type $A \to \epsilon$ and $A \to a$) to parse a string with $n$ tokens?

- (A) $n/2$
- (B) $n-1$
- (C) $2n-1$
- (D) $2^n$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Each reduce move corresponds to one internal node of the parse tree. The tree has $n$ leaves (the tokens).

With no $\epsilon$-productions, every internal node has at least one child; with no unit productions, every internal node has **at least 2** children.

A tree with $n$ leaves where every internal node has $\ge 2$ children has at most $n-1$ internal nodes (the maximum is reached by a binary tree). So at most $n-1$ reduces.

**Why the others fail:**

- (A) $n/2$ is too small: a binary tree needs $n-1$ reductions.
- (C) $2n-1$ is the total node count of a full binary tree including leaves, but leaves are shifted, not reduced.
- (D) $2^n$ is far too large; each reduce removes at least one stack entry.

**Key idea:** reduces equal internal nodes, and without $\epsilon$ or unit productions that is at most $n-1$.

</details>
