# 2024 CS1 Q38: Fill in grammar productions from FIRST and FOLLOW sets

## Question

Consider the following grammar $G$, with $S$ as the start symbol. The grammar $G$ has three incomplete productions denoted by (1), (2), and (3).

$$S \to da\,T \mid (1)$$
$$T \to aS \mid bT \mid (2)$$
$$R \to (3) \mid \epsilon$$

The set of terminals is $\lbrace a,b,c,d,f \rbrace$. The FIRST and FOLLOW sets of the different non-terminals are as follows.

$FIRST(S) = \lbrace c,d,f \rbrace$, $FIRST(T) = \lbrace a,b,\epsilon \rbrace$, $FIRST(R) = \lbrace c,\epsilon \rbrace$

$FOLLOW(S) = FOLLOW(T) = \lbrace c,f,\$ \rbrace$, $FOLLOW(R) = \lbrace f \rbrace$

Which one of the following options CORRECTLY fills in the incomplete productions?

- (A) (1) $S \to Rf$, (2) $T \to \epsilon$, (3) $R \to cTR$
- (B) (1) $S \to fR$, (2) $T \to \epsilon$, (3) $R \to cTR$
- (C) (1) $S \to fR$, (2) $T \to cT$, (3) $R \to cR$
- (D) (1) $S \to Rf$, (2) $T \to cT$, (3) $R \to cR$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**(2) must be $T \to \epsilon$.** $FIRST(T) = \lbrace a,b,\epsilon \rbrace$ has $\epsilon$ but no $c$. A production $T \to cT$ would put $c$ into $FIRST(T)$. So options (C) and (D) fail.

**(1): $S \to Rf$ gives the right FIRST.** $R$ can be empty, so $FIRST(Rf) = \lbrace c \rbrace \cup \lbrace f \rbrace$. With $S \to daT$ giving $d$, $FIRST(S) = \lbrace c,d,f \rbrace$ ✓. The alternative $S \to fR$ gives only $\lbrace d,f \rbrace$, missing $c$, so (B) fails.

**(3): $R \to cTR$** gives $FIRST(R) = \lbrace c,\epsilon \rbrace$ ✓, and $R$ is followed by $f$ in $S \to Rf$, so $FOLLOW(R) = \lbrace f \rbrace$ ✓.

**FOLLOW checks for option (A):** $S \to aS$ puts $FOLLOW(S)$ in $FOLLOW(T)$. $T$ sits at the end of $S \to daT$, so $FOLLOW(T) \supseteq FOLLOW(S)$. In $R \to cTR$, $T$ is followed by $R$ (FIRST $= c$, nullable, then $FOLLOW(R) = f$), so $FOLLOW(T)$ gains $\lbrace c, f \rbrace$. All sets come out to $FOLLOW(S) = FOLLOW(T) = \lbrace c,f,\$ \rbrace$ ✓.

**Verified.** I computed FIRST and FOLLOW for all four options with a small program. Only option (A) reproduces the given sets.

**Key idea:** use FIRST of the right-hand sides to decide which production starts with which terminal. $\epsilon$ in a FIRST set means a nullable production.

</details>
