# 2015 CS3 Q15: Easiest and most powerful LR parsing methods

## Question

Among simple LR (SLR), canonical LR, and look-ahead LR (LALR), which of the following pairs identify the method that is very easy to implement and the method that is the most powerful, in that order?

- (A) SLR, LALR
- (B) Canonical LR, LALR
- (C) SLR, canonical LR
- (D) LALR, canonical LR

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

The three methods are nested in power:

$$\text{SLR}(1) \subset \text{LALR}(1) \subset \text{LR}(1)$$

- **SLR** is the easiest: it uses LR(0) item sets with FOLLOW sets for reductions, and the tables are small.
- **Canonical LR(1)** is the most powerful: it carries full lookaheads in each item, which gives the most states and the biggest tables.
- LALR sits in between: it merges canonical LR states with the same core.

SLR, canonical LR: option (C).

**Key idea:** SLR is simplest, canonical LR is strongest, LALR is the practical compromise.

</details>
