# 2011 CS Q27: Parse tree with two operators of different precedence

## Question

Consider two binary operators '$\uparrow$' and '$\downarrow$' with the precedence of operator $\downarrow$ being lower than that of the operator $\uparrow$. Operator $\uparrow$ is right associative while operator $\downarrow$ is left associative. Which one of the following represents the parse tree for expression $(7 \downarrow 3 \uparrow 4 \uparrow 3 \downarrow 2)$?

![Four parse trees for the expression, shown as tilted binary trees. (A) Root down-arrow with children 7 and an up-arrow; that up-arrow has children 3 and another up-arrow whose children are 4 and a down-arrow with leaves 3 and 2. (B) Root down-arrow with children a down-arrow and 2; the inner down-arrow has children 7 and an up-arrow, which has children 3 and an up-arrow with leaves 4 and 3. (C) Root down-arrow with children 7 and a down-arrow; that has children an up-arrow and 2; the up-arrow has children an up-arrow with leaves 3 and 4, and the leaf 3. (D) Root down-arrow with children an up-arrow and 2; the up-arrow has children an up-arrow and 3; that up-arrow has children a down-arrow with leaves 7 and 3, and the leaf 4.](../../assets/2011/set1-q27.png)

Options are the four trees (A) to (D) in the figure.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Parenthesize using precedence first, then associativity.

1. $\uparrow$ binds tighter than $\downarrow$, so the $\uparrow$ operands group first: $3 \uparrow 4 \uparrow 3$.
2. $\uparrow$ is right associative: $3 \uparrow (4 \uparrow 3)$.
3. Now the expression is $7 \downarrow [3 \uparrow (4 \uparrow 3)] \downarrow 2$, and $\downarrow$ is left associative:

$$\big(7 \downarrow (3 \uparrow (4 \uparrow 3))\big) \downarrow 2$$

The tree has root $\downarrow$ with right child $2$ and left child $\downarrow(7, \uparrow(3, \uparrow(4, 3)))$. This is (B).

- (A) groups $3 \downarrow 2$ last and makes $\downarrow$ right associative.
- (C) makes $\downarrow$ right associative and $\uparrow$ left associative: $(3 \uparrow 4) \uparrow 3$.
- (D) makes $\uparrow$ left associative and joins $7 \downarrow 3$ first, ignoring precedence.

**Code check:** a precedence-climbing parser with these rules produced `(v (v 7 (^ 3 (^ 4 3))) 2)`, which is tree (B).

**Key idea:** higher precedence groups first; associativity breaks ties among equal operators (right for the up-arrow, left for the down-arrow).

</details>
