# 2013 CS Q40: Merging LR(1) item sets into LALR

## Question

Consider the following two sets of LR(1) items of an LR(1) grammar.

| Set 1 | Set 2 |
|---|---|
| $X \to c.X,\ c/d$ | $X \to c.X,\ \$$ |
| $X \to .cX,\ c/d$ | $X \to .cX,\ \$$ |
| $X \to .d,\ c/d$ | $X \to .d,\ \$$ |

Which of the following statements related to merging of the two sets in the corresponding LALR parser is/are **FALSE**?

1. Cannot be merged since look aheads are different.
2. Can be merged but will result in S-R conflict.
3. Can be merged but will result in R-R conflict.
4. Cannot be merged since *goto* on $c$ will lead to two different sets.

- (A) 1 only
- (B) 2 only
- (C) 1 and 4 only
- (D) 1, 2, 3 and 4

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

LALR merges LR(1) states that have the **same core** (same items ignoring look-aheads). The two sets have the same core: $X \to c.X$, $X \to .cX$, $X \to .d$.

1. **False.** Different look-aheads are exactly what LALR merges (it unions them to $c/d/\$$).
2. **False.** A shift-reduce conflict needs a completed item $A \to \alpha.$ . The sets have none (every dot sits before a symbol), so no conflict.
3. **False.** A reduce-reduce conflict needs two completed items. There are none.
4. **False.** On $c$, both sets go to the state with items $X\to c.X,\ X\to .cX,\ X\to .d$ (same core), so the *goto* targets also merge.

All four statements are false: (D).

**Why the others fail:** (A), (B), (C) each treat at least one of the false statements as true.

**Key idea:** LALR merging is by core; conflicts can only arise from reduce items, and these sets have none.

</details>
