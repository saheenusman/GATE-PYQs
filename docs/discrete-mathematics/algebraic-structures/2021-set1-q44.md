# 2021 CS1 Q44: Cyclic groups of order 6 and their subgroups

## Question

Let $G$ be a group of order 6, and $H$ be a subgroup of $G$ such that $1<|H|<6$. Which one of the following options is correct?

- (A) Both $G$ and $H$ are always cyclic.
- (B) $G$ may not be cyclic, but $H$ is always cyclic.
- (C) $G$ is always cyclic, but $H$ may not be cyclic.
- (D) Both $G$ and $H$ may not be cyclic.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**$H$.** By Lagrange's theorem $|H|$ divides 6, so $|H|\in\lbrace2,3\rbrace$. A group of prime order is cyclic, so $H$ is **always cyclic**.

**$G$.** There are two groups of order 6 up to isomorphism: $\mathbb Z_6$ (cyclic) and $S_3$ (not cyclic, since it has no element of order 6). So $G$ **may not be cyclic**.

So (B). (A) and (C) wrongly say $G$ is always cyclic, and (C) and (D) wrongly say $H$ may fail to be cyclic.

**Key idea:** groups of prime order are cyclic, but a group of composite order need not be.

</details>
