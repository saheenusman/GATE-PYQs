# 2020 CS Q28: Largest proper subgroup of a group of 35 elements

## Question

Let $G$ be a group of 35 elements. Then the largest possible size of a subgroup of $G$ other than $G$ itself is ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**7**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Upper bound from Lagrange's theorem.** The order of a subgroup divides the order of the group. The divisors of $35 = 5 \times 7$ are $1, 5, 7, 35$. Removing $35$ (the group itself), the largest remaining value is **7**.

**It is actually reached.** "Largest possible" asks for the best case over all groups of order 35, so we need a group that has a subgroup of order 7. By Cauchy's theorem, since 7 is a prime dividing 35, every group of order 35 contains an element of order 7, and that element generates a subgroup of order 7. (In fact every group of order 35 is cyclic.) In $\mathbb{Z}_{35}$, the element 5 generates $\lbrace 0, 5, 10, \dots, 30 \rbrace$, which has 7 elements.

I also listed all subgroups of $\mathbb{Z}_{35}$ in code: their orders are exactly $1, 5, 7, 35$.

**Key idea:** Lagrange gives the candidate sizes (the divisors), and Cauchy shows a subgroup of each prime order actually exists.

</details>
