# 2007 CS Q21: Non-isomorphic Abelian groups of order 4

## Question

How many different non-isomorphic Abelian groups of order 4 are there?

- (A) 2
- (B) 3
- (C) 4
- (D) 5

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

By the structure theorem for finite abelian groups, a group of order $p^2$ is a product of cyclic groups whose orders multiply to $p^2$. For order $4 = 2^2$ there are exactly two ways:

- $\mathbb{Z}_4$ (cyclic),
- $\mathbb{Z}_2 \times \mathbb{Z}_2$ (the Klein four-group).

They are not isomorphic ($\mathbb{Z}_4$ has an element of order 4, the Klein group has none). So $2$: (A).

- (B), (C), (D) overcount. The number of abelian groups of order $p^k$ is the number of partitions of $k$, and $2$ has only two partitions.

**Code check:** not needed (the count equals the number of partitions of 2).

**Key idea:** abelian groups of order $p^k$ correspond to partitions of $k$; for $k=2$ that is $\mathbb{Z}_{p^2}$ and $\mathbb{Z}_p\times\mathbb{Z}_p$.
