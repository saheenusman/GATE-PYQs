# 2008 CS Q2: Simplifying a union of set expressions

## Question

If $P, Q, R$ are subsets of the universal set $U$, then

$$(P \cap Q \cap R) \cup (P^c \cap Q \cap R) \cup Q^c \cup R^c \quad \text{is}$$

- (A) $Q^c \cup R^c$
- (B) $P \cup Q^c \cup R^c$
- (C) $P^c \cup Q^c \cup R^c$
- (D) $U$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Step 1.** The first two terms share $Q \cap R$, so they combine:

$$(P \cap Q \cap R) \cup (P^c \cap Q \cap R) = (P \cup P^c) \cap Q \cap R = Q \cap R.$$

**Step 2.** The expression is now $(Q \cap R) \cup Q^c \cup R^c$. By De Morgan, $Q^c \cup R^c = (Q \cap R)^c$. So we are taking $(Q \cap R) \cup (Q \cap R)^c = U$.

The whole expression is the universal set: (D).

- (A), (B) and (C) are proper parts of $U$ in general: they miss the elements of $Q \cap R$ (and, for (B) and (C), more).

**Key idea:** a set united with its complement is $U$; combine terms with a common factor first.

</details>
