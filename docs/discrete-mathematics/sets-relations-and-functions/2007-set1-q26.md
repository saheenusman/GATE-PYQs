# 2007 CS Q26: Hasse diagram of the refinement order on partitions

## Question

Consider the set $S = \lbrace a, b, c, d \rbrace$. Consider the following 4 partitions $\pi_1, \pi_2, \pi_3, \pi_4$ on $S$: $\pi_1 = \lbrace \overline{abcd} \rbrace$, $\pi_2 = \lbrace \overline{ab}, \overline{cd} \rbrace$, $\pi_3 = \lbrace \overline{abc}, \overline{d} \rbrace$, $\pi_4 = \lbrace \overline{a}, \overline{b}, \overline{c}, \overline{d} \rbrace$. Let $\prec$ be the partial order on the set of partitions $S' = \lbrace \pi_1, \pi_2, \pi_3, \pi_4 \rbrace$ defined as follows: $\pi_i \prec \pi_j$ if and only if $\pi_i$ refines $\pi_j$. The poset diagram for $(S', \prec)$ is

![Four candidate Hasse diagrams. (A) A vertical chain pi1 over pi2 over pi3 over pi4. (B) A diamond with pi1 at the top, pi2 and pi4 in the middle, and pi3 at the bottom. (C) A diamond with pi1 at the top, pi2 and pi3 in the middle, and pi4 at the bottom. (D) pi1, pi2 and pi3 in a row at the top, each joined to pi4 at the bottom.](../../assets/2007/set1-q26.png)

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

$\pi_i$ **refines** $\pi_j$ when every block of $\pi_i$ lies inside some block of $\pi_j$.

- $\pi_4$ (all singletons) refines every partition, so it is the bottom.
- $\pi_1$ (one block) is refined by every partition, so it is the top.
- $\pi_2 = \lbrace ab, cd \rbrace$ refines $\pi_1$ only. $\pi_3 = \lbrace abc, d \rbrace$ refines $\pi_1$ only.
- $\pi_2$ and $\pi_3$ are **incomparable**: the block $cd$ is not inside $abc$ or $d$, and $abc$ is not inside $ab$ or $cd$.

Hasse diagram: $\pi_4$ at the bottom, $\pi_2$ and $\pi_3$ side by side above it, $\pi_1$ at the top. That is a diamond: (C).

- (A) is a chain; it would need $\pi_2$ and $\pi_3$ comparable.
- (B) is also a diamond, but it puts $\pi_3$ at the bottom and $\pi_4$ in the middle. In fact $\pi_4$ refines $\pi_3$, not the other way round.
- (D) puts $\pi_1, \pi_2, \pi_3$ on the same level, so $\pi_2 \prec \pi_1$ would be missing.

**Code check:** Python: computing "refines" over the four partitions gives exactly the pairs $(2,1),(3,1),(4,1),(4,2),(4,3)$. Hence the diamond with $\pi_2,\pi_3$ incomparable.

**Key idea:** refinement order on partitions: finer is lower; incomparable partitions sit side by side in the Hasse diagram.
