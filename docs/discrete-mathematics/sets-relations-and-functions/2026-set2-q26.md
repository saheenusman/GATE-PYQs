# 2026 CS2 Q26: Relation defined by square products

## Question

Let $R$ be a binary relation on the set $\lbrace 1, 2, \dots, 10 \rbrace$, where $(x, y) \in R$ if the product $xy$ is the square of an integer. Which of the following properties is/are satisfied by $R$?

- (A) Reflexive
- (B) Symmetric
- (C) Transitive
- (D) Antisymmetric

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A, B and C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**(A) Reflexive: yes.** $x \cdot x = x^2$ is always a square.

**(B) Symmetric: yes.** $xy = yx$, so if $xy$ is a square, so is $yx$.

**(C) Transitive: yes.** Suppose $xy = a^2$ and $yz = b^2$. Multiply:

$$(xy)(yz) = x z\, y^2 = (ab)^2 \ \Rightarrow\ xz = \left(\frac{ab}{y}\right)^2.$$

So $xz$ is the square of a rational number. A positive integer that is the square of a rational is the square of an integer, so $xz$ is a perfect square.

**(D) Antisymmetric: no.** Antisymmetric means $(x,y) \in R$ and $(y,x) \in R$ force $x = y$. But $2 \cdot 8 = 16 = 4^2$, so $(2,8) \in R$ and $(8,2) \in R$ with $2 \ne 8$.

**Picture.** $R$ is reflexive, symmetric and transitive, so it is an equivalence relation. Two numbers are related exactly when they have the same square-free part. Its classes on $\lbrace 1,\dots,10 \rbrace$:

| Class | Members |
|---|---|
| square-free part 1 | 1, 4, 9 |
| 2 | 2, 8 |
| 3 | 3 |
| 5 | 5 |
| 6 | 6 |
| 7 | 7 |
| 10 | 10 |

**Verified** by testing all pairs and triples in Python.

**Key idea:** an equivalence relation is not antisymmetric unless every class has one element. Any class with two members breaks antisymmetry.

</details>
