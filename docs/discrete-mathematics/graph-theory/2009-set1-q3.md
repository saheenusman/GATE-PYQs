# 2009 CS Q3: Degrees of vertices in a connected graph

## Question

Which one of the following is TRUE for any simple connected undirected graph with more than 2 vertices?

- (A) No two vertices have the same degree.
- (B) At least two vertices have the same degree.
- (C) At least three vertices have the same degree.
- (D) All vertices have the same degree.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Take $n \ge 3$ vertices. Because the graph is connected, every vertex has degree at least $1$, and because it is simple, at most $n - 1$. So the degrees come from the $n - 1$ values $1, 2, \dots, n-1$, but there are $n$ vertices. By the pigeonhole principle, **two vertices share a degree**: (B).

- (A) is false: the triangle has all degrees equal to $2$.
- (C) is false: the "paw" graph (a triangle with one pendant vertex attached) has degrees $3, 2, 2, 1$. No three vertices share a degree.
- (D) is false: the path on $3$ vertices has degrees $1, 2, 1$.

**Code check:** all labelled connected graphs on $3$ to $6$ vertices ($27474$ graphs) were generated. Every one had two vertices of equal degree, and none had all degrees distinct. Some had a degree repeated exactly twice and no more, which rules out (C).

**Key idea:** $n$ vertices, only $n-1$ possible degrees (from $1$ to $n-1$), so a repeat is forced.

</details>
