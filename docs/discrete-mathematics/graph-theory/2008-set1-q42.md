# 2008 CS Q42: What cannot be said about a graph made of two edge-disjoint spanning trees

## Question

$G$ is a graph on $n$ vertices and $2n - 2$ edges. The edges of $G$ can be partitioned into two edge-disjoint spanning trees. Which of the following is NOT true for $G$?

- (A) For every subset of $k$ vertices, the induced subgraph has at most $2k - 2$ edges
- (B) The minimum cut in $G$ has at least two edges
- (C) There are two edge-disjoint paths between every pair of vertices
- (D) There are two vertex-disjoint paths between every pair of vertices

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

- **(A) True.** Each spanning tree restricted to $k$ vertices is a forest with at most $k - 1$ edges, so the induced subgraph has at most $2(k-1) = 2k - 2$ edges.
- **(B) True.** Every spanning tree crosses every cut at least once, and the two trees share no edge, so every cut has at least $2$ edges.
- **(C) True.** In each tree there is a path between the two vertices, and the two paths use different edges.
- **(D) Not true.** Two edge-disjoint trees can still share a **cut vertex**. Take two copies of $K_4$ glued at one vertex $v$: $n = 7$ vertices and $12 = 2n - 2$ edges, and each $K_4$ splits into two edge-disjoint Hamiltonian paths, giving two edge-disjoint spanning trees of the whole graph. Every path from a vertex in one $K_4$ to a vertex in the other goes through $v$, so there are not two vertex-disjoint paths.

So (D) is the statement that is not true.

**Code check:** for the two $K_4$'s sharing a vertex, a search found a split of the 12 edges into two spanning trees; removing the shared vertex leaves 2 components.

**Key idea:** edge-disjoint paths do not give vertex-disjoint paths: a cut vertex can be shared.

</details>
