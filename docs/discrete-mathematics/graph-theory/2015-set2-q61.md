# 2015 CS2 Q61: Possible orders of self-complementary graphs

## Question

A graph is self-complementary if it is isomorphic to its complement. For all self-complementary graphs on $n$ vertices, $n$ is

- (A) A multiple of 4
- (B) Even
- (C) Odd
- (D) Congruent to $0 \bmod 4$, or, $1 \bmod 4$.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

If $G \cong \overline{G}$, then $G$ and its complement have the same number of edges. Together they make up $K_n$, which has $\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}$ edges. So each has half of that, and

$$\frac{n(n-1)}{2} \text{ must be even} \;\Rightarrow\; 4 \mid n(n-1)$$

$n$ and $n-1$ share no factor, so 4 divides one of them: $n \equiv 0$ or $1 \pmod 4$. Option (D).

**Why the others fail:**

- (A) $n = 5$: the 5-cycle $C_5$ is self-complementary.
- (B) $n = 5$ again, which is odd.
- (C) $n = 4$: the path $P_4$ is self-complementary.

**Code check:** among all graphs on up to 7 vertices, the self-complementary ones occur only for $n = 1$ (one graph), $n = 4$ (one) and $n = 5$ (two). None for $n = 2, 3, 6, 7$, where $n(n-1)/2$ is odd.

**Key idea:** equal edge counts force $\binom{n}{2}$ to be even, so $n \equiv 0, 1 \pmod 4$.

</details>
