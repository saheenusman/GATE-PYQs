# 2007 CS Q4: Non-planar graph with the fewest edges

## Question

Let $G$ be the non-planar graph with the minimum possible number of edges. Then $G$ has

- (A) 9 edges and 5 vertices
- (B) 9 edges and 6 vertices
- (C) 10 edges and 5 vertices
- (D) 10 edges and 6 vertices

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

By Kuratowski, a non-planar graph contains a subdivision of $K_5$ (10 edges) or $K_{3,3}$ (9 edges). So the minimum edge count is $9$, achieved by $K_{3,3}$, which has $6$ vertices.

- (A) 5 vertices and 9 edges is $K_5$ minus an edge, which is planar.
- (C) $K_5$ is non-planar but has 10 edges, which is not the minimum.
- (D) 10 edges is not the minimum.

So $G = K_{3,3}$: 9 edges, 6 vertices: (B).

**Code check:** networkx planarity test: $K_5$ and $K_{3,3}$ non-planar, $K_5 - e$ planar. All 23,832 labelled graphs on 5 or 6 vertices with at most 8 edges are planar.
