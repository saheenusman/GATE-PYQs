# 2022 CS Q58: Directed spanning trees rooted at vertex 5

## Question

Let $G(V, E)$ be a directed graph, where $V = \lbrace 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 \rbrace$ is the set of vertices and $E$ is the set of directed edges, as defined by the following adjacency matrix $A$.

$$A[i][j] = \begin{cases} 1, & 1 \le j \le i \le 5 \\ 0, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

$A[i][j] = 1$ indicates a directed edge from node $i$ to node $j$. A directed spanning tree of $G$, rooted at $r \in V$, is defined as a subgraph $T$ of $G$ such that the undirected version of $T$ is a tree, and $T$ contains a directed path from $r$ to every other vertex in $V$. The number of such directed spanning trees rooted at vertex 5 is ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**24**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Edges.** There is an edge $i \to j$ exactly when $j \le i$. So vertex $i$ points to every vertex with a smaller or equal number (the diagonal entries are self-loops, which a tree never uses).

**Structure of a tree rooted at 5.** Every vertex $v \ne 5$ needs exactly one incoming tree edge, from its parent $p(v)$. That edge $p(v) \to v$ exists when $v < p(v)$ (the parent is larger). Choosing parents independently:

| Vertex | Possible parents | Choices |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | 2, 3, 4, 5 | 4 |
| 2 | 3, 4, 5 | 3 |
| 3 | 4, 5 | 2 |
| 4 | 5 | 1 |

Every choice gives a valid tree. Each parent is larger than its child, so there is no cycle and following parents always ends at 5. The 4 edges give a tree, and every vertex is reachable from 5.

$$4 \times 3 \times 2 \times 1 = 24$$

**Code check:** enumerating all parent assignments and checking that each vertex reaches 5 gave 24.

**Key idea:** a rooted directed spanning tree is a choice of one parent per non-root vertex without cycles; here "parent larger than child" rules out cycles automatically.

</details>
