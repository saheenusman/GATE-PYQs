# 2016 CS2 Q28: Parity of reaction counts among compounds

## Question

Consider a set $U$ of 23 different compounds in a Chemistry lab. There is a subset $S$ of $U$ of 9 compounds, each of which reacts with exactly 3 compounds of $U$. Consider the following statements:

- I. Each compound in $U \setminus S$ reacts with an odd number of compounds.
- II. At least one compound in $U \setminus S$ reacts with an odd number of compounds.
- III. Each compound in $U \setminus S$ reacts with an even number of compounds.

Which one of the above statements is ALWAYS TRUE?

- (A) Only I
- (B) Only II
- (C) Only III
- (D) None

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Model the compounds as vertices and "reacts with" as undirected edges. By the handshake lemma the sum of all degrees is even.

- The 9 vertices of $S$ have degree $3$ each: sum $= 27$, which is **odd**.
- So the degrees of the other $23 - 9 = 14$ vertices must also add up to an **odd** number.

A sum of integers is odd only if at least one of them is odd. So at least one compound in $U \setminus S$ reacts with an odd number of compounds: **II is always true**.

- III is never possible (all even would make the sum even).
- I can fail: it is enough that one compound there has an even degree.

**Key idea:** $27$ (from $S$) is odd, so the rest must sum to an odd number: at least one odd degree.

</details>
