# 2025 CS1 Q30: Ternary strings with two equal neighbours

## Question

Let $S$ be the set of all ternary strings defined over the alphabet $\lbrace a, b, c\rbrace$. Consider all strings in $S$ that contain at least one occurrence of two consecutive symbols, that is, "aa", "bb" or "cc". The number of such strings of length 5 that are possible is _______. (Answer in integer)

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**195**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Count the complement: strings of length 5 with **no** two equal adjacent symbols.

- The first symbol can be anything: 3 ways.
- Each next symbol must differ from the previous one: 2 ways each, for 4 more positions.

$$\text{no equal neighbours} = 3 \times 2^4 = 48.$$

Total strings: $3^5 = 243$. Strings with at least one equal adjacent pair:

$$243 - 48 = 195.$$

A brute-force count over all 243 strings also gives 195.

**Key idea:** "at least one" is easier as total minus "none".

</details>
