# 2020 CS Q52: Derangements of LILAC

## Question

The number of permutations of the characters in LILAC so that no character appears in its original position, if the two L's are indistinguishable, is ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**12**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Positions: 1 = L, 2 = I, 3 = L, 4 = A, 5 = C.

**Forbidden placements:**

- L not in positions 1 or 3.
- I not in position 2.
- A not in position 4.
- C not in position 5.

**Place the two L's first.** They must go in two of the allowed positions $\lbrace 2, 4, 5 \rbrace$. The L's are identical, so this is $\binom{3}{2} = 3$ choices. Then I, A, C fill the three remaining positions (positions 1 and 3 plus the unused one of 2, 4, 5).

| L's at | Free positions | Restriction on I, A, C | Count |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2, 4 | 1, 3, 5 | C not at 5 | $3! - 2! = 4$ |
| 2, 5 | 1, 3, 4 | A not at 4 | $3! - 2! = 4$ |
| 4, 5 | 1, 2, 3 | I not at 2 | $3! - 2! = 4$ |

In each case only one of the three letters still has a forbidden spot, because the other two forbidden positions are already taken by L's or are not among the free positions.

Total: $4 + 4 + 4 = 12$.

I confirmed this by listing all distinct permutations of LILAC and counting those with no letter in its original position. The count is 12.

**Key idea:** with repeated letters, place the repeated letter first (choosing positions, not orderings), then count the restricted arrangements of the rest.

</details>
