# 2012 CS Q38: Number of 4-cycles in the complete graph on 6 labelled vertices

## Question

Let $G$ be a complete undirected graph on $6$ vertices. If vertices of $G$ are labeled, then the number of distinct cycles of length $4$ in $G$ is equal to

- (A) 15
- (B) 30
- (C) 90
- (D) 360

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**MTA (marks awarded to all; the key names no single option)**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Choose the $4$ vertices of the cycle: $\binom{6}{4} = 15$ ways. On four fixed vertices, the number of distinct (undirected) 4-cycles is $\dfrac{(4-1)!}{2} = 3$.

$$15 \times 3 = 45.$$

**$45$ is not among the options.** The options match common miscounts:

- $15$ counts vertex sets only.
- $30$ and $90$ are $15 \times 2$ and $45 \times 2$ (counting both directions or similar).
- $360 = 6 \cdot 5 \cdot 4 \cdot 3$ counts ordered sequences of four distinct vertices.

Because no option equals the true count, the official key gives marks to all (MTA).

**Code check:** brute force over all 4-subsets and their orderings, with each cycle counted once, gave $45$.

**Key idea:** choose the vertices, then divide the $(k-1)!$ cyclic orderings by $2$ for direction.

</details>
