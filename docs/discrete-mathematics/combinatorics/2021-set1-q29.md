# 2021 CS1 Q29: Assigning 6 jobs to 3 computers

## Question

There are 6 jobs with distinct difficulty levels, and 3 computers with distinct processing speeds. Each job is assigned to a computer such that:

- The fastest computer gets the toughest job and the slowest computer gets the easiest job.
- Every computer gets at least one job.

The number of ways in which this can be done is ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**65**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

The toughest job goes to the fastest computer and the easiest job goes to the slowest computer. Those two are fixed.

The other 4 jobs can go to any of the 3 computers. The only remaining requirement is that the **middle** computer gets at least one of them (the fastest and slowest already have a job each).

Total assignments of the 4 jobs: $3^4=81$.

Assignments where the middle computer gets none: each job goes to one of the other 2 computers, $2^4=16$.

$81-16=\mathbf{65}$.

Verified by brute force over all $3^6$ assignments with the two fixed conditions and "all three computers used": 65.

**Key idea:** fix the forced assignments, then use complement counting for the "at least one" condition.

</details>
