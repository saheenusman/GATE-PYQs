# 2015 CS2 Q63: Which first-order formula is a tautology

## Question

Which one of the following well formed formulae is a tautology?

- (A) $\forall x\, \exists y\, R(x,y) \leftrightarrow \exists y\, \forall x\, R(x,y)$
- (B) $(\forall x\, [\exists y\, R(x,y) \to S(x,y)]) \to \forall x\, \exists y\, S(x,y)$
- (C) $[\forall x\, \exists y\, (P(x,y) \to R(x,y))] \leftrightarrow [\forall x\, \exists y\, (\neg P(x,y) \vee R(x,y))]$
- (D) $\forall x\, \forall y\, P(x,y) \to \forall x\, \forall y\, P(y,x)$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Status: disputed.** The official key is (C). I find that **both (C) and (D) are valid**, so I could not match the key's "one answer". The analysis follows.

**(A) Not valid.** $\exists y\,\forall x\,R$ implies $\forall x\,\exists y\,R$, but not the other way. Counter-model: domain $\lbrace 0,1 \rbrace$, $R = \lbrace (0,1),(1,0) \rbrace$. Every $x$ has a partner $y$, but no single $y$ works for all $x$.

**(B) Not valid.** Take $R = S = \emptyset$. Then $\exists y\,R(x,y)$ is false for every $x$, so the antecedent is true, while $\forall x\,\exists y\,S(x,y)$ is false. This works however the variable $y$ in $S(x,y)$ is scoped (it is outside the $\exists y$ as printed).

**(C) Valid.** $P \to R \equiv \neg P \vee R$. The two sides of the biconditional are the same formula with a subformula replaced by an equivalent one, so the biconditional always holds.

**(D) Also valid.** $\forall x\,\forall y\,P(x,y)$ says $P$ holds for *every* ordered pair. Then $P(y,x)$ holds for every pair as well. Equivalently, $\forall x\,\forall y\,P(y,x)$ is the same sentence as $\forall x\,\forall y\,P(x,y)$ with the bound variables renamed.

**Code check:** I tested all relations $P$, $R$, $S$ on domains of size 1, 2 and 3 (with (B) over every value of its free $y$). (C) and (D) never failed. (A) and (B) failed with the counter-models above.

**Conclusion.** I record the official key (C), but (D) is also a tautology as printed in this paper. That is why this is marked `disputed`.

**Key idea:** $\forall\exists$ does not imply $\exists\forall$, and $P \to R \equiv \neg P \vee R$.

</details>
