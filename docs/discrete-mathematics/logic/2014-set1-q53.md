# 2014 CS1 Q53: Formula that is true when exactly two of p, q, r are true

## Question

Which one of the following propositional logic formulas is TRUE when exactly two of $p$, $q$, and $r$ are **TRUE**?

- (A) $((p \leftrightarrow q) \wedge r) \vee (p \wedge q \wedge \sim r)$
- (B) $(\sim(p \leftrightarrow q) \wedge r) \vee (p \wedge q \wedge \sim r)$
- (C) $((p \rightarrow q) \wedge r) \vee (p \wedge q \wedge \sim r)$
- (D) $(\sim(p \leftrightarrow q) \wedge r) \wedge (p \wedge q \wedge \sim r)$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Exactly two true means either $r$ is true and exactly one of $p, q$ is true, or $r$ is false and both $p, q$ are true.

- $\sim(p \leftrightarrow q)$ is $p \oplus q$ (exactly one of $p, q$). With $r$: $(\sim(p \leftrightarrow q)) \wedge r$.
- Both $p, q$ true and $r$ false: $p \wedge q \wedge \sim r$.

OR these two cases: **(B)**.

- (A) $(p \leftrightarrow q) \wedge r$ is true for $p=q=0, r=1$ (one true) or $p=q=r=1$ (three true).
- (C) $(p \to q) \wedge r$ is true at $p=0,q=0,r=1$, and $p=q=r=1$, so it is true for the wrong counts.
- (D) the two sides are joined by $\wedge$, and they cannot both hold (the first needs $r$ true, the second needs $r$ false), so it is always false.

**Code check:** a truth table over all 8 assignments matches "exactly two true" only for (B).

**Key idea:** split "exactly two" by the value of $r$.

</details>
