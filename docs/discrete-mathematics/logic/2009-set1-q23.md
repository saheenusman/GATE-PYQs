# 2009 CS Q23: Logical formula for gold and silver ornaments are precious

## Question

Which one of the following is the most appropriate logical formula to represent the statement:

"Gold and silver ornaments are precious"

The following notations are used:

- $G(x)$: $x$ is a gold ornament.
- $S(x)$: $x$ is a silver ornament.
- $P(x)$: $x$ is precious.

Options:

- (A) $\forall x \big(P(x) \rightarrow (G(x) \wedge S(x))\big)$
- (B) $\forall x \big((G(x) \wedge S(x)) \rightarrow P(x)\big)$
- (C) $\exists x \big((G(x) \wedge S(x)) \rightarrow P(x)\big)$
- (D) $\forall x \big((G(x) \vee S(x)) \rightarrow P(x)\big)$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

The sentence says every gold ornament is precious **and** every silver ornament is precious:

$$\forall x\,(G(x) \rightarrow P(x)) \ \wedge\ \forall x\,(S(x) \rightarrow P(x)) \;\equiv\; \forall x\,\big((G(x) \vee S(x)) \rightarrow P(x)\big).$$

That is (D).

- (A) says every precious thing is both gold and silver. The direction is wrong and "both" is wrong.
- (B) covers only things that are **both** gold and silver. A plain gold ornament would not be covered.
- (C) uses $\exists$ with $\rightarrow$. It is true as soon as some $x$ is not a gold-and-silver ornament (the implication holds vacuously), so it says almost nothing.

**Code check:** over all assignments of $G, S, P$ on domains of $1$, $2$ and $3$ elements ($584$ models), (D) agreed with "every gold ornament is precious and every silver ornament is precious" in all $584$. Options (A), (B) and (C) disagreed with it in $282$, $244$ and $426$ models.

**Key idea:** "A and B are C" with a universal reading is $\forall x\,((A(x) \vee B(x)) \rightarrow C(x))$; a union in the antecedent, never a conjunction.

</details>
