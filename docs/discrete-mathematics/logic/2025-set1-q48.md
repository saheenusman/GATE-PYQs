# 2025 CS1 Q48: Predicate logic for exactly one mother

## Question

Which of the following predicate logic formulae/formula is/are CORRECT representation(s) of the statement: "Everyone has exactly one mother"?

The meanings of the predicates used are:

- $mother(y, x)$: $y$ is the mother of $x$
- $noteq(x, y)$: $x$ and $y$ are not equal

- (A) $\forall x\, \exists y\, \exists z\, (mother(y, x) \wedge \neg mother(z, x))$
- (B) $\forall x\, \exists y\, [mother(y, x) \wedge \forall z\, (noteq(z, y) \rightarrow \neg mother(z, x))]$
- (C) $\forall x\, \forall y\, [mother(y, x) \rightarrow \exists z\, (mother(z, x) \wedge \neg noteq(z, y))]$
- (D) $\forall x\, \exists y\, [mother(y, x) \wedge \neg \exists z\, (noteq(z, y) \wedge mother(z, x))]$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B and D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

"Exactly one mother" means: **at least one** mother $y$, and **no mother different from $y$**.

| Option | Meaning | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| A | Every $x$ has a mother and also has some non-mother $z$ | Wrong: says nothing about uniqueness (and the non-mother condition is almost always true) |
| **B** | Every $x$ has a mother $y$, and every $z$ different from $y$ is **not** a mother of $x$ | **Correct** |
| C | If $y$ is a mother of $x$, then some mother $z$ equals $y$ | Wrong: always true (take $z = y$), and it does not even say a mother exists |
| **D** | Every $x$ has a mother $y$, and there is **no** mother $z$ different from $y$ | **Correct** (equivalent to B) |

I tested the four formulas on every possible "mother" relation over a 3-element domain (512 cases) against the statement "each $x$ has exactly one $y$ with $mother(y,x)$": B and D agreed in all cases, and A and C did not.

**Key idea:** "exactly one" $=$ existence plus uniqueness. Uniqueness is stated as "every other element fails the property".

</details>
