# 2017 CS1 Q2: Which quantified sentences are implied by forall x exists y R(x,y)

## Question

Consider the first-order logic sentence $F: \forall x\,(\exists y\, R(x,y))$. Assuming non-empty logical domains, which of the sentences below are *implied* by $F$?

- I. $\exists y\,(\exists x\, R(x,y))$
- II. $\exists y\,(\forall x\, R(x,y))$
- III. $\forall y\,(\exists x\, R(x,y))$
- IV. $\neg\exists x\,(\forall y\, \neg R(x,y))$

Options:

- (A) IV only
- (B) I and IV only
- (C) II only
- (D) II and III only

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**What $F$ says.** Every $x$ has at least one $y$ with $R(x,y)$.

| Sentence | Implied by $F$? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| I. $\exists y\,\exists x\, R(x,y)$ | **Yes** | The domain is non-empty, so pick any $x$; $F$ gives a $y$ with $R(x,y)$. Swapping adjacent $\exists$ quantifiers changes nothing. |
| II. $\exists y\,\forall x\, R(x,y)$ | No | One $y$ must work for all $x$. $F$ lets $y$ depend on $x$. |
| III. $\forall y\,\exists x\, R(x,y)$ | No | Needs every $y$ to be reached. $F$ only says every $x$ reaches some $y$. |
| IV. $\neg\exists x\,\forall y\,\neg R(x,y)$ | **Yes** | Push the negation in: $\forall x\,\exists y\, R(x,y)$, which is $F$ itself. |

**Counterexamples for II and III.** Take the domain $\lbrace 1,2\rbrace$. With $R = \lbrace(1,2),(2,1)\rbrace$ every $x$ has a partner, but no single $y$ works for both $x$ (II fails). With $R = \lbrace (1,1),(2,1)\rbrace$ every $x$ has a partner, but $y=2$ is never reached (III fails).

So I and IV only: **(B)**.

**Code check:** enumerating every relation on domains of size 1, 2 and 3 (with $F$ true) found no case where I or IV is false, but 176 cases where II fails and 80 where III fails.

**Key idea:** $\exists\exists$ can be swapped freely and $\neg\exists\forall\neg \equiv \forall\exists$, but $\forall\exists$ never gives $\exists\forall$.

</details>
