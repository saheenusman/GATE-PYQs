# 2021 CS1 Q17: Tautology check for two implications

## Question

Let $p$ and $q$ be two propositions. Consider the following two formulae in propositional logic.

$S_1: (\neg p \wedge (p \vee q)) \rightarrow q$

$S_2: q \rightarrow (\neg p \wedge (p \vee q))$

Which one of the following choices is correct?

- (A) Both $S_1$ and $S_2$ are tautologies.
- (B) $S_1$ is a tautology but $S_2$ is not a tautology.
- (C) $S_1$ is not a tautology but $S_2$ is a tautology.
- (D) Neither $S_1$ nor $S_2$ is a tautology.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Simplify $\neg p\wedge(p\vee q)=(\neg p\wedge p)\vee(\neg p\wedge q)=\neg p\wedge q$.

- $S_1=(\neg p\wedge q)\to q$. Whenever the antecedent holds, $q$ holds. **Tautology.**
- $S_2=q\to(\neg p\wedge q)$. Take $p=1,q=1$: the antecedent is true and $\neg p\wedge q$ is false. **Not a tautology.**

Truth table (checked by script):

| $p$ | $q$ | $S_1$ | $S_2$ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| 0 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| 1 | 0 | 1 | 1 |
| 1 | 1 | 1 | 0 |

So (B).

**Key idea:** $(A\wedge B)\to B$ is always true, but its converse is not.

</details>
