# 2025 CS2 Q15: Which induction-style implication is valid

## Question

Let $P(x)$ be an arbitrary predicate over the domain of natural numbers.

Which ONE of the following statements is TRUE?

- (A) $(P(0) \wedge (\forall x\,[P(x) \Rightarrow P(x+1)])) \Rightarrow (\forall x\, P(x))$
- (B) $(P(0) \wedge (\forall x\,[P(x) \Rightarrow P(x-1)])) \Rightarrow (\forall x\, P(x))$
- (C) $(P(1000) \wedge (\forall x\,[P(x) \Rightarrow P(x-1)])) \Rightarrow (\forall x\, P(x))$
- (D) $(P(1000) \wedge (\forall x\,[P(x) \Rightarrow P(x+1)])) \Rightarrow (\forall x\, P(x))$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**(A) is the principle of mathematical induction**: a base case $P(0)$ and a step $P(x) \Rightarrow P(x+1)$ give $P(0), P(1), P(2), \dots$, so $\forall x\, P(x)$.

The others fail because truth only flows in the wrong direction or from the wrong start:

| Option | Counter-example predicate | Why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| B | $P(x) = (x \le 0)$ | $P(0)$ holds. The step "$P(x) \Rightarrow P(x-1)$" passes truth **downward** (and for $x \ge 1$ it is vacuous since $P(x)$ is false), but $P(1)$ is false. (For $x = 0$ the term $P(-1)$ lies outside the naturals and is ignored.) |
| C | $P(x) = (x \le 1000)$ | $P(1000)$ holds and truth passes downward, so $P(0..1000)$ hold, but $P(1001)$ is false |
| D | $P(x) = (x \ge 1000)$ | $P(1000)$ holds and truth passes upward, so $P(x)$ holds for all $x \ge 1000$, but $P(0)$ is false |

**Key idea:** induction needs a base case at the **bottom** and a step that goes **upward**.

</details>
