# 2015 CS2 Q11: Inference from two conditional statements

## Question

Consider the following two statements.

- $S1$: If a candidate is known to be corrupt, then he will not be elected.
- $S2$: If a candidate is kind, he will be elected.

Which one of the following statements follows from $S1$ and $S2$ as per sound inference rules of logic?

- (A) If a person is known to be corrupt, he is kind
- (B) If a person is not known to be corrupt, he is not kind
- (C) If a person is kind, he is not known to be corrupt
- (D) If a person is not kind, he is not known to be corrupt

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Let $C$ = corrupt, $E$ = elected, $K$ = kind.

- $S1$: $C \to \neg E$, with contrapositive $E \to \neg C$.
- $S2$: $K \to E$.

Chain $S2$ with the contrapositive of $S1$ (hypothetical syllogism):

$$K \to E \to \neg C \;\Rightarrow\; K \to \neg C$$

"If a person is kind, he is not known to be corrupt": option (C).

(A) is $C \to K$. It does not follow: nothing links corruption to kindness, and S1 and S2 together are consistent with a corrupt person who is not kind. (B) is $\neg C \to \neg K$, the contrapositive of $K \to C$, which is not given. (D) is $\neg K \to \neg C$, the converse of the valid conclusion $K \to \neg C$, and a converse does not follow.

**Key idea:** use the contrapositive of S1 to chain the two conditionals.

</details>
