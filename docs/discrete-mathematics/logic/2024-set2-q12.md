# 2024 CS2 Q12: Translating a sentence into propositional logic

## Question

Let $p$ and $q$ be the following propositions:

- $p$: Fail grade can be given.
- $q$: Student scores more than 50% marks.

Consider the statement: "Fail grade cannot be given when student scores more than 50% marks." Which one of the following is the CORRECT representation of the above statement in propositional logic?

- (A) $q \to \neg p$
- (B) $q \to p$
- (C) $p \to q$
- (D) $\neg p \to q$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

The pattern "**X cannot happen when Y**" means: *if Y, then not X*.

- Y = "student scores more than 50%" = $q$
- X = "fail grade can be given" = $p$

So the statement is $q \to \neg p$.

**Why the others fail:**

- **(B)** $q \to p$ says a high score *allows* a fail grade, the opposite.
- **(C)** $p \to q$ says a fail grade can be given only if the score is high.
- **(D)** $\neg p \to q$ says that if no fail is given, the score must be above 50%.

**Key idea:** "A when B" translates to $B \to A$, not $A \to B$.

</details>
