# 2026 CS2 Q11: Quantifiers for a person known by everyone

## Question

For two different persons $x$ and $y$, the predicate $M(x, y)$ means "$x$ knows $y$". Consider the statement:

> There is a person who does not know anyone else, but that person is known by everyone else.

Which expression represents it?

- (A) $(\exists y)(\forall x)\ \big((x \ne y) \rightarrow (M(x,y) \wedge \neg M(y,x))\big)$
- (B) $(\forall y)(\exists x)\ \big((x \ne y) \rightarrow (M(x,y) \wedge \neg M(y,x))\big)$
- (C) $(\exists y)(\exists x)\ \big((x \ne y) \rightarrow (M(x,y) \wedge \neg M(y,x))\big)$
- (D) $(\forall y)(\forall x)\ \big((x \ne y) \rightarrow (M(x,y) \wedge \neg M(y,x))\big)$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Translate the sentence piece by piece.**

| English | Logic |
|---|---|
| there is a person $y$ | $\exists y$ |
| for everyone else $x$ | $\forall x$, with guard $x \ne y$ |
| $x$ knows $y$ (everyone knows $y$) | $M(x, y)$ |
| $y$ knows no one else | $\neg M(y, x)$ |

Putting "$\exists y$" first and "$\forall x \ne y$" inside gives A. The guard $x \ne y$ is an implication because the condition only applies to *other* people.

**Why the others fail:**

- **(B)** $\forall y\, \exists x$ with an implication is always true: for each $y$, pick $x = y$. The premise $x \ne y$ is then false, so the implication holds automatically. It says nothing about knowing.
- **(C)** $\exists y\, \exists x$ with an implication is also true as soon as we pick $x = y$, for the same reason. It says almost nothing.
- **(D)** $\forall y\, \forall x$ says everyone is known by everyone and knows no one. For two people $a \ne b$ it would need $M(a,b) \wedge \neg M(b,a)$ and also $M(b,a) \wedge \neg M(a,b)$, a contradiction.

**Verified.** I checked all 64 possible "who knows whom" relations on 3 people. Formula A agrees with the English statement in every model. B and C disagree in 52 of 64 models and D in 12.

**Key idea:** "there is $y$ such that for all others $x$..." is $\exists y\, \forall x$ with a guard $x \ne y$. A guarded $\exists$ with an implication is a trap, because it becomes trivially true.

</details>
