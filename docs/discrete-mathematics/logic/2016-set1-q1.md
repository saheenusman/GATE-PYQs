# 2016 CS1 Q1: Integer satisfying a negated propositional formula

## Question

Let $p, q, r, s$ represent the following propositions.

- $p$: $x \in \lbrace 8, 9, 10, 11, 12 \rbrace$
- $q$: $x$ is a composite number
- $r$: $x$ is a perfect square
- $s$: $x$ is a prime number

The integer $x \ge 2$ which satisfies $\neg\big((p \Rightarrow q) \wedge (\neg r \vee \neg s)\big)$ is ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**11**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Split the negation (De Morgan).**

$$\neg\big((p \Rightarrow q) \wedge (\neg r \vee \neg s)\big) \equiv \neg(p \Rightarrow q) \vee (r \wedge s)$$

**The term $r \wedge s$ is never true.** A number that is both a perfect square and prime would be $k^2$ with $k \ge 2$, which has the factor $k$, so it is not prime. For $x \ge 2$, $r \wedge s$ is always false.

**What is left.**

$$\neg(p \Rightarrow q) \equiv p \wedge \neg q$$

So $x \in \lbrace 8, \dots, 12 \rbrace$ and $x$ is **not** composite.

| $x$ | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| composite? | yes | yes | yes | no (prime) | yes |

Only $x = 11$ works.

**Code check:** a brute-force scan of $x = 2..199$ against the original formula returned only $11$.

**Key idea:** negate an implication as $p \wedge \neg q$, and note that no integer is both a perfect square and a prime.

</details>
