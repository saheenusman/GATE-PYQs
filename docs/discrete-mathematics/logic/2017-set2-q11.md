# 2017 CS2 Q11: Symbolic form of a raining, cold and pleasant statement

## Question

Let $p$, $q$, $r$ denote the statements "*It is raining*", "*It is cold*", and "*It is pleasant*", respectively. Then the statement "*It is not raining and it is pleasant, and it is not pleasant only if it is raining and it is cold*" is represented by

- (A) $(\neg p \wedge r) \wedge (\neg r \to (p \wedge q))$
- (B) $(\neg p \wedge r) \wedge ((p \wedge q) \to \neg r)$
- (C) $(\neg p \wedge r) \vee ((p \wedge q) \to \neg r)$
- (D) $(\neg p \wedge r) \vee (r \to (p \wedge q))$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Translate the pieces.**

- "It is not raining and it is pleasant" $=\ \neg p \wedge r$.
- "It is not pleasant **only if** it is raining and it is cold": "$X$ only if $Y$" means $X \to Y$. Here $X = \neg r$ and $Y = p\wedge q$, so $\neg r \to (p \wedge q)$.
- The two parts are joined by "**and**": $\wedge$.

$$(\neg p \wedge r) \wedge (\neg r \to (p \wedge q))$$

This is **(A)**.

**Why the others fail.**

- (B) writes $(p\wedge q)\to\neg r$, which says "*if* it is raining and cold *then* it is not pleasant". That is the converse of "only if".
- (C) and (D) join the two parts with "or" instead of "and".

**A subtle point.** As truth tables, (A) and (B) turn out to be equal: the first conjunct forces $p$ false and $r$ true, which makes both second conjuncts true. So the two formulas have the same truth table. The question asks which formula *represents the sentence*, and only (A) translates "only if" correctly.

**Code check:** a truth table over $(p,q,r)$ showed (A) and (B) agree everywhere, while (C) and (D) differ from them. Both (A) and (B) reduce to $\neg p\wedge r$.

**Key idea:** "$X$ only if $Y$" is $X \to Y$ (not $Y \to X$).

</details>
