# 2014 CS3 Q1: Good phones are not cheap vs cheap phones are not good

## Question

Consider the following statements:

- P: Good mobile phones are not cheap
- Q: Cheap mobile phones are not good

- L: P implies Q
- M: Q implies P
- N: P is equivalent to Q

Which one of the following about L, M, and N is **CORRECT**?

- (A) Only L is TRUE.
- (B) Only M is TRUE.
- (C) Only N is TRUE.
- (D) L, M and N are TRUE.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Write $g$ = "the phone is good" and $c$ = "the phone is cheap".

- $P$: $g \to \lnot c$ (good phones are not cheap)
- $Q$: $c \to \lnot g$ (cheap phones are not good)

$Q$ is the **contrapositive** of $P$: the contrapositive of $g \to \lnot c$ is $c \to \lnot g$. A statement and its contrapositive are logically equivalent, so $P \equiv Q$.

Hence $P$ implies $Q$ (L true), $Q$ implies $P$ (M true), and $P$ is equivalent to $Q$ (N true). So (A), (B) and (C) are each missing a true statement: **D**.

**Code check:** a truth table over $(g, c)$ shows $P$ and $Q$ agree in all 4 cases.

**Key idea:** $p \to q \equiv \lnot q \to \lnot p$.

</details>
