# 2013 CS Q27: None of my friends are perfect

## Question

What is the logical translation of the following statement?

"None of my friends are perfect."

- (A) $\exists x \left( F(x) \wedge \neg P(x) \right)$
- (B) $\exists x \left( \neg F(x) \wedge P(x) \right)$
- (C) $\exists x \left( \neg F(x) \wedge \neg P(x) \right)$
- (D) $\neg \exists x \left( F(x) \wedge P(x) \right)$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Let $F(x)$: "$x$ is my friend" and $P(x)$: "$x$ is perfect".

"None of my friends are perfect" means there is no $x$ that is both a friend and perfect:

$$\neg \exists x\,(F(x) \wedge P(x)) \;\equiv\; \forall x\,(F(x) \to \neg P(x))$$

**Why the others fail** (all of them use $\exists$ and assert that something exists):

- (A) says *some* friend is not perfect, which is compatible with other friends being perfect.
- (B) says some non-friend is perfect.
- (C) says some non-friend is imperfect.

**Code check:** over all models on 3 elements only (D) matched "no friend is perfect".

**Key idea:** "none" is $\neg\exists$ of the conjunction, equivalently $\forall$ with an implication.

</details>
