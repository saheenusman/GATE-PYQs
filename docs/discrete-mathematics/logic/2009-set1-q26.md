# 2009 CS Q26: Equivalent quantified formulas

## Question

Consider the following well-formed formulae:

- I. $\neg \forall x (P(x))$
- II. $\neg \exists x (P(x))$
- III. $\neg \exists x (\neg P(x))$
- IV. $\exists x (\neg P(x))$

Which of the above are equivalent?

- (A) I and III
- (B) I and IV
- (C) II and III
- (D) II and IV

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Push the negation inward ($\neg\forall \equiv \exists\neg$ and $\neg\exists \equiv \forall\neg$):

- I $\equiv \exists x\,\neg P(x)$
- II $\equiv \forall x\,\neg P(x)$
- III $\equiv \forall x\,P(x)$
- IV $= \exists x\,\neg P(x)$

So I and IV are the same formula: (B).

- (A) I says some $x$ fails $P$, III says every $x$ satisfies $P$: opposites.
- (C) II says no $x$ satisfies $P$, III says every $x$ does: not equivalent.
- (D) II ("no $x$ satisfies $P$") implies IV ("some $x$ fails $P$") on a non-empty domain, but IV does not imply II (some $x$ may fail while others satisfy $P$).

**Code check:** every predicate $P$ on domains of size $1$ to $4$ was enumerated. I and IV gave the same truth value every time; the pairs (I, III), (II, III) and (II, IV) did not.

**Key idea:** $\neg\forall x\,P(x) \equiv \exists x\,\neg P(x)$ and $\neg\exists x\,P(x) \equiv \forall x\,\neg P(x)$.

</details>
