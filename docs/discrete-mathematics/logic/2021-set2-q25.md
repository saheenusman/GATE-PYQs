# 2021 CS2 Q25: Tautology check of a nested implication

## Question

Choose the correct choice(s) regarding the following propositional logic assertion $S$:

$$S:\ ((P\wedge Q)\to R)\to((P\wedge Q)\to(Q\to R))$$

- (A) $S$ is neither a tautology nor a contradiction.
- (B) $S$ is a tautology.
- (C) $S$ is a contradiction.
- (D) The antecedent of $S$ is logically equivalent to the consequent of $S$.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**B and D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

Let $A=P\wedge Q$. Then

- antecedent of $S$: $A\to R$
- consequent of $S$: $A\to(Q\to R)$

When $A$ is true, $Q$ is true, so $Q\to R$ is just $R$. When $A$ is false, both $A\to R$ and $A\to(Q\to R)$ are true. Hence the two sides always have the same value:

$$(A\to R)\equiv(A\to(Q\to R)).$$

So the antecedent is equivalent to the consequent (D), and an implication $X\to X'$ with $X\equiv X'$ is always true, so $S$ is a tautology (B).

- (A) False: it is a tautology.
- (C) False: a tautology is never a contradiction.

A truth-table check over all 8 assignments confirmed that $S$ is always true and the antecedent equals the consequent in every row.

Answer: **B and D**.

**Key idea:** $(P\wedge Q)\to(Q\to R)$ is the same as $(P\wedge Q)\to R$ (exportation).

</details>
