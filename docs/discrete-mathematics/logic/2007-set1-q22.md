# 2007 CS Q22: First-order sentence that does not mean not every graph is connected

## Question

Let $Graph(x)$ be a predicate which denotes that $x$ is a graph. Let $Connected(x)$ be a predicate which denotes that $x$ is connected. Which of the following first order logic sentences **DOES NOT** represent the statement: "*Not every graph is connected*"?

- (A) $\neg \forall x\,(Graph(x) \Rightarrow Connected(x))$
- (B) $\exists x\,(Graph(x) \wedge \neg Connected(x))$
- (C) $\neg \forall x\,(\neg Graph(x) \vee Connected(x))$
- (D) $\forall x\,(Graph(x) \Rightarrow \neg Connected(x))$

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**D**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

The statement is $\neg\forall x\,(Graph(x) \Rightarrow Connected(x))$, which is exactly (A). Push the negation in:
$$\neg\forall x\,(\neg Graph(x) \vee Connected(x)) \equiv \exists x\,(Graph(x) \wedge \neg Connected(x)).$$

- (A) is the statement itself.
- (B) is the negation pushed inside (using $\neg(p \Rightarrow q) \equiv p \wedge \neg q$). Equivalent.
- (C) is (A) with $p \Rightarrow q$ rewritten as $\neg p \vee q$. Equivalent.
- **(D)** says *every* graph is **not** connected (no graph is connected). That is much stronger than "some graph is not connected", so it does not represent the statement.

**Code check:** Python: evaluated all four sentences over all 340 finite models (1 to 4 elements, each with Graph/Connected true or false). (B) and (C) agree with (A) on every model; (D) disagrees on 160 of them.

**Key idea:** $\neg\forall$ becomes $\exists\neg$; "not every" is not the same as "every is not".
