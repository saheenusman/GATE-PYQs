# 2016 CS2 Q36: New-order traversal of an expression tree

## Question

Consider the following New-order strategy for traversing a binary tree:

- Visit the root;
- Visit the right subtree using New-order;
- Visit the left subtree using New-order;

The New-order traversal of the expression tree corresponding to the reverse polish expression `3 4 * 5 - 2 ^ 6 7 * 1 + -` is given by:

- (A) + - 1 6 7 * 2 ^ 5 - 3 4 *
- (B) - + 1 * 6 7 ^ 2 - 5 * 3 4
- (C) - + 1 * 7 6 ^ 2 - 5 * 4 3
- (D) 1 7 6 * + 2 5 4 3 * - ^ -

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**C**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Build the tree from the postfix expression.**

$$\big((3 * 4) - 5\big)\ ^\wedge\ 2 \;-\; \big((6 * 7) + 1\big)$$

The root is the last `-`, with left subtree $\big((3*4)-5\big)^2$ and right subtree $(6*7)+1$.

**New-order = root, right, left.**

- Root: `-`
- Right subtree $(6*7)+1$: root `+`, right child `1`, left child `*` with children $6$ (left) and $7$ (right). Root, right, left gives `+ 1 * 7 6`.
- Left subtree $\big((3*4)-5\big)^2$: root `^`, right child `2`, left child `-`. For `-`: right `5`, left `*` (children $3$, $4$): `- 5 * 4 3`. So the subtree gives `^ 2 - 5 * 4 3`.

Together:

```
- + 1 * 7 6 ^ 2 - 5 * 4 3
```

That is **(C)**.

- (A) starts with `+`, but the root is `-`.
- (B) visits `6` before `7`, i.e. the left child before the right child of `*`.
- (D) starts with a leaf; it is not a traversal that begins at the root.

**Code check:** building the tree from the postfix string and traversing root, right, left printed exactly `- + 1 * 7 6 ^ 2 - 5 * 4 3`.

**Key idea:** swap the children in a preorder: root, right, left.

</details>
