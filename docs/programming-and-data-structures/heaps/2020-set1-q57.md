# 2020 CS Q57: Comparisons to find the maximum in a min-heap

## Question

Consider the array representation of a binary min-heap containing 1023 elements. The minimum number of comparisons required to find the maximum in the heap is ______.

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**511**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**Where the maximum can be.** In a min-heap every internal node is $\le$ its children. A node with a child therefore cannot be larger than that child, so a maximum value is always attained at a **leaf**.

**How many leaves.** With $n = 1023 = 2^{10} - 1$ nodes the tree is a perfect binary tree with 10 levels.

- In the array, the leaves are at indices $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor + 1 = 512$ to $1023$.
- That is $1023 - 511 = 512$ leaves.

**Cost.** The leaves are not ordered relative to each other, so finding the largest of 512 values takes

$$512 - 1 = 511$$

comparisons. Fewer is impossible: every leaf except the maximum must lose at least one comparison, and each comparison produces at most one loser.

I confirmed the leaf count and the $511$ figure in code.

**Common wrong answers:**

- 1022 comes from scanning all 1023 elements. The 511 internal nodes need no comparison, because each is at most one of its children.
- 0 or $\log n$ is the cost of finding the *minimum* (it is the root) or deleting it. It does not apply to the maximum.

**Key idea:** in a min-heap the maximum sits among the leaves, which are about half the nodes and are unordered. Finding the maximum of $m$ unordered items needs $m - 1$ comparisons.

</details>
