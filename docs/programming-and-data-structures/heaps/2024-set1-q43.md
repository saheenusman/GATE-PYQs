# 2024 CS1 Q43: Possible positions of the maximum in a min-heap

## Question

Consider a binary min-heap containing 105 distinct elements. Let $k$ be the index (in the underlying array) of the maximum element stored in the heap. The number of possible values of $k$ is

- (A) 53
- (B) 52
- (C) 27
- (D) 1

<details class="success" markdown="1">
<summary>Answer</summary>

**A (53)**

</details>

<details class="info" markdown="1">
<summary>Solution</summary>

**In a min-heap every parent is smaller than its children.** So the maximum cannot have a child, because a child would have to be larger. The maximum must be a **leaf**.

**Leaves in a heap of $n$ nodes** (1-based indices) are $\lfloor n/2 \rfloor + 1$ to $n$:

$$\lfloor 105/2 \rfloor + 1 = 53 \;\text{ to }\; 105 \;\Rightarrow\; 105 - 52 = 53 \text{ positions.}$$

Any leaf can hold the maximum. A leaf has no children to compare against, and its parent is smaller because the leaf holds the largest value.

**Why the others fail:** 52 counts the internal nodes. 27 and 1 do not match the number of leaves (1 would be for a max-heap's root).

**Key idea:** in a min-heap the maximum is always at a leaf, and about half of the nodes are leaves.

</details>
